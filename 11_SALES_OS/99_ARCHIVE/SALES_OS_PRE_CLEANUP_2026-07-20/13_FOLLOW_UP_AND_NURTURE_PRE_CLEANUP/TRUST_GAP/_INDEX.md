Version: v0.1 · Status: DRAFT · Date: 2026-07-11 · Supersedes: none
Sources: Risk Assurance v2.0 §9 (Trust Deficit); Decision Psychology v2.1 RB-10; Case Study Bank (non-self-serving cases); Proof Bank

## Gap definition
Client not yet convinced the advice is advisory rather than sales-driven, or wants to verify data/method (RB-10). `[T3]`

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Objective | Earn trust through transparency + verifiable sources + willingness to say no |
| Assets | Method/how-numbers-are-built explainer; what-to-avoid list; non-self-serving ("don't buy") case study; public sources (URA/EdgeProp/99.co) |
| Channel | Email (verifiable material) → WhatsApp; joint call if trust is in method |
| Timing | Within 48h; case study +5 days; no more than 2 touches before pausing |
| Owner | Consult advisor |
| Exit condition | Client engages with the verification / asks a next-step question → re-book |

## DRAFT templates — pending platform verification · advisory tone · zero pressure

**WhatsApp (DRAFT):**
> Hi [name] — you asked a fair question about how we build our numbers. Rather than ask you to take my word, here's exactly where the data comes from and the public sources you can check it against yourself. Have a look on your own time. If the review ever points to "don't move", I'll tell you that too.

**Email (DRAFT):**
> Subject: How to check our numbers yourself
> Hi [name],
> A good advisor should welcome being verified. Here's how the figures in your session were built, and the independent public sources (URA, EdgeProp, PropertyGuru, 99.co) you can cross-check them against.
> I've also included a short case where our honest recommendation was *not* to buy — because that's the standard I'd hold your decision to as well.
> No agenda here. When you're comfortable, we map what to avoid first, then what makes sense.
