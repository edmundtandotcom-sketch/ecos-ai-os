## 1. Core belief

A property does not qualify because it is popular, profitable-looking or affordable today. It qualifies only when it fits the household's route, survives the holding period, remains sellable to a clear future buyer and preserves the next move.

A good property can still be the wrong property for the client's plan.

---

## 2. Public and backend boundary

**Share publicly:** how the route is assessed; how the property's job is defined; common rejection principles; anonymised examples; sample redacted outputs; why popular properties can fail a particular plan; how new-launch and resale are compared; why future-buyer affordability matters.

**Keep for engaged clients only:** the exact ranked shortlist; complete project and unit research; personalised calculations; detailed scoring and weighting; negotiation and execution plan; unit, stack and price recommendation.

### What each stage of contact provides

| Stage | Provides |
|---|---|
| Second Property Readiness Check™ | Indicative route; main readiness gaps; possible cost of waiting; next recommended action. |
| Discovery | Deeper understanding of situation, challenges, objectives, cost of inaction; overview of the method; anonymised prior cases. |
| Next Move Strategy Session™ | Financial and property assessment; route comparison; affordability and timeline; initial Property Job; explanation of the selection logic. |
| After formal engagement | Detailed market research; specific projects and resale options; rejection analysis; ranked shortlist; unit and stack selection; price boundaries; transaction execution. |

The detailed research, disqualification work and ranked shortlist are delivered only after the client formally proceeds — never before.

## 3. Public selection process (verbatim, locked)

> Define the Job → Set the Search Boundaries → Remove Weak Fits → Compare the Survivors → Recommend the Best Fit

This is the only public-facing process wording. Do not use the shorter 3-step ("Define the Job → Reject What Fails → Research What Remains") or 4-step ("...→ Rank the Best Fit") variants that appear in earlier workshop drafts — both are superseded by the 5-step wording above. Do not create another branded framework name for it.

**Search-boundary logic** ("Set the Search Boundaries") is defined by the Property Job (Section 5): location and travel boundaries, property type and age, quantum, bedrooms/bathrooms/usable space, and holding-period requirements. Research and comparison only begin once these boundaries are fixed.

## 4. Risk classification and override mechanism

Do not use rigid pass/fail rules or universal hard-rejection vetoes. Use four risk classifications, judged in context of location, project, price, unit availability and the client's objective:

- **Critical Conflict** — the property directly contradicts the client's agreed route (e.g. the plan requires preserving future borrowing capacity, but this purchase consumes almost all of it).
- **Significant Risk** — the weakness could materially affect performance or exit (e.g. heavy competing supply arriving when the client reaches the intended selling period).
- **Acceptable Trade-Off** — the property is weaker in one area but the client gains something more important (e.g. average rental yield offset by strong family demand and a broader resale audience).
- **Preference Issue** — personal rather than strategic (e.g. floor preference where a lower floor meets the investment and liveability requirements at a better price).

### Override rule

A property with a Critical Conflict or Significant Risk classification may still proceed only when the following are documented: what the weakness is; why the client is prepared to accept it; which strong attributes compensate for it; what the client gives up; what price makes the trade-off acceptable; what future event would trigger a review or exit.

### The five most common drivers of a Critical Conflict classification

These replace the "non-negotiable rejection gate" framing from earlier drafts — they are the most frequent reasons a property lands in Critical Conflict, not absolute vetoes. Every one remains overridable under the documentation rule above.

**A. Breaks the household route** — wrong ownership name; consumes a name required for the following move; prevents the intended route structure; conflicts with the client's actual timeline; cannot serve the role assigned to it.

**B. Not safely affordable through the intended period** — fails the Safety Net test (Section 6): monthly payment, cash/CPF remaining, one-income stress, interest-rate stress, vacancy, family expenses, completion-stage cashflow, ability to hold longer than expected.

**C. Exit audience is too weak** — too few realistic future buyers; future quantum exceeds likely buyer affordability; niche unit type; investor-heavy exit with few owner-occupiers; too little transaction volume.

**D. Wrong holding period** — client may need liquidity before the property is ready to perform; major transformation lands after the intended exit; surrounding supply peaks when the client expects to sell.

**E. Removes the next move** — too much loan used; insufficient reserves remaining; both names locked; too much dependency on rental; the next planned purchase becomes financially impossible.

A project that fails one of these should not be rescued by an MRT line, a nice view, a popular developer or an impressive showroom — but it can still proceed if the override documentation is completed.

## 5. Formal Property Job template

Approved as a one-page client-approved mandate, created before detailed research begins:

- Primary purpose (own-stay, investment or mixed role)
- Route: Keep and Add / Sell and Rebuild / Strengthen One First
- Maximum purchase quantum
- Comfortable monthly commitment
- Required Safety Net
- Preferred holding period
- Required location or travel boundaries
- Property type and age
- Bedrooms, bathrooms and usable-space requirements
- Future buyer type
- Rental fallback
- Ownership-name requirement
- What must remain possible after this purchase
- Unacceptable compromises

Without a confirmed Property Job, selection can drift once clients see attractive projects — this document is mandatory before research begins.

---

## 6. Safety Net formula

Use the term **Safety Net**, not Holding Buffer or reserve rule.

**Starting baseline:** at least 12 months of property commitments after completing the purchase.

**Includes:** mortgage payments for the relevant property position; maintenance fees; property tax; property insurance where applicable; expected rental shortfall or vacancy exposure; fixed property-related commitments.

**Excludes:** general household living expenses; projected bonuses; hoped-for capital gains; hoped-for rent above a conservative assumption; money already committed to legal fees, renovation, completion or other known expenses.

**Adjustments:** the 12-month baseline may be increased based on income stability, number of properties, reliance on rental, completion risk and household circumstances (e.g. self-employed client, single-income household, multiple properties, heavy rental reliance, distant completion, rising family expenses).

## 7. Future-buyer analysis (mandatory)

Approved as compulsory in every recommendation. For each recommended property, define: primary future buyer; secondary future buyer; likely household income; likely down payment; likely mortgage; why they would want the property; competing alternatives; whether the future buyer pool is expanding or shrinking; whether the expected future selling quantum remains financeable.

**Core question:** *At the future selling price, how many households can realistically afford and want this exact property?*

## 8. Selection evidence and scoring

**Evidence areas:**

1. Plan Fit
2. Entry Position
3. Demand Growth
4. Exit Depth
5. Supply & Liquidity
6. Holding Strength
7. Unit Usability

**Scoring:** use route-specific scoring, not one universal weighting. Combine numerical score, evidence, risks, trade-offs and Edmund's professional judgement. A high total score must never average away a serious strategic weakness.

**Required advisor conclusion — one of:**

- Strong Fit
- Fit at the Right Price
- Conditional Fit
- Not Recommended

Every conclusion must state: main reason; main weakness; accepted trade-off; maximum acceptable price; intended holding period; confidence; review triggers; conditions that would change the recommendation.

## 9. The 90% language (internal only)

**Internal target:** 90% Disqualification Target.

**Public wording (only wording permitted):** *"We screen the relevant market and remove most options before producing the shortlist."*

Do not publicly claim that 90% of properties are traps or that 90% are always rejected, until the Rejection Ledger proves the measured result across enough completed cases.

### Rejection Ledger specification

Record, per client search: Property Job; market reviewed; options considered; options rejected; rejection reasons; finalists; recommendation; result. Once enough cases are measured, a factual percentage may replace the internal-only target in public claims.

## 10. First-discussion demonstration

The advisor does not disclose the full property shortlist at first contact. Instead, demonstrate the method in five steps:

1. Convert the client's stated desire into a simple property job.
2. Identify two or three likely rejection conditions from what they've shared.
3. Show an anonymised prior rejection case (what the client originally wanted, what was rejected, why, what replaced it, what it preserved).
4. Show a redacted sample output (Property Job, Must-Have Conditions, Automatic Rejection Conditions, Properties Reviewed, Rejected Options, Shortlisted Options, Reason for Final Recommendation).
5. Explain the boundary plainly: detailed market research, rejection work and the ranked shortlist happen after the household position is confirmed and the client formally engages.

### Demonstration formats to build from this doctrine

- **Rejected Property Case** — what the client originally wanted, why it looked attractive, why it was rejected, what route was taken instead, what the final choice preserved.
- **The Property Job** — an anonymised one-page brief showing the job the next property had to perform.
- **Good Property, Wrong Client** — a recognised project explained as suitable in general but wrong for a specific household's plan.
- **Three Similar Properties, Three Different Jobs** — same budget, three properties, three different roles (rental / family / next-move preservation).
- **The Rejection List** — five reasons a property never entered the shortlist.

### Three illustrative client-example case patterns

1. **Decoupled couple** — desire: buy an investment property with the freed name. Job: reasonable rental support, broad future buyer group, defensible quantum, preserves capacity for a later own-stay move. Typical rejections: niche one-bedroom units; high investor concentration; excessive future supply; speculative-transformation pricing.
2. **HDB family moving into private property** — desire: sell HDB and upgrade. Job: supports family lifestyle, remains affordable through children's growing expenses, holds value across the holding period, preserves a later investment-property option. Typical rejections: undersized units; excessive monthly commitment; weak family-buyer demand; high exit quantum.
3. **Existing condo owner** — desire: keep the condo and buy another. Job: diversify rather than duplicate exposure, different buyer/tenant group, manageable if one unit is vacant, preserves reserves. Typical rejections: two assets exposed to the same tenant market; both depending on the same transformation story; total commitment too dependent on perfect occupancy.

## 11. Final Selection Pack (post-engagement output)

The complete future output — delivered only after formal engagement — contains:

1. Client route and Property Job
2. Route and financial boundaries (ownership, budget, reserve, loan, holding period, future move)
3. Market universe reviewed (regions, districts, new launch, resale, projects and unit types screened)
4. Rejection summary (number rejected, categories, major reasons, key examples)
5. Finalists (fit, transformation, entry-price logic, future buyer, exit affordability, supply, rentability, liveability, next-move impact, weaknesses)
6. Primary recommendation (why it wins, acceptable unit types, preferred stacks, target/maximum price, holding expectation, exit audience, conditions that would change the recommendation)
7. Backup options (up to two, with activation conditions) and, only when genuinely useful, up to two conditional alternatives
8. Rejected popular options (why obvious alternatives failed)
9. Execution plan (viewing order, documents, financing, negotiation, unit selection, decision deadline, next review)

Target output: one primary recommendation, two backup recommendations, conditional alternatives only when genuinely useful. Do not present five equal options — that transfers the decision back to the client.

## 12. Year-4 review position

Approximately a four-to-six-year mid-term play, adjusted by client and property. Year 4 is a formal review checkpoint, not an automatic selling date. Use the actual purchase date and applicable SSD rules for every client.

Review: whether the property has done its job; future growth reasons; increasing risks; current exit audience; rental performance; future supply; net equity available; better use of the released equity; ability to add, restructure or progress.

Possible decisions: Hold; Sell and redeploy; Restructure; Prepare for the next move; Review again at a defined trigger.

A full Exit Review Doctrine (including the exact decision rule for when to take the gain and move) is deferred to a later phase.
