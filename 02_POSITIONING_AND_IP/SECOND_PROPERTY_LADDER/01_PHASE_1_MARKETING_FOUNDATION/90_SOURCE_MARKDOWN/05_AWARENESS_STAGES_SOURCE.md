The "Problem Aware", "Solution Aware", and "Product Aware" concepts come from Eugene Schwartz's 1966 classic copywriting and marketing book, Breakthrough Advertising. He defined five distinct levels of customer awareness to describe what the buyer knows about their pain point and your offering.

The five levels are:

Unaware: The prospect doesn't know they have a problem or need.

Problem-Aware: The prospect feels the pain but doesn't know a solution exists.

Solution-Aware: The prospect knows solutions exist but doesn't know about your specific product.

Product-Aware: The prospect knows your product exists but isn't sure if it's the right fit for them.

Most Aware: The prospect knows your product well and just needs a final incentive or push to buy.

Where second property, decoupling, NAV and Exit Before Entry fit in awareness

There are two concepts that are often mixed up:

Funnel position: cold, warm or hot audience.

Customer awareness: how much the person already understands.

A decoupling advertisement can be shown to a cold Meta audience, but the message itself is solution-aware because the person already knows decoupling may be an option.

Correct Schwartz awareness mapping for our business

The correct awareness ladder-for marketing

Level 1 — Desire aware

Desire creates attraction

Desire tells people:

“This is the future I want.”

It is usually:

less confrontational;

easier to engage with;

more aspirational;

better for broader cold audiences;

suitable for homeowners who do not feel urgent pain.

Examples:

Own your second property.
Turn one property into more family options.
Make your strongest earning years count.
Build toward rental income and greater flexibility.

They know what they want:

“We want another property.”

Use:

own the next property;

create rental income;

stop depending on one home;

use our present equity better;

build more options.

This is your broadest Door A entry.

They want a better outcome but have not clearly identified the obstacle.

Their thoughts:

“It would be good to own another property.”
“Maybe our current home can help us do more.”
“Other couples seem to be progressing faster.”
“We should probably plan before we get older.”

Marketing job:

make the outcome feel possible;

show what they may gain;

help them picture a stronger future;

reveal that their current property may be the starting point.

Example:

Your first property may not be the finish line. It could be the starting point for your next one.

Level 2 — Problem aware

Problems create urgency

Problems tell people:

“Something may prevent me from getting what I want.”

They are usually:

more immediate;

more emotionally intense;

stronger for people already stuck;

effective for retargeting and solution-aware prospects.

Examples:

Both names are tied up.
Waiting may reduce your loan runway.
A wrong second property may block the third move.
Decoupling without a plan may waste the freed name.

They know something is blocking them:

“Both names are tied up.”
“We do not know whether to keep or sell.”
“We may be wasting time.”
“Our home went up, but we have no plan.”

Use:

both-name problem;

paper gain but no next move;

shrinking loan runway;

one-property dependence;

right actions in the wrong order.

They recognise something is blocking the desire.

Their thoughts:

“Both names are tied up.”
“We do not know whether to keep or sell.”
“Our property increased in value, but we have no plan.”
“We are worried we may act in the wrong order.”

Marketing job:

name the obstacle;

show the consequence;

create urgency without exaggerating;

explain why doing nothing is also a decision.

Example:

Your property may have increased in value—but if it has not created your next move, the gain may still be sitting idle.

Level 3 — Solution aware

They already know possible tactics:

“Maybe we should decouple.”
“Maybe sell one and buy two.”
“Maybe refinance.”
“Maybe upgrade first.”

This is where A3 belongs.

Your message is:

The tactic is not the strategy.

Your existing client intelligence states the same hard truth: freeing one name is only a mechanism; using the free name without knowing what it must achieve two moves later can become an expensive mistake.

They know tactics exist:

decoupling;

selling one and buying two;

refinancing;

buying under one name;

upgrading;

buying new launch or resale.

Marketing job:

challenge the tactic-first belief;

show that the same tactic produces different results for different households;

explain why route, order and property selection matter.

Example:

Decoupling can free one name. It cannot tell you whether the property bought under that name is the right one.

Level 4 — Mechanism aware

They understand why your approach is different:

“We need the correct route and order—not merely a property recommendation.”

Introduce:

the Five Moves;

route comparison;

Hold Two versus Trade Up First;

sequence before selection;

Exit Before Entry;

Your Next 3 Moves.

The mechanism explains:

“Why should Edmund’s process produce a better decision?”

The mechanism is the bridge between knowing that solutions exist and believing your offer is different.

For example:

Solution they already know

“I need someone to advise whether to decouple and what to buy.”

Your mechanism

“We define the job of the next property, reject what fails that job, then research and rank what survives.”

Your product

“The Second Property Ladder service applies that process to your household and later produces the researched shortlist.”

Therefore:

Share the method. Reserve the personalised prescription.

Level 5 — Product aware

“I know Edmund has a specific service that may solve this.”

They know your tangible offer:

Second Property Readiness Check;

Second Property Strategy Session;

Second Property Route Map;

client case studies;

what they receive;

what happens during the session.

Product-aware does not mean they know about NAV or exit strategy.

It means they know:

“Edmund has a specific process and deliverable that solves this problem.”

They know your specific service or process exists.

They understand that you provide:

the Second Property Readiness Check;

the Second Property Ladder;

the Strategy Session;

the Route Map;

the later property research and shortlist.

Marketing job:

demonstrate how your process works;

show sample outputs;

show rejected options;

show case studies;

make the service tangible.

Example:

We first define what your next property must achieve. Then we reject the options that fail the plan before building the shortlist.

Level 6 — Most aware

They understand your offer and are deciding whether to act.

They need:

founder proof;

client proof;

sample outputs;

clear scope;

objection handling;

what happens next;

evidence that you sometimes recommend waiting or not buying.

They know your process and are considering whether to work with you.

Marketing job:

remove final uncertainty;

establish credibility;

show the engagement steps;

demonstrate results;

answer why they should act now;

make the CTA clear.

Example:

See how we mapped one couple’s route, rejected 14 unsuitable options and narrowed the market to three properties that protected their next move.

Where NAV fits

NAV is not your primary marketing hook.

It is an explanation of why the second property matters.

People do not normally wake up thinking:

“I need to optimise my net asset value.”

They think:

“Can we own another?”

“Are we wasting our current property?”

“Should we sell?”

“Are we too late?”

“What should we do with both names?”

“Will one property be enough?”

Use NAV in middle-funnel education as:

The goal is not merely owning more properties. It is building a stronger overall position and more options.

In public language, use:

stronger position;

more options;

greater flexibility;

less dependence on one home;

a better route for the next 10–20 years.

Introduce the term net asset value only after they understand the practical benefit.

Your Marketing OS already contains the correct principle: lead with the climb and use the safe order as the reason to trust you.

Where Exit Before Entry fits

Exit Before Entry is your decision doctrine, not the entire front-end offer.

Use it strongly for:

people already viewing;

people who have decoupled;

new-launch versus resale comparisons;

ready movers;

project shortlists;

retargeting;

strategy-session differentiation.

Example:

You may be able to buy the second property. But who will buy it from you later—and will it still help your third move?

It can also be used in top-of-funnel content for ready movers, but it should not replace the main desire:

Get the next property through the correct route.
