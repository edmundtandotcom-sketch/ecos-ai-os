Doctrine v1.0

Core belief

A property does not qualify because it is popular, profitable-looking or affordable today. It qualifies only when it fits the household route, survives the holding period, remains sellable to a clear future buyer and preserves the next move.

Public process

Define the Job → Reject What Fails → Research What Remains → Rank the Best Fit

Public promise

We do not begin by asking which project you like. We first define what the property must achieve, screen the relevant Singapore market and remove the options that would fail your plan.

Strongest differentiation line

The project presentation is not the beginning of our process. It is the result of everything that has already been rejected.

We should not use absolute vetoes

Your clarification is correct.

A property does not automatically fail merely because:

it has fewer than 100 units;

rentability is weaker;

supply is higher;

entry price is expensive;

the future audience is narrower.

The context matters.

A 70-unit project may still make sense when:

it is genuinely scarce;

there are few competing alternatives;

owner-occupier demand is strong;

the price is reasonable;

the location is difficult to replicate;

the client accepts lower transaction liquidity;

the intended holding period is long enough.

Likewise, a property with average rentability may still be appropriate as a family home when the primary objective is not rental income.

Better decision structure

Instead of Pass/Fail, use four classifications.

Critical Conflict

The property directly contradicts the client’s agreed route.

Example:

The plan requires preserving future borrowing capacity, but this purchase consumes almost all of it.

Significant Risk

The weakness could materially affect performance or exit.

Example:

There may be heavy competing supply when the client reaches the intended selling period.

Acceptable Trade-Off

The property is weaker in one area, but the client gains something more important.

Example:

Rental yield is average, but the project has strong family demand, usable space and a broader resale audience.

Preference Issue

Personal rather than strategic.

Example:

The client prefers a higher floor, but a lower-floor unit meets the investment and liveability requirements at a much better price.

This preserves flexibility without allowing attractive features to excuse serious weaknesses.

The override rule

A property with a Critical Conflict or Significant Risk may still proceed only when we clearly document:

What the weakness is

Why the client is prepared to accept it

Which strong attributes compensate for it

What the client gives up

What price makes the trade-off acceptable

What future event would cause us to review or exit

This is stronger than pretending that every selection rule is absolute.
