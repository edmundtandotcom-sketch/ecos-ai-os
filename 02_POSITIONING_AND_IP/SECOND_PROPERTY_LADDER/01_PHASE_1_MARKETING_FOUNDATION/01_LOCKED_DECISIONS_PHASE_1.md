## 3. Category and positioning

### Category

**The Second Property Ladder™**

### Direct desire

Help an existing owner work towards a **second property**.

### Honest advisory position

A second property is not automatically the correct immediate recommendation.

Valid routes include:

- build towards two;
- strengthen one property first;
- restructure;
- prepare and wait;
- no-go when the evidence does not support a move.

### Core belief

A good property can still be the wrong property for the household's plan.

### Stronger commercial outcome

The service must not stop at confirming a route.

It must eventually help the client:

- decide what the next property must achieve;
- narrow the relevant market;
- remove weak fits;
- compare the strongest survivors;
- identify the best-fitting projects and units;
- execute the purchase without breaking the next move.

## 4. Campaign architecture

Use **two campaign doors**, not two separate businesses.

### Door A — Second Property

For:

- joint-name private-property owners;
- suitable EC owners;
- DINK couples;
- couples considering decoupling;
- existing owners actively wanting the next property.

Core question:

> How can we own the second property, and what must happen first?

Primary public wording:

> Already own one property? See whether you can get the next one—and what must happen first.

### Door B — HDB/EC Next Route

For:

- HDB owners with an estimated value of approximately **S$800,000+**;
- EC owners with an estimated value of approximately **S$1.5 million+**;
- households deciding whether to keep, sell, upgrade, restructure or build towards two.

Core question:

> What should our current HDB or EC unlock next?

Public qualification callout:

> Own an HDB worth around S$800K+ or an EC worth around S$1.5M+?

Property value is a campaign filter, not final qualification. Final suitability still depends on equity, income, funds, ownership, age, loan runway, timeline, alignment and willingness to proceed.

### Landing pages

- Two message-matched landing pages.
- Multiple ad angles may lead to each page.
- One Readiness Check and one advisory backend.

## 5. Awareness model

Use the following internal campaign-planning stages:

1. **Desire-Aware**
2. **Problem-Aware**
3. **Solution-Aware**
4. **Product-Aware**
5. **Decision / Most-Aware**

This is a practical adaptation for the business. Do not publicly claim that it replaces Eugene Schwartz's original model.

### Meaning

- Desire-Aware: wants another property, rental income, more options or better use of current equity.
- Problem-Aware: recognises names, timing, funds, current property or wrong-order risk as obstacles.
- Solution-Aware: knows tactics such as decoupling, upgrading, selling one and buying two, refinancing or buying under one name.
- Product-Aware: knows the Second Property Ladder and its specific process exist.
- Decision / Most-Aware: is deciding whether to engage and needs proof, scope, process and confidence.

### Marketing principle

Desire attracts. Problems create urgency. The strongest campaigns connect:

> desired future + obstacle + credible new path

## 8. Safety Net

Use the term **Safety Net**, not Holding Buffer or reserve rule.

### Starting baseline

At least **12 months of property commitments** after completing the purchase.

### Includes

- mortgage payments for the relevant property position;
- maintenance fees;
- property tax;
- property insurance where applicable;
- expected rental shortfall or vacancy exposure;
- fixed property-related commitments.

### Excludes from the baseline

- general household living expenses;
- projected bonuses;
- hoped-for capital gains;
- hoped-for rent above a conservative assumption;
- money already committed to legal fees, renovation, completion or other known expenses.

The 12-month baseline may be increased based on income stability, number of properties, reliance on rental, completion risk and household circumstances.

## 9. Selection judgement

Do not use rigid universal vetoes.

Use these risk classifications:

- Critical Conflict
- Significant Risk
- Acceptable Trade-Off
- Preference Issue

Context, location, project, price, unit availability and the client's objective matter.

A risk may be accepted only when the trade-off, compensating strengths, acceptable price and future review trigger are documented.

### Scoring

Use **route-specific scoring**, not one universal weighting.

Combine:

- numerical score;
- evidence;
- risks;
- trade-offs;
- Edmund's professional judgement.

Suggested evidence areas:

1. Plan Fit
2. Entry Position
3. Demand Growth
4. Exit Depth
5. Supply & Liquidity
6. Holding Strength
7. Unit Usability

Do not allow a high total score to average away a serious strategic weakness.

### Required advisor conclusion

- Strong Fit
- Fit at the Right Price
- Conditional Fit
- Not Recommended

Every conclusion should state:

- main reason;
- main weakness;
- accepted trade-off;
- maximum acceptable price;
- intended holding period;
- confidence;
- review triggers;
- conditions that would change the recommendation.

## 10. Mandatory future-buyer analysis

Approved as compulsory in every recommendation.

For each recommended property, define:

- primary future buyer;
- secondary future buyer;
- likely household income;
- likely down payment;
- likely mortgage;
- why they would want the property;
- competing alternatives;
- whether the future buyer pool is expanding or shrinking;
- whether the expected future selling quantum remains financeable.

Core question:

> At the future selling price, how many households can realistically afford and want this exact property?

## 11. Selection evidence

The current doctrine considers, among other items:

- entry price and quantum;
- comparable resale and new launch;
- similar-age properties;
- OCR, RCR and CCR price gaps;
- land prices and replacement cost;
- transformation and master-plan catalysts;
- timing and certainty of transformation;
- future demand created;
- competing supply created;
- exit audience;
- future affordability;
- rentability;
- transaction liquidity;
- project size;
- unit mix;
- layout and daily liveability;
- usable versus wasted space;
- premiums for MRT, integration, schools, view, floor, developer, facilities and extra space;
- initial launch versus balance-unit price;
- completion-year supply;
- holding period;
- ownership structure;
- next-move preservation.

Small developments below 100 units are a risk flag, not an automatic rejection.

## 13. Holding and Year-4 Review

- Intended positioning: approximately four-to-six-year mid-term play, adjusted by client and property.
- Year 4 is a formal review checkpoint, not an automatic selling date.
- Use the actual purchase date and applicable SSD rules for every client.
- Review:
  - whether the property has done its job;
  - future growth reasons;
  - increasing risks;
  - current exit audience;
  - rental performance;
  - future supply;
  - net equity available;
  - better use of the released equity;
  - ability to add, restructure or progress.

Possible decisions:

- Hold
- Sell and redeploy
- Restructure
- Prepare for the next move
- Review again at a defined trigger

An Exit Review Doctrine is deferred to a later phase.

## 14. Client journey

Working structure:

> Discovery → Review → Evaluate → Identify

### Discovery

Understand the current position, desired future, obstacles, readiness, decision-makers, cost of inaction and fit.

### Review

Analyse property, finances, ownership, sale requirement, routes, affordability, age, loan runway, Safety Net and downside.

### Evaluate

Use personalised education to explain the recommended route, market evidence and the criteria the next property must meet.

Avoid turning this into a generic market lecture.

### Identify

After formal engagement:

- define the relevant market;
- research new launch and resale;
- remove weak fits;
- compare survivors;
- recommend projects and units;
- execute.

The Discovery + REI Method Advisory Audit is a separate subset and must not derail Phase 1 marketing.
