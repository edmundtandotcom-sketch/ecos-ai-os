## Purpose

Turn a real client decision tension into a clear public hook without changing the advisory truth.

## Required inputs

Before generating a hook, identify:

- Door A or Door B;
- actual property/ownership position;
- awareness stage;
- desired future option;
- current assumption or failed solution;
- material consequence;
- governing Second Property Ladder principle;
- evidence/proof available;
- content format;
- CTA and next commitment.

## Approved trigger families

### 1. Position trigger

The current property appears successful, but the household does not know what position it has created.

### 2. Order trigger

Individually sensible moves become harmful when executed in the wrong sequence.

### 3. Optionality trigger

A move looks attractive now but may remove future ownership, financing or exit options.

### 4. Ownership-structure trigger

Names, ownership and financing structure are being treated as administration instead of strategy. Decoupling is a mechanism, not the strategy.

### 5. Exit-before-entry trigger

The household evaluates what to buy before defining who must buy it from them later and what the move must unlock.

### 6. Survivability trigger

The route appears affordable under normal conditions but has not been tested against buffer, holding and downside pressure.

### 7. Decision-clarity trigger

More projects, comparisons and opinions increase information without resolving which move should happen first.

### 8. Timing trigger

Waiting, selling, restructuring or buying creates a clock. The hook must explain the consequence without manufacturing urgency.

### 9. One-property-trap trigger

The household’s entire property outcome remains dependent on one asset, one exit and one future buyer pool.

### 10. Family-alignment trigger

Different decision-makers are solving different problems without one shared direction. Family situations remain inside Door A/Door B and A0–A2; do not recreate Family Legacy or Mash-up as standalone doors.

## Hook build sequence

1. Identify the recognisable situation.
2. Name the flawed assumption.
3. Show the hidden consequence.
4. Introduce one governing reframe.
5. Open the loop toward diagnosis.
6. Use the minimum relevant CTA.

## Hook quality test

A strong hook is specific, understandable without internal jargon, accurate without exaggeration, connected to a real decision and able to lead naturally to the Readiness Check or Strategy Session.

## Retired language

Do not revive Family Legacy as a campaign door, Mash-up as a door/stage, SI-06, A3/A4, broad Legacy Launch positioning, “decouple first, decide later” messaging, fake scarcity or unverified financial outcomes.
