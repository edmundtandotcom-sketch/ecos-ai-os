## 1. Operating Role

This file governs what happens after a lead, prospect, consult attendee, past client, or old database contact does not move immediately.

Nurture is not generic follow-up. Nurture is the discipline of staying relevant until readiness changes.

The purpose is to make sure every qualified contact has:
- a clear reason they are not moving now;
- a next trigger to monitor;
- a next review date;
- a recommended next action;
- a suitable communication rhythm;
- a path back into Diagnosis Call, Strategy Session, ProDeck review, or client development.

## 2. Core Principle

Time does not create readiness. Events create readiness.

People usually move when something changes: property status, family status, financial status, market context, spouse alignment, belief clarity, or internal conviction.

Operations must therefore track what changed, what is likely to change next, and what conversation should be ready when that change happens.

## 3. Nurture Is Not A Holding Bin

Nurture is an active relationship state. A contact in nurture is not forgotten, but also not over-served.

| Nurture State | Meaning | Advisor Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| Active Nurture | Clear fit, real intent, but one obstacle remains | Human follow-up with specific context |
| Trigger Watch | Fit is likely, but timing depends on a future event | Monitor dates and re-open before event |
| Content Nurture | Low urgency or early awareness | Authority content and light check-ins |
| Client Development | Past client or completed case with future rung potential | Review asset, exit thesis, next rung, referral |
| Parked / Excluded | Poor fit, weak intent, no realistic pathway, or not advisory aligned | Do not chase; revive only if facts change |

## 4. Priority Model

Priority is determined by fit, readiness, trigger strength, engagement, and opportunity value. Lead source alone does not determine priority.

| Priority | Profile | Action |
|---|---|---|
| P1 Highest Priority | Tier 1, clear fit, active trigger, strong engagement, near-term decision | Advisor outreach; book or confirm consult within 72 hours where possible |
| P2 High Priority | Strong Tier 2, clear intent, one major obstacle, warm engagement | Belief-specific follow-up; move toward Diagnosis or Strategy Session |
| P3 Medium Priority | Tier 2 or Tier 3, forming trigger, occasional engagement | Content-led nurture with periodic personal check-in |
| P4 Low Priority | Low trigger, low urgency, general education audience | Automation or broad authority content only |
| Excluded | Outside advisory fit or no meaningful pathway | Close, park, or redirect politely |

## 5. Trigger Monitoring System

Every qualified lead should have a primary trigger and a next trigger to monitor.

| Trigger Type | Examples | What It May Activate |
|---|---|---|
| Property Trigger | SSD expiry, TOP, valuation change, rental renewal, lease age, exit offer, MOP | Exit review, HDB / condo exit safety, reinvestment review |
| Family Trigger | Child turns 21, child starts work, marriage, spouse alignment, retirement planning, inheritance | Family/child-name structure review inside a standard A1/A2 engagement (the Family Legacy door and A3/A4 levels are retired — Decisions 058/063) |
| Financial Trigger | Bonus, asset sale, income increase, loan approval, savings milestone, CPF/cash readiness | Capacity review and pathway assessment |
| Market Trigger | Rate shift, supply shift, price gap, new launch window, policy change | Entry / exit timing review |
| Behaviour Trigger | WhatsApp reply, video binge, form submission, returning enquiry, guide download, consult rebooking | Reactivation and diagnosis update |
| Belief Trigger | Repeated concern, spouse objection, fear of timing, fear of overpaying, complexity resistance | Belief resolution and proof-based follow-up |

## 7. Readiness Direction

Readiness should be updated after meaningful interactions.

| Direction | Meaning | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Increasing | Momentum improved; facts or confidence changed | Increase human attention; offer consult or review |
| Stable | No meaningful change | Maintain relevance; use content or scheduled check-in |
| Declining | Momentum weakened or trigger passed | Reduce intensity; preserve relationship; set later review |
| Unknown | Not enough data | Ask one diagnostic question before advising |

## 8. Follow-Up Cadence

Cadence should match priority, not advisor emotion.

| Contact Type | Suggested Rhythm | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| P1 Active Trigger | Same day to weekly | Use direct human contact |
| P2 Warming | Weekly to monthly | Tie each follow-up to obstacle or trigger |
| P3 Forming Trigger | Monthly to quarterly | Use content plus occasional personal check-in |
| P4 Content Audience | Quarterly or automated | Do not spend heavy advisor time |
| Past Client Review | Quarterly to annually | Review asset, valuation, exit thesis, next rung |
| Parked / Excluded | No fixed cadence | Only revive if facts change |

## 9. Reactivation Scenarios

| Scenario | Signal | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Behaviour Reactivation | Replies after silence, watches many videos, submits another form, asks about a project | Update CRM, identify new trigger, offer Diagnosis Call if fit is clear |
| Property Reactivation | SSD/TOP/MOP/rental/valuation event arrives | Run property position review and possible exit / redeploy conversation |
| Family Reactivation | Child eligibility, spouse alignment, inheritance, retirement, marriage | Run family structure review |
| Financial Reactivation | Cash, CPF, bonus, asset sale, loan readiness changes | Run capacity and pathway review |
| Belief Reactivation | Same objection repeats or new concern appears | Use belief-specific proof and invite to clarify risk |
| Past Client Reactivation | Asset result, exit window, referral, child ladder, new family goal | Run client development review |

## 10. Follow-Up Message Rules

Do not chase without context.

Every follow-up should contain at least one of these:
- what changed;
- why it matters to their situation;
- what risk or option has appeared;
- what previous concern this relates to;
- what decision window is approaching;
- what simple next step would clarify things.

Avoid weak follow-up such as:
- “just checking in”; 
- “any update?”;
- “still interested?”;
- “market moving fast” without context;
- project-pushing without diagnosis.

## 11. Database Review Rhythm

The database should be reviewed before increasing ad spend.

| Review Segment | What To Look For |
|---|---|
| P1 / P2 Leads | Active or warming prospects with clear advisory fit |
| Previous Consults | People who delayed due to timing, spouse, capital, or confidence |
| Ghosted High-Trigger Leads | People who had a strong trigger but disappeared |
| Webinar / Event Leads | Educated audience that may have renewed readiness |
| Past Clients | Exit thesis, next rung, referral, child ladder, redeployment |
| Family Legacy Prospects (historic campaign tag — door retired per Decision 058; nurture them as standard A1/A2 family situations) | Child status, family alignment, capital availability |
| HDB / Condo Exit Watch | Lease age, MOP, SSD, rental, upgrading window, exit risk |

## 13. Success Test

A nurtured contact is well-managed when any advisor, admin, or AI system can open the record and know:
- who they are;
- what they own;
- where they are in the journey;
- why they are not moving now;
- what changed last;
- what trigger to monitor next;
- when to follow up;
- what conversation should be sent next.

## 14. Golden Rule

Do not chase people. Stay relevant until readiness changes.

When readiness changes, the right conversation should already be waiting.
