# 01_DIAGNOSIS_CALL_SYSTEM_v2.0

Status: ACTIVE SOURCE OF TRUTH

Purpose: This file governs the first serious conversation: triage, diagnosis, qualification, and consult readiness.

## 1. Core Principle

The first call is not for selling.

It is for understanding.

The advisor’s job is to find out:

- what triggered the enquiry;
- what position the person is really in;
- what future outcome matters;
- who else is involved in the decision;
- what belief block is stopping movement;
- whether the person is worth a proper Strategy Session;
- what should happen next.

Do not advise deeply before the position is known.

Do not recommend a project before diagnosis.

Do not answer “which property is best?” before understanding the client’s numbers, names, timeline, family structure, risk tolerance, and desired future position.

## 2. Diagnosis Call Outcome

By the end of a good diagnosis call, the advisor should be able to state:

- Who is this person or family?
- What property position are they in?
- What triggered them now?
- What are they really trying to achieve?
- Which Ascent Stage are they in?
- What buyer segment or psychology is present?
- What is the dominant belief block?
- Who must be involved before any decision can move?
- What information is missing?
- Are they Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3, or Excluded?
- What is the next best action?

If the advisor cannot answer these, the call is not complete.

## 3. Simple Call Structure

### Phase 1 — Control The Frame

Use a calm opener:

“Before I share any views, let me first understand your current property situation, what you are trying to achieve, and what is making this unclear right now. Then we can see whether it makes sense to map this properly. Fair?”

The frame is important because it tells the client:

- this is not a project pitch;
- advice comes after diagnosis;
- the advisor is not rushing;
- the client needs to participate honestly.

### Phase 2 — Trigger Discovery

Find the “why now”.

Useful prompts:

- What prompted you to reach out?
- Why is this worth discussing now?
- What changed recently?
- Was it valuation, spouse discussion, project interest, MOP/TOP/SSD, child milestone, market movement, or something else?
- What made this feel more urgent than before?

Capture the exact trigger. Generic labels are not enough.

### Phase 3 — Current Position

Understand today before discussing tomorrow.

Capture:

- current property type and estimated value;
- loan position;
- years held;
- ownership names;
- spouse or family involvement;
- income, CPF, cash, and broad capacity;
- current home / investment / family situation;
- timeline and constraints;
- what advice they have already received.

Do not over-prescribe. The purpose is to understand the starting point.

### Phase 4 — Desired Future

Move from “what property?” to “what position?”

Ask:

- What are you hoping this next move achieves?
- If this is solved properly, what becomes possible?
- What future options do you want to create?
- What does a strong outcome look like in 3, 5, or 10 years?
- What would your future self thank you for reviewing earlier?

This is where the client starts to own the reason for the conversation.

### Phase 5 — Ascent Stage And Property Position

Use one main stage system only.

Active Ascent Stages:

- A1 First Climb: current property position should unlock a structured next move.
- A2 Compound: exit, redeployment, optimisation, or second-cycle planning matters.

> **RETIRED (Decision 063, 2026-07-11):** the former A3 Legacy and A4 Bespoke Portfolio stages are removed — the Ascent Model is A0–A2. Family capital, children's names, multi-generational and multi-property situations are diagnosed inside standard A1/A2 engagements.

Property Position is only context.

Examples:

- one private condo that appreciated;
- qualified HDB/EC upgrade-window household;
- DINK couple before major lifestyle constraints;
- decoupling/free-name relevance;
- parent with adult child name;
- portfolio owner needing exit/redeploy clarity.

A0 / Activation is not a standalone active stage. “My property went up, now what?” is an awareness moment inside A1 First Climb.

### Phase 6 — Buyer Segment And Decision Style

Classify lightly. Do not force-fit.

Possible buyer segment signals:

- Ready Mover: has trigger, capacity, timing, and decision pressure.
- DINK / Golden Window: high-income couple with flexible years before children or bigger constraints.
- Decoupling Planner: name structure or ABSD efficiency is relevant, but they may mistake mechanism for strategy.
- Qualified HDB/EC Upgrade-Window: real upgrade or next-move window, not generic HDB/EC owner.
- Family Planner: parent, adult child, family capital, or legacy structure is central.
- Exit / Redeploy Owner: asset has performed and next cycle matters.
- Portfolio / Bespoke: multiple assets, names, cashflow, or protection issues.

Possible decision styles:

- Analytical: needs proof, numbers, comparisons.
- Validation Seeker: wants confidence and similar cases.
- Collaborative: spouse or family alignment is central.
- Opportunity Seeker: future options and growth matter.
- Comfort Preserver: safety and lifestyle protection matter.
- Action Taker: wants clean next steps once convinced.

## 4. Belief Block Discovery

Do not stop at surface objection.

A client may say:

- “Need to think.”
- “Market too high.”
- “Everything is expensive.”
- “Spouse not convinced.”
- “Decoupling sounds risky.”
- “I don’t want to take more loan.”
- “I’m not an investor.”
- “Maybe just wait.”

The advisor should listen for the belief underneath:

- fear of wrong timing;
- need more certainty;
- comfort greater than growth;
- sell high buy higher;
- fully paid equals safe;
- new launches are overpriced;
- spouse / family not aligned;
- complexity means danger;
- trust deficit;
- identity conflict.

The diagnosis call should identify the belief. It does not need to resolve everything immediately.

## 5. Decision-Maker Rule

Do not close one champion while the sceptic is absent.

Before a Strategy Session, clarify:

- Who owns the current property?
- Who contributes financially?
- Who must agree before any move?
- Who is excited?
- Who is worried?
- Who will block the decision if they are not included?

If the spouse, parent, child, or co-owner is material to the decision, bring them into the proper session.

## 6. Qualification And Tiering

### Tier 1 — Ready For Strategy Session

Signals:

- clear trigger;
- suitable timing;
- meaningful capacity;
- relevant ownership/name issue;
- decision-makers can attend;
- willing to share numbers;
- wants advisory clarity;
- belief block is resolvable.

Action: book Strategy Session and collect missing information.

### Tier 2 — Active Nurture

Signals:

- strong fit but timing uncertain;
- spouse or family not aligned;
- missing numbers;
- belief block unresolved;
- not ready to act but issue is real.

Action: assign belief-specific follow-up and next trigger.

### Tier 3 — Authority Nurture

Signals:

- early research;
- low urgency;
- no immediate trigger;
- needs education first.

Action: content nurture, case studies, occasional review.

### Excluded

Signals:

- no feasible path;
- no realistic capacity;
- wants only discounts or cheapest unit;
- refuses numbers;
- purely speculative;
- poor values fit;
- no advisory relevance.

Action: do not over-serve.

## 7. Required Pre-Strategy Information

Before deep advice, collect enough information to avoid guessing:

- current property details;
- estimated value;
- outstanding loan;
- ownership names;
- income and loan capacity;
- CPF and cash;
- timeline;
- spouse/family decision involvement;
- risk comfort;
- objectives;
- existing project/options considered.

Rule: no numbers, no real advice.

The doctor analogy is valid internally: no scan, no diagnosis.

## 8. Diagnosis Call Close

A good close sounds like:

“Based on what you shared, I don’t think the issue is simply which property to buy. The bigger issue is that your current position, names, timing, and next 2-3 moves need to be mapped properly before you commit. The next useful step is a Strategy Session where we review your real position, evaluate the options, and identify the safest next move. For that to be useful, we need the relevant numbers and all key decision-makers present.”

Then give one of four next actions:

- book Strategy Session;
- collect documents / numbers first;
- place into active nurture;
- disqualify politely.

## 9. Working Rule

The first call should create clarity, not dependency.

If the client leaves the call thinking “I need to map this properly before I make a move,” the diagnosis call worked.
