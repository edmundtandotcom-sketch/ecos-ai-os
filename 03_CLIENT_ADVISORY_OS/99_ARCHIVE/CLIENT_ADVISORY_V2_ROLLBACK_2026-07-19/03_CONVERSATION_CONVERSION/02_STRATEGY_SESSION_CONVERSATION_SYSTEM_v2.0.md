## 1. Core Principle

The Strategy Session is not a showflat presentation.

It is not a project pitch.

It is the structured advisory session where Coach Edmund + Cindior Ho help the client review their real position, evaluate what the market rewards, and identify the next 2-3 moves.

The client should leave knowing:

- where they really stand;
- what they are not seeing;
- what the market may reward or punish;
- what pathways are suitable or unsuitable;
- what to avoid;
- what next move makes the most sense;
- what happens next.

## 2. Pre-Session Gate

Do not run a proper Strategy Session until enough diagnosis is known.

Before the session, confirm:

- Ascent Stage;
- Property Position;
- Strategic Intent;
- Buyer Segment;
- Decision-Maker Map;
- Trigger Moment;
- Belief Block;
- Tier / Readiness;
- Numbers needed for meaningful advice;
- Missing information;
- Next action the client is open to.

If the information is weak, the next step is not “strategy session”. The next step is data collection or another diagnosis conversation.

## 3. Official REI Spine

REI is the consult diagnosis method.

### R — Review Real Position

Question: Where is the client really standing now?

Review:

- current property / home / asset position;
- equity and loan position;
- CPF / cash / liquidity;
- income and loan capacity;
- ownership names;
- spouse / family structure;
- age and timeline;
- constraints;
- current assumptions;
- current risk;
- current opportunity cost;
- current Ascent Stage.

Output: the client sees their real position more clearly than before.

Success sign: “I never looked at it that way.”

### E — Evaluate Market Rewards

Question: What does the market reward or punish now and later?

Evaluate:

- hold / sell / buy again / decouple / upgrade / redeploy paths;
- new launch vs resale trade-offs;
- exit buyer pool;
- supply and demand;
- quantum and affordability;
- rental and holding risk;
- timeline and market windows;
- ABSD / ownership / name considerations where relevant;
- opportunity cost of waiting;
- downside scenarios.

Output: the client understands what each pathway gives and what each pathway costs.

Success sign: “Now I understand why this option may or may not fit me.”

### I — Identify Next 2-3 Moves

Question: What sequence should the client follow?

Identify:

- strongest pathway;
- avoid list;
- next move;
- move after that;
- timeline;
- decision gate;
- data still needed;
- risk safeguards;
- implementation sequence;
- next review trigger.

Output: the client knows what makes sense, why it makes sense, and what happens next.

Success sign: “This is the roadmap.”

## 4. Strategy Session Flow

### Step 1 — Reset The Frame

Start with:

“Today’s goal is not to push you into a property. The goal is to review your real position, evaluate your options, and identify the next 2-3 moves before you make the next 1 move.”

### Step 2 — Replay Diagnosis

Summarise:

- what they own;
- what they want;
- what triggered them;
- who matters;
- what they are worried about;
- what decision they are trying to make.

Ask:

“Did I capture your situation correctly?”

This creates trust and prevents misdiagnosis.

### Step 3 — Name The Real Decision

Most clients think the decision is:

- which property;
- which project;
- whether now is expensive;
- whether decoupling is possible;
- whether to upgrade;
- whether to wait.

The advisor reframes the real decision:

- What should this property position unlock?
- What future position are we building?
- What next 2-3 moves are we protecting?
- What should we avoid so you do not get stuck?

### Step 4 — Review Real Position

Use facts, not hype.

Show the current position:

- equity;
- debt;
- age / tenure;
- cash / CPF;
- names;
- family;
- timeline;
- current property performance;
- risk if unchanged;
- opportunity if reviewed.

Do not overcomplicate. The client must see clearly.

### Step 5 — Reveal Hidden Gap

Examples of hidden gaps:

- property went up, but strategy did not;
- fully paid feels safe, but future flexibility may be shrinking;
- decoupling frees a name, but does not create strategy by itself;
- upgrading bigger may reduce future options;
- waiting feels safe, but can quietly close windows;
- buying the “best project” is not the same as building a next-move plan;
- HDB/EC upgrade is only relevant when timing, capacity, and pathway are real.

### Step 6 — Evaluate Pathways

Compare realistic options only.

Possible pathways:

- hold and review later;
- sell and buy again;
- decouple and structure;
- upgrade safely;
- new launch vs resale;
- exit and redeploy;
- parent-child / legacy structure;
- build capital first;
- no action now.

Each pathway should be judged by:

- fit to Ascent Stage;
- fit to Strategic Intent;
- financial safety;
- exit buyer pool;
- next move after this;
- family alignment;
- risk of regret;
- what it unlocks.

### Step 7 — Identify Recommended Sequence

The recommendation should be a sequence, not a single property.

Output format:

- Current Position
- Target Position
- Main Risk
- Main Opportunity
- Pathway Options
- Recommended Pathway
- Avoid List
- Next 2-3 Moves
- Immediate Next Action
- Follow-Up Trigger

## 5. Conversation Rules

Never start with property.

Never start with project.

Never start with developer marketing.

Never start with “good deal”.

Start with position.

Do not close a champion while the sceptic is absent.

Do not force-fit clients outside advisory fit.

Do not use “advisor not agent” as the main point. Demonstrate it through diagnosis.

Do not create false certainty. Confidence comes from structure, not prediction.

## 8. Strategy Session Close

Do not close by pressure.

Close by clarity.

The final close should answer:

- What is the decision?
- What is the recommended next action?
- What information or document is needed?
- Who needs to be involved?
- What is the timeline?
- What happens if they do nothing?
- What review trigger should be tracked?

Possible closes:

### Proceed

“You are ready to move into implementation planning. The next action is…”

### Prepare

“We are not ready to decide today because these numbers / decision-makers / documents are missing. The next action is…”

### Nurture

“The direction is valid, but timing or belief block is not ready. We should track this trigger…”

### No Fit

“This may not be the right strategy for you now. The safer action is…”

## 9. Working Rule

A successful Strategy Session does not make the client dependent on hype.

It makes the client sharper, safer, and more decisive.
