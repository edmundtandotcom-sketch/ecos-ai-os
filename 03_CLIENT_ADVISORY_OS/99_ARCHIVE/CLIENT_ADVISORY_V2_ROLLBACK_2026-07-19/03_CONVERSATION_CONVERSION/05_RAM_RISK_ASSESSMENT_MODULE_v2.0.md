## Purpose

RAM — Risk Assessment Module — is the structured financial safety framework used inside the Strategy Session (client-facing: Next Move Strategy Session™).

Its purpose is to remove the fear of financial over-commitment and demonstrate that the recommended move is structurally safe — not just theoretically sound. RAM resolves fear **with numbers and structure, never with urgency**.

**When to deploy RAM:**
- After REI Step E (Evaluate Market Rewards) — once pathways are understood but the client hesitates on financials
- When the client signals concern about affordability or loan exposure
- When Belief Block RB-05 (Fully Paid Equals Safe), RB-03 (Comfort Greater Than Growth), RB-08 (Complexity Means Danger), RB-12 (Monthly Commitment / Installment Stress), RB-16 (Loan Mechanics), RB-17 (CPF), or RB-18 (Opportunity Cost) is active
- When the client says any variation of: "It's too much," "What if something goes wrong?" "Two loans is too risky."

**What RAM is NOT:**
- A financial planning substitute for a licensed financial advisor
- A replacement for bank pre-approval or official loan assessment
- A guarantee of outcome

**RAM shows:** What a well-structured property move looks like when the downside scenarios are planned for. Confidence comes from seeing the stress-tested picture — not from blind optimism, and not from pressure.

## TECHNIQUE 1 — THE 3Ps FRAMEWORK

### What It Is

Property generates wealth through three simultaneous streams — not one. Clients who see only the loan obligation are missing two-thirds of the picture.

| Stream | Description | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| Passive Income | Rental yield from the investment property | Tenant pays monthly rent; income offsets holding cost |
| Principal Income | Loan balance reduction funded by tenant | Every rental dollar that goes toward the mortgage reduces what you owe — on an asset you own |
| Profit | Long-term capital appreciation | Asset increases in value over the holding period |

### Purpose in the Advisory Session

Use the 3Ps to reframe the client's mental model from:
> "I am taking on more debt"

to:

> "I am acquiring an asset that generates three parallel streams — and my tenant helps fund two of them."

### Advisory Script

*"Before we look at the numbers, let me show you how this works. When you purchase an investment property, three things happen simultaneously.*

*First — Passive Income. Your tenant pays rent every month. That income directly offsets your holding cost. In many scenarios, it covers 60–80% of the monthly mortgage.*

*Second — Principal Income. Every dollar of that rent that goes toward the loan reduces what you owe. Your tenant is building your net worth.*

*Third — Profit. Over time, the asset appreciates. When you exit, the capital gain is yours.*

*Three streams. Running at the same time. The loan is not a burden — it is the structure that activates all three."*

### When to Use

- Primary use: When client sees only the liability side of a second property
- Profile: All profiles, especially the comfort-protective and analytical dispositions
- Belief Block: **RB-05 (Fully Paid Equals Safe)** primary; **RB-03 (Comfort Greater Than Growth)**; related **RB-16 (Loan Mechanics / Why Borrow When I Have Cash)**, **RB-18 (Opportunity Cost / Competing Asset Class)**

## TECHNIQUE 2 — SAFETY NET

### What It Is

The Safety Net is a pre-planned financial resilience structure that shows what happens to the family's property position if income is disrupted — and demonstrates that the move is survivable even in the worst case.

### Five-Layer Safety Net Structure

| Layer | Component | How It Protects |
|---|---|---|
| Layer 1 | Rental Income | Offsets mortgage even if employment income drops temporarily |
| Layer 2 | CPF OA Balance | Can service mortgage instalments in Singapore (CPF OA can be used for private property loan servicing) |
| Layer 3 | Emergency Cash Reserve | 6–12 months of combined mortgage obligations as a liquidity buffer |
| Layer 4 | Stretched Tenure Buffer | If tenure is extended, monthly obligation is already at its most manageable level |
| Layer 5 | Asset Liquidity | The property itself can be sold if sustained disruption requires — but it is the last resort, not the plan |

### Advisory Script

*"One of the biggest concerns families have is: what if something goes wrong? Let me show you the safety structure.*

*First — rental income. Even in a down market, properties in [target area/type] have historically maintained [X]% occupancy. That income alone covers [Y]% of your second mortgage.*

*Second — your CPF. You have [$XX] in your Ordinary Account. Even without touching your salary, that balance services approximately [Z] months of mortgage payments.*

*Third — your cash reserve. We are going to recommend you hold [6 months' worth] in a liquid account before the purchase is made. This is your family's breathing room.*

*So the scenario that concerns you — income disruption — has three layers of protection before the property itself becomes a consideration. This move is not as exposed as it looks on paper."*

### Calculation Framework

Before the strategy session, prepare:

| Item | Figure |
|---|---|
| Estimated rental income from investment property | $XXXX/month |
| Estimated second mortgage monthly payment (at current rates) | $XXXX/month |
| Net monthly cash flow gap (if rented) | +/- $XXX/month |
| CPF OA balance available | $XXXXX |
| Months of CPF coverage (if income stops entirely) | XX months |
| Emergency cash reserve target (6 months of both mortgages) | $XXXXX |

### When to Use

- Primary use: When client expresses fear of income disruption or job loss
- Profile: Comfort-protective; spouse-concern (collaborative) dispositions
- Belief Block: **RB-05 (Fully Paid Equals Safe)**, **RB-12 (Monthly Commitment / Installment Stress)** primary; **RB-03 (Comfort Greater Than Growth)**; related **RB-14 (Renting / Transition-Gap Fear)**, **RB-15 (Exit Risk / Being Stuck)**
- Ascent Stage: Any, but most common at **A1 First Climb**. (Former "Level 4 Legacy" family-safety situations now run inside a standard A1/A2 engagement — the A3 Legacy / A4 stages are RETIRED per Decision 063.)

## TECHNIQUE 3 — STRETCH TENURE

### What It Is

Extending the loan tenure reduces monthly obligation while preserving the ability to make the move. Clients often assume the shortest tenure is the safest — this technique reframes flexibility as a structural asset.

### How It Works

Singapore bank loans for private property are typically available up to:
- Age 65 as the loan ceiling (in most bank assessments)
- Maximum 30-year loan tenure for private property (bank-dependent)

A client aged 40 may qualify for up to 25 years on a resale, and 31–34 years on a new launch (depending on bank policy and property remaining lease).

### Monthly Payment Comparison

| Loan Amount | Tenure | Monthly Payment (est. 3.5% rate) |
|---|---|---|
| $1,000,000 | 25 years | ~$5,000 |
| $1,000,000 | 30 years | ~$4,490 |
| $1,000,000 | 35 years | ~$4,150 |

*Figures are illustrative. Actual rates depend on bank assessment and TDSR position.*

**The Monthly Saving Argument:**
A 10-year tenure extension on a $1M loan reduces monthly obligation by approximately $850/month — $10,200/year — in additional cash flow.

That liquidity can be used as:
- A partial rental income buffer
- An emergency reserve top-up
- A voluntary lump sum prepayment when income allows

### Advisory Script

*"Let me show you something important about how this works.*

*If we take the shorter tenure — 25 years — your monthly obligation is approximately $5,000. At 30 years, it drops to $4,490. That $510/month difference — roughly $6,000/year — stays liquid.*

*Here is the key point: you can always prepay ahead of schedule if you have surplus income. You cannot go back and reduce a commitment you already made at a shorter tenure. Flexibility today creates options tomorrow.*

*The goal is not to hold the loan for 30 years. The goal is to not feel financial pressure every month for the first 5 years while the rental income and the asset position stabilise."*

### When to Use

- Primary use: When client is concerned about monthly cash flow impact of second property
- Profile: Comfort-protective, collaborative, analytical dispositions
- Belief Block: **RB-12 (Monthly Commitment / Installment Stress)** primary; related **RB-05 (Fully Paid Equals Safe)**, **RB-08 (Complexity Means Danger)**, **RB-16 (Loan Mechanics)**
- Field rule: show **2–3 LTV/tenure variants live — never just the maximum** (Decision Psychology v2.1, RB-12 field response).

## TECHNIQUE 4 — TRADE CPF WITH CASH

### What It Is

Most clients default to using CPF for the down payment and ongoing mortgage servicing. This technique demonstrates that keeping CPF compounding at guaranteed risk-free rates — and using cash for the property instead — may produce a stronger long-term financial position.

### The Compounding Argument

CPF Ordinary Account earns:
- 2.5% per annum, guaranteed
- Risk-free, government-backed
- Compounds annually

If a client has $200,000 in CPF OA and uses it for a down payment:
- They access $200,000 today but lose the compounding base
- CPF withdrawn for property must be returned with accrued interest upon sale

If the same client uses $200,000 cash for the down payment and leaves CPF untouched:
- CPF continues compounding at 2.5%
- Over 20 years: $200,000 → approximately $328,000 (compounded)
- The property gain must exceed the opportunity cost of this compounding for the CPF draw-down to be worth it

### Advisory Script

*"Most people reach for CPF first — it's there, it's available, and it's meant for housing. That's true.*

*But here is what many families miss. Every dollar you withdraw from CPF today is a dollar that stops compounding at 2.5% guaranteed. Over 20 years, $200,000 in CPF becomes approximately $328,000 without you doing anything.*

*If you use cash instead — and you have the cash available — CPF keeps working in the background. Your retirement base stays intact. The property generates its own return. And you haven't eroded your long-term safety net.*

*This isn't about never using CPF for property. It's about making the ratio a deliberate calculation — not a default."*

### Application Rules

- This technique is most relevant when the client has significant CPF OA balance AND available cash
- Do not apply if the client does not have sufficient cash for the down payment without CPF — in that case, the blended approach is correct
- The ratio decision depends on: age, retirement horizon, projected property gain, and total available capital

### When to Use

- Primary use: When client has both CPF and cash available and hasn't considered the opportunity cost
- Profile: Analytical, opportunity-seeking dispositions
- Belief Block: **RB-17 (CPF / Beat the 2.5%)** primary; related **RB-16 (Loan Mechanics / Why Borrow When I Have Cash)**, **RB-18 (Opportunity Cost / Competing Asset Class)**
- Field tie-in: pairs with the **"combined ~5% growth hurdle"** framework (2.5% CPF opportunity cost + 2.5% loan interest) — Decision Psychology v2.1, RB-17.

## TECHNIQUE 5 — SAVE AS YOU EARN (SAYE)

### What It Is

SAYE is used when the client is not yet ready for the next move — but wants to be. It converts a vague "someday" into a specific date with a defined financial target.

It is also applicable as a Progressive Payment strategy on new launches — where the payment schedule during the construction period creates a forced savings discipline that aligns with readiness.

### Two Applications

**Application A — Readiness Building (not yet at entry)**
Used when:
- The client understands the move and wants it, but needs 12–36 months to build the required capital
- Readiness: **R4 Framework-Ready in intent, but capital-not-ready** — i.e. Tier 2 (nurture) in the diagnosis tiering (per `02_READINESS_STAGE_MODEL_v2.0.md`)

Structure:
1. Calculate the target entry amount (down payment + BSD + stamp duty + cash buffer)
2. Calculate the monthly savings required to reach it within the target window
3. Set up a dedicated property fund account
4. Book a 12-month review to reassess progress and market conditions

| Target Entry | Timeline | Monthly Saving Required |
|---|---|---|
| $150,000 | 24 months | $6,250/month |
| $150,000 | 36 months | $4,167/month |
| $200,000 | 36 months | $5,556/month |

*Figures are illustrative. Actual target depends on specific purchase price and structure.*

**Application B — Progressive Payment Discipline on New Launches**
Used when:
- The client purchases a new launch under progressive payment schedule
- Early construction phases have low monthly commitment (typically 5–10% of purchase price over 12–24 months)
- The SAYE principle applies: the low-payment period is used to build the cash reserve for when full payments kick in post-TOP

### Advisory Script

*"You're telling me you want to make this move but you're not ready yet — and I respect that. What I want to do is make 'not yet' a specific date, not an open-ended plan.*

*Let's map what you need to get there: down payment, stamp duty, and a 6-month cash buffer. That is your target number. Now let's work backwards — how much do you need to set aside monthly to reach that number in 18 months?*

*That number is your SAYE commitment. It's not complicated. It's a monthly transfer to a dedicated account, every month, until the window opens. When it does, you will be ready — because you built the runway on purpose, not by chance."*

> **Scarcity removed (Decision 071(2) doctrine check):** the v1.0 script closed on *"you will be the one who moves while others are still wishing they had started earlier"* — a competitive/FOMO framing. Replaced with a non-comparative structural close, consistent with the no-scarcity rule (constitution §2/§10; RAM resolves fear with numbers and structure, never urgency). See REMAP LOG item 10.

### When to Use

- Primary use: Clients who have intent but not yet capital readiness (Tier 2 nurture)
- Profile: Collaborative, comfort-protective dispositions
- Belief Block: **RB-02 (Need More Certainty / readiness)** primary; related **RB-03 (Comfort Greater Than Growth)**
- Readiness: **R4 Framework-Ready in intent** — needs structure and a dated forcing event, not just more content. Pair with a cost-of-waiting number (Decision Psychology v2.1, RB-02).

## RAM Deployment Sequence in the Strategy Session

RAM is not presented as a block — it is introduced in response to the specific concern that surfaces.

| Client Concern | RAM Technique to Deploy | RB block(s) resolved |
|---|---|---|
| "Two loans feels like too much" | Safety Net first, then 3Ps to reframe the asset | RB-05, RB-12 |
| "Monthly payments will strain us" | Stretch Tenure, followed by Safety Net | RB-12, RB-16 |
| "What if something goes wrong" | Safety Net primary | RB-05, RB-12, RB-15 |
| "I have CPF, should I use it?" | Trade CPF with Cash | RB-17 (related RB-16) |
| "I'm not ready yet" | SAYE — convert vague readiness to a date | RB-02 (related RB-03) |
| "Property is just a loan obligation" | 3Ps — reframe the asset | RB-05 (related RB-16, RB-18) |
| "I'm afraid of overcommitting" | Safety Net + Stretch Tenure together | RB-05, RB-12 |

**Rule:** Never present all 5 techniques in one session. Use only what addresses the active concern. Overloading RAM is as bad as not using it.

## MAS Language Notes

When presenting RAM in client sessions, use this language:

| Avoid | Use Instead |
|---|---|
| "Risk mitigation" | "Financial assurance" or "safety structure" |
| "Worst-case scenario" | "What the plan looks like even if something changes" |
| "Leverage" (as a standalone term) | "Structured entry" or "financing the growth position" |
| "Stress test" | "How the numbers look even in a conservative scenario" |
| "ABSD" without context | Always explain the implication for their specific structure |

RAM builds confidence — not by minimising risk, but by making the risk visible, bounded, and manageable. The client who can see the downside — and sees it is survivable — is the client who can commit to the upside. Never with urgency; always with numbers and structure. Staff are **advisors and coaches, never "agents."**
