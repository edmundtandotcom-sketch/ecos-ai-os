# 02_BELIEF_SHIFT_QUESTION_SEQUENCES_v2.0

Status: ACTIVE SOURCE OF TRUTH  
Folder: 02_CLIENT_OS / 03_TRIGGER_QUESTION  
Purpose: Provide question sequences that move clients from surface objection to self-realisation and decision confidence.

## 1. Sequence Rule

Do not attack the objection.

Move in this order:

1. Agree with the concern.
2. Clarify the real fear.
3. Contrast the current assumption with the better question.
4. Make the cost of inaction visible.
5. Bridge to evidence, framework, or numbers.
6. Let the client say the insight.
7. Move only to the next safe action.

## 2. Universal Belief-Shift Sequence

Use this when the belief block is unclear.

- That concern makes sense. What part worries you most?
- Is the concern mainly timing, money, spouse alignment, trust, or uncertainty?
- What would need to be true for this to feel safe enough to review properly?
- If nothing changes, what happens to your position over the next few years?
- Are we trying to predict perfectly, or make the position structurally safer?
- What would you want to see before deciding the next step?

## 3. Property Went Up, Now What Sequence

Use for A1 First Climb recognition.

- Your property has gone up. What do you believe that gain should unlock?
- Is the gain giving you more options, or just making you feel safer on paper?
- If the property keeps growing but the plan does not change, what is the risk?
- Should the next decision be based on price today, or future flexibility tomorrow?
- Would it be useful to map hold, sell, decouple, upgrade, and buy-again paths side by side?

## 4. Comfort Greater Than Growth Sequence

Use when the client says current home is fine, no urgency, or why disturb life.

- I understand why staying feels safe. What does your current home protect today?
- What future options do you want this property to create later?
- If nothing changes for 5–10 years, would the position still serve the family well?
- Is the real goal to avoid disturbance, or to protect future flexibility without over-stretching?
- What kind of move would feel safe enough to even review?

## 5. Waiting For Better Timing Sequence

Use when client says market is high, waiting for correction, waiting for rates, or still studying.

- What exactly are you waiting to see?
- If that happens, how will you know it is finally time?
- Are you waiting for certainty, or waiting for the decision to feel easier?
- What could become harder if the window only looks obvious after it has passed?
- Should we evaluate timing by prediction, or by whether your position improves under stress-tested scenarios?

## 6. Sell High Buy Higher Sequence

Use when client says everything is expensive or does not make sense to switch.

- That concern is valid. Nobody wants to sell well and overpay next.
- Are you comparing purchase price, or comparing future position?
- What does your current asset still offer from here?
- What would the next structure need to give you that your current position cannot?
- If the next move improves ownership flexibility, exit pathway, and future options, would that change the way we judge the price?

## 7. Fully Paid Equals Safe Sequence

Use when client says house fully paid, no need loan, no stress.

- Being debt-free does feel safe. What does that safety mean to you?
- Is the objective only to avoid loans, or to improve family flexibility safely?
- If the asset stays fully paid but idle, what does it create for the next stage?
- What level of buffer would make a move worth reviewing without threatening lifestyle?
- Should we first stress-test whether any move is safe before discussing what to buy?

## 8. New Launch / Resale Confusion Sequence

Use when client says new launches are overpriced or resale is cheaper.

- Many launches are not suitable just because they are new. That is true.
- Are we comparing price alone, or exit pathway, buyer pool, supply, and future demand?
- What is the risk of buying cheaper but exiting weaker?
- What is the risk of buying newer but at the wrong quantum or wrong stack?
- Should we compare both options using exit logic rather than emotional preference?

## 9. Decoupling Sequence

Use when client asks about decoupling or freeing a name.

- What do you expect decoupling to solve?
- After the name is freed, what is the actual next decision?
- Are you trying to reduce ABSD exposure, create future entry, improve ownership spread, or all three?
- What could go wrong if the mechanism is done before the roadmap is clear?
- Would it be safer to review decoupling as part of the next 2–3 moves, not as a standalone tactic?

## 10. Qualified HDB / EC Upgrade-Window Sequence

Use only for qualified upgrade-window households.

- What decision window are you really in: MOP, post-MOP, TOP, SSD, or planned sale timing?
- Are you reviewing because of space, asset growth, names, or long-term wealth position?
- What numbers have you already checked?
- What would make the upgrade unsafe?
- If the numbers say wait, would you rather wait with a plan or move because of pressure?
- Should we map what is possible, what is risky, and what should be avoided first?

## 11. DINK / Golden Window Sequence

Use for high-income couples with flexibility before future constraints.

- What does this season of life allow you to do that may be harder later?
- Are you using flexibility intentionally, or assuming it will always be there?
- What future constraint could change your options: children, career shift, lifestyle, family support, or loan tenure?
- What kind of property decision would protect lifestyle freedom rather than trap it?
- Should we review whether this is a window to act, prepare, or deliberately wait?

## 12. Family Legacy Sequence

Use when parents, adult children, family capital, or names matter.

> **Note (Decision 063, 2026-07-11):** the Family Legacy campaign door and the former A3/A4 ascent levels are retired. This sequence stays usable for family situations that surface **inside a standard A1/A2 engagement** — it is a conversation tool, not a level or door.

- What are you trying to create for the family, beyond buying one property?
- What role should the child play in the decision?
- What does the parent need to protect?
- What does the child need to understand?
- Is the family aligned on support, responsibility, and risk?
- Should we run a shared review so everyone sees the same numbers and trade-offs?

## 13. Spouse / Family Not Aligned Sequence

Use when champion is present but sceptic is absent or not convinced.

- What part do you think your spouse or family will disagree with?
- Have they seen the same numbers and trade-offs you have seen?
- Is their concern about money, risk, lifestyle, trust, or timing?
- Would making a decision without them create friction later?
- Should the next step be a shared clarity session rather than you trying to convince them alone?

## 14. Trust Deficit Sequence

Use when client worries agent is pushing or every advisor has an agenda.

- I understand why you would feel that way. What would make this feel advisory rather than sales-driven?
- Would it help if we first identify what not to buy?
- Are you comfortable if the review concludes that no move is suitable now?
- What information would you need to see before trusting the recommendation?
- Should the first outcome simply be a diagnosis and avoid list before any project discussion?

## 15. Closing The Loop

After the client answers, summarise in this structure:

1. What I hear is...
2. The real issue may not be [surface objection], but [belief / decision gap].
3. The next safe step is not to buy. It is to review [missing diagnosis area].
4. If the review says move, we map it. If it says wait, we wait with a plan.

Do not close beyond the client’s confidence level.
