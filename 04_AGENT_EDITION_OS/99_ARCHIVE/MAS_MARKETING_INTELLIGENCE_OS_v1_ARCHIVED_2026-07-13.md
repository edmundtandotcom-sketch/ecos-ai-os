## 1. THE MASTER BRIEF

### Who we are selling to
Two buyer sub-types with distinct psychologies. Know which one you're writing for before you write a single word.

**Sub-Type A — 3-Year Grinding Agent**
- GCI: S$80K–S$150K
- Stuck in transaction mode. Works hard. No system beneath the effort.
- Core pain: month restarts from zero. Every. Single. Month.
- Deepest fear: this is as good as it gets.

- GCI: S$150K–S$300K
- Earning. But the ceiling doesn't move. Referrals keep coming but can't scale.

- Identity shift needed: from "experienced agent" → "command-level advisor"

## 2. THE YES-LADDER FRAMEWORK (Primary Conversion Architecture)

**Source doctrine:** Russell Brunson (Dotcom Secrets), Alex Hormozi ($100M Leads), Dan Kennedy (No B.S. Direct Marketing), Tom Ferry (Yes Momentum), Peng Joon (Agreement Stacking)

The yes-ladder is the non-negotiable structural spine of every MAS funnel. Every piece of copy — from an ad to a landing page to a GHL follow-up — must stack micro-agreements before asking for a decision.

### The 7-Rung Agreement Stack

**Rung 1 — IDENTITY MIRROR**  
Make them feel seen. Name their current situation so accurately they think you've been watching them.  
> "If you're earning S$150K and it feels like you should be earning more — you're right. And it's not the market."

**Rung 2 — PROBLEM OWNERSHIP**  
Transfer the problem from "market conditions" → "structure." This is the pivot that makes the solution possible.  
> "Most agents blame their income ceiling on leads, market cycles, or agencies. The agents who break through stop blaming the market and start fixing their system."

**Rung 3 — FUTURE PACING (NEGATIVE)**  
Show them what staying where they are costs. Be specific. Use time and money.  
> "Every month without a system is another month where S$30K–60K in pipeline walks past you and calls the agent who was positioned to receive it."

**Rung 4 — FUTURE PACING (POSITIVE)**  
Show them the new identity — not the tactic, the person they become.  
> "Command-level advisors don't negotiate fees. They don't chase leads. They don't start every month from zero. Not because they're smarter — because their system does the work their effort used to do."

**Rung 5 — SOCIAL PROOF (SPECIFIC)**  
Credibility through specificity. Never "many agents improved." Always: "S. Raj, ERA, 8 years, S$240K → S$520K in 11 months."

**Rung 6 — OFFER AS OBVIOUS CONCLUSION**  
Present the offer not as something they should buy but as the natural next step for someone who already agrees.  
> "The bootcamp exists because the fastest way to close this gap isn't a course. It's one day, in person, with everything built before you leave."

**Rung 7 — URGENCY AS TRUTH**  
Scarcity must be real and specific. Never "limited time." Always: "40 agents per cohort. Edmund works with everyone in the room. When it's closed, the next date isn't guaranteed."

## 3. THE 4 MASTER AD ANGLES

Every MAS ad campaign must be anchored to one of these four angles. Test in this order — system angle consistently outperforms identity in cold traffic; identity angle wins in warm retargeting.

### Angle 1 — SYSTEM (Highest cold traffic performer)
**Core premise:** You don't have a lead problem. You have a system problem.  
**Trigger:** Agents who work hard but results don't compound.  
**Hook patterns:**
- "You don't have a lead problem. You have a [specific problem] problem."
- "The reason your income keeps hitting S$[X] every year isn't what you think."
- "73% of SG property agents never break S$200K GCI. Not because of the market."

**Example ad copy (short form):**
> "If you're working 6 days a week in property and still starting every month from zero — that's not a market problem. That's a structure problem. And structure is fixable in a day. 👉 Take the free diagnostic."

### Angle 2 — PLATEAU (Best for retargeting)
**Core premise:** Why your income stopped growing at S$X.  
**Trigger:** Agents earning "okay" but stuck.  
**Hook patterns:**
- "The plateau most SG property agents never escape — and the one thing that breaks it."
- "You've been in property [X] years. Your income should be compounding. Here's why it isn't."
- "S$[X] isn't a ceiling. It's just where agents stop building."

**Example ad copy:**
> "After 5 years in property, your income should be compounding — not flat. The agents who break S$300K, S$500K, S$1M aren't luckier or smarter. They built something underneath the effort. You haven't. Yet."

### Angle 3 — MARKET SHIFT
**Core premise:** The market changed. Your approach hasn't.  
**Trigger:** Agents feeling competitive pressure from specialists.  
**Hook patterns:**
- "The clients in your sector are looking for specialists. What are they calling you?"
- "The reason you're losing deals to agents with less experience than you."
- "Your market shifted in 2024. Most agents still don't know it."

### Angle 4 — IDENTITY / CATEGORY (Best for retargeting + event promotion)
**Core premise:** Stop being a salesperson. Become a trusted advisor.  
**Trigger:** Agents who feel stuck between transaction agent and professional.  
**Hook patterns:**
- "You know you're better than your commission rate suggests."
- "Most agents get paid like salespeople because that's how they position themselves."
- "There's a version of your property career where clients seek you out. Here's what it takes."

## 4. COPY PRINCIPLES — THE 12 LAWS

Apply these to every piece of copy. Violation of any of these is grounds to rewrite.

**Law 1 — Specificity over vagueness**  
Always: "S$240K → S$520K in 11 months." Never: "Income significantly improved."

**Law 2 — Problem before solution**  
Never lead with what you offer. Lead with what they're feeling. Minimum 40% of any piece must be problem/pain before the offer appears.

**Law 3 — Their language, not yours**  
Use the exact phrases real agents use: "starting from zero," "chasing leads," "fee negotiations," "stuck at the same level." Never use coaching jargon.

**Law 4 — One idea per piece**  
Every ad, every email, every section of a landing page has one job. One idea. One call to action. Multiple ideas = zero conversions.

**Law 5 — Make the cost of inaction visible**  
Every piece of copy must answer: "What does it cost them to not do this?" This is Dan Kennedy's core principle. Make the math visible.

**Law 6 — Social proof must be named and numbered**  
"Many agents" = 0% credibility. "S. Raj, ERA, 8 years, S$240K → S$520K" = 87% credibility increase. Names. Numbers. Time frames.

**Law 7 — Headlines must stop the scroll**  
Headline = 80% of the work. Test a minimum of 3 headline variants per campaign. The Hormozi test: would someone screenshot this and send to a friend?

**Law 8 — The CTA must be a logical conclusion, not a command**  
Bad: "Buy now." Good: "The fastest path out of the plateau is one day. Reserve your spot."

**Law 9 — Never make a claim without the mechanism**  
Claim: "Agents who do this earn more." Mechanism: "Because when clients can describe your process to friends in specific terms, your referral rate compounds without additional marketing spend."

**Law 10 — Objections are handled before they're raised**  
Identify the top 3 objections for each buyer type. Address them in the copy before they appear in the mind. Never wait for the FAQ.

**Law 11 — The guarantee must neutralise the risk completely**  
Weak: "30-day refund policy." Strong: "Show up for the full day. If by 4pm you haven't built something you can deploy on Monday, we refund you on the spot. We've never processed one."

**Law 12 — End on momentum, not a summary**  
The last line of every piece must carry kinetic energy forward. Never end with a recap. End with a consequence, a decision point, or an identity statement.

## 5. PSYCHOLOGICAL TRIGGER LIBRARY

Deploy these triggers consciously. Never accidentally. Stack maximum 3 per piece — more than 3 creates resistance, not conversion.

### SCARCITY (Real only — never manufactured)
- Cohort cap: 40 agents per bootcamp. This is real. Use it.
- Seat count: live and visible. Update the counter honestly.
- Date scarcity: "Next cohort date not yet confirmed."
- Never: "Offer ends Sunday." (Unless it literally does.)

### SOCIAL PROOF HIERARCHY (In order of persuasion power)
1. Named result with specific numbers and time frame
2. Named testimonial with role and agency
3. Aggregate claim with methodology cited (e.g., "CEA Industry Analysis")
4. Peer behaviour ("47 agents from your sector have pre-registered")
5. Authority positioning (16 years, 500+ agents coached)

### FEAR OF MISSING OUT vs FEAR OF STAYING STUCK
FOMO drives 30% of action. Fear of staying stuck drives 70%. Lead with "cost of inaction" before "benefit of action" in every major piece.

### IDENTITY SHIFT TRIGGER
The most powerful conversion lever. Not "this will help you earn more" but "this is how you become the agent who earns more." People buy futures, not features.

### AUTHORITY POSITIONING
- 16 years in Singapore property
- 500+ agents coached
- MAS Pattern Intelligence Library
- Proprietary named frameworks (3C System — internal only)
- Community of results (the proof community)

### COMMITMENT & CONSISTENCY (Cialdini)
The scorecard exists for this reason. Once someone self-diagnoses their income ceiling, they are psychologically primed to close it. Every micro-commitment (filling in the scorecard, pre-registering, watching a video) makes the next step more likely.

### PRICE ANCHORING
Always show the anchor before the real price.  
Format: ~~S$997 standard investment~~ → S$297 priority window  
Always pair price with value stack. Never show price without the full offer above it.

## 6. LANDING PAGE ARCHITECTURE (Standard MAS Formula)

Section sequence for all MAS landing pages. Do not deviate from this order.

```
1. URGENCY BAR (seat count / deadline / social proof number)
2. NAV (minimal — logo, live indicator, single CTA)
3. HERO (headline = transformation promise / subhead = mechanism / CTA above fold)
4. PROOF STRIP (3–5 stats. Real numbers only.)
5. PROBLEM (name the pain. Use their language. 40% of page.)
6. AGITATION (cost of inaction. Time. Money. Identity loss.)
7. MECHANISM (what makes this different — the "how" not the "what")
8. PROOF (case studies. Named. Numbered. Time-framed.)
9. WHO IT'S FOR / NOT FOR (filters in the right buyers, filters out the wrong ones)
10. OFFER STACK (what they get. Present as logical package, not list of features.)
11. PRICE + ANCHOR (price is the final reveal. Not before the value is established.)
12. GUARANTEE (neutralise all remaining risk)
13. INCOME GAP VISUAL (the cost of not buying vs cost of buying)
14. FAQ (handle the top 5 objections in their exact language)
15. FINAL CTA (repeat the transformation promise + urgency + price)
16. STICKY BAR (persistent CTA visible on scroll)
```

### Above-fold rules
- Headline must state the transformation, not the product name
- Subheadline must state the mechanism (how it delivers the transformation)
- CTA must be visible without scrolling on mobile
- No more than 3 elements above the fold on mobile
- Load time target: under 2 seconds

### Colour system (MAS dark theme — production)
- Background: #07070F / #0E0E1A / #141424
- Gold: #C9952A / #E8B84B (gradient)
- Text: #F0F0F5 / #9090B0 (muted)
- Green (success/positive): #10B981
- Red (problem/loss): #EF4444
- Amber (warning/plateau): #F59E0B

## 7. EMAIL SEQUENCE ARCHITECTURE

### Pre-event nurture sequence (post-scorecard lead)
**Email 1 — Immediate (0 min):** Score delivery + diagnosis  
Subject: "Your Agent Command Score: [X]% — here's what it means"  
Goal: Deliver score. Confirm identity. Plant the first seed of the bootcamp.

**Email 2 — Day 1:** The cost of the gap  
Subject: "The S$[X] gap in your diagnostic"  
Goal: Make the income ceiling tangible in their specific number.

**Email 3 — Day 3:** Proof story (most relevant to their sector)  
Subject: "How [Name] went from S$[X] to S$[Y] in [time frame]"  
Goal: Show the mechanism working for someone like them.

**Email 4 — Day 5:** The mechanism (what the bootcamp actually does)  
Subject: "What happens in the room"  
Goal: Explain the build process. Convert curiosity into intent.

**Email 5 — Day 7:** Scarcity + decision  
Subject: "[X] seats remaining"  
Goal: Create urgency. Force the decision. Close or disqualify.

**Email 6 — Day 10 (re-engagement):** The cost of not deciding  
Subject: "The year you didn't build the system"  
Goal: Negative future pacing. What does next December look like if nothing changes?

### Subject line formula
Best-performing patterns for MAS audience:
- "[Specific number] → [Specific number]: what changed"
- "The reason your GCI isn't compounding"
- "I reviewed your diagnostic. Here's what I see."
- "[Name], your score says [specific thing]"
- "What the top 1% of SG agents have that you don't (yet)"

## 8. VIDEO SCRIPT FRAMEWORK (Hook + Bridge + CTA)

**The MAS 3-Part Video Formula:**

**Part 1 — HOOK (first 3–5 seconds)**
Must stop the scroll. Must name the viewer's exact situation.  
Format: "[Pain statement] — if that's you, this is for you."  
> "If you've been in property for 3+ years and your income is still starting from zero every month — this is the most important thing you'll watch today."

**Part 2 — BRIDGE (the middle)**
Sub-sections:
- Credibility establisher (who is Edmund, why listen — max 20 seconds)
- Problem deepener (make the pain more real, more specific)
- Mechanism teaser (hint at the solution without revealing the full system)
- Proof moment (one case study, named and numbered)
- Offer bridge (natural transition to the CTA)

**Part 3 — CTA (last 10–15 seconds)**
Single action. Urgent but not desperate.  
> "If you're ready to stop running a business that only works when you do — link is below. The diagnostic takes 3 minutes. The bootcamp takes one day. The income shift takes the first conversation you have with a client after."

### Video length by platform
- Facebook/Instagram Reel: 60–90 seconds (hook must land in 3 seconds)
- YouTube pre-roll: 15–30 seconds (unskippable hook, CTA before 30s mark)
- YouTube long-form: 8–15 minutes (full problem/proof/offer arc)
- WhatsApp/Telegram broadcast: 60–90 seconds (conversational tone)
- LinkedIn: 90 seconds–3 minutes (professional framing, less urgency)

## 9. DESIGN INTELLIGENCE LIBRARY

### Visual hierarchy rules
1. Headline → Subhead → Proof → Offer. Never deviate.
2. Gold (#C9952A) is reserved for: headlines, CTAs, key numbers, callouts. Nothing else.
3. White space is not wasted space. Density = resistance.
4. Every section must have one primary visual anchor (stat, chart, image, pullquote).
5. Numbers are always the strongest visual. Make them big.

### Typography principles
- Headlines: 800–900 weight. Letter-spacing -0.02em to -0.04em. Never uppercase for main headline.
- Body copy: 400–500 weight. Line-height 1.7–1.85. Never below 11px on mobile.
- Stats/numbers: 900 weight. Oversized. Make them the first thing the eye sees.
- Callouts: 700 weight. Colour-differentiated. Never more than 2 lines.

### Image and visual principles
- Real photos outperform stock. Always.
- Face-forward photography converts 23% higher than product/object photography.
- Dark backgrounds + gold accents = premium positioning signal for SG real estate professionals.
- Charts and data visuals increase perceived authority by ~40% if correctly sourced.
- Animated elements (progress bars, counters) increase engagement time by 60–80% — use deliberately.

### Mobile-first rules
- CTA button minimum 44px touch target
- Font minimum 11px body / 16px headline on mobile
- No horizontal scroll
- Sticky CTA visible at all times on mobile (bottom bar)
- Forms: maximum 4 fields. Every additional field cuts conversion by ~10%.

## 10. PLATFORM-SPECIFIC SPECIFICATIONS

**IMPORTANT:** Verify platform specs before finalising any platform-specific output. These are current as of 2026-07-02 but platform rules change. Label all platform-specific copy DRAFT pending final verification before publishing.

### Meta (Facebook + Instagram)
- Ad copy: primary text 125 chars before "See more" (front-load the hook)
- Headlines: 40 chars max (or truncation)
- Images: 1080×1080 (square), 1080×1920 (Story/Reel), 1200×628 (link ad)
- Text overlay: <20% of image (Facebook still penalises heavy text)
- Recommended: Video > Single image > Carousel for cold traffic
- Audience: Custom audience from scorecard completers for retargeting

### Google Ads
- Headlines: 30 chars × 3 (responsive search)
- Descriptions: 90 chars × 2
- Landing page must match the ad intent exactly (quality score impact)

### YouTube
- Thumbnail: text + face, high contrast, 1280×720
- Pre-roll: hook must land before 5-second skip point
- Long-form: first 30 seconds must establish credibility + promise

### WhatsApp / GHL SMS
- Open rate: ~95% (use sparingly — highest-value communications only)
- Maximum 160 chars for SMS, 600 chars for WhatsApp
- Always personalise: [First Name]
- One link per message

### Email
- Subject line: 40–50 chars optimum (avoid spam triggers: FREE, !!!, ALL CAPS)
- Preview text: 80–100 chars (supports the subject line — don't repeat it)
- Single CTA per email
- Mobile preview mandatory before send
