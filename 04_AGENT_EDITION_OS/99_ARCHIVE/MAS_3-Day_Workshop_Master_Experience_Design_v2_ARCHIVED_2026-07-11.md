# MAS 3-Day Implementation Workshop — Master Experience Design v2
## Version: v2 | Date: 2026-06-12 | Supersedes: v1 (v1 retained in vault, untouched)
## What changed: the millionaire through-line, the MAS Snapshot install model, branded deliverables, Cindior's block redesigned, simplified day structure. Built on the Peng Joon and MREA reference docs (separate files).

---

# PART 1 — THE HONEST DIAGNOSIS: WHY v1 WAS 5/10

v1 was operationally complete and emotionally flat. Five specific gaps:

1. **No millionaire through-line.** The programme is called the Millionaire Advisory System, yet nothing in the 3 days made the participant's own million visible, personal, and dated. v1 confronted them with their broken business ($82/hour) but never showed them THEIR mathematical route to $1M. Confrontation without a dated destination = sober, not excited.
2. **Day 3 was a tech class.** Building pipelines from blank screens is slow, failure-prone, and emotionally dead. (Peng Joon solved this years ago: pre-built snapshots, install-and-personalise. The event time goes to activation and wins, not configuration.)
3. **Deliverables had no names and no dollar values.** "A positioning statement" is homework. "Your One-Liner — the sentence that books advisory conversations" is an asset. Unnamed deliverables can't be desired in advance, stacked in an offer, or talked about by alumni.
4. **No score, no stages, no game.** The participant never knew where they stood or what the next stage was worth. MREA's staging and Peng Joon's money staircase both exist because located participants stay emotionally invested.
5. **The event never paid for itself in the room.** One send-moment was good; it wasn't engineered to produce BOOKED REVENUE CONVERSATIONS before Day 3 ended.

v2 fixes all five. Everything from v1 that worked — the facilitation protocols (demo, hot seat, interrupt-coaching, build-block), room/staffing design, energy rules, qualification grid, proof capture — carries over unchanged. This doc covers what's NEW or CHANGED.

# PART 2 — THE NEW SPINE: THE MILLIONAIRE MAP

One physical artifact runs the entire 3 days: **The Millionaire Map** — an A2 canvas at every seat, mostly empty on Day 1 morning, completely filled by Day 3 evening. Every single block in the 3 days fills in one zone of the Map. Participants always know where they are, what's filled, what's next.

The Map has four zones, which are also the four stages of the workshop — the MAS adaptation of Keller's staging and Peng Joon's money staircase:

| Stage | Zone on the Map | The Question It Answers | When Built |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Stage 1 — SEE THE MILLION** | The Math | "What does MY million actually require — in deals, conversations, and leads, by what date?" | Day 1 morning |
| **Stage 2 — POSITION FOR THE MILLION** | The Who + The One-Liner | "Who pays it, and why me?" | Day 1 afternoon |
| **Stage 3 — THE MILLION-DOLLAR CONVERSATION** | The Method | "What do I say that a $200K agent can't?" | Day 2 |
| **Stage 4 — THE MACHINE** | The System | "What runs without my memory, my mood, or my hustle?" | Day 3 |

**The opening move of the workshop (Day 1, 9:20) — The Dated Million.** Before anything else is taught, every participant computes one number from their pre-work: *at your current average commission, current close rate, and current annual deal count — the calendar year in which your cumulative GCI crosses $1M.* For most agents the answer is somewhere between 2034 and never. They write the year, large, at the top of their Map. At Day 3's close they compute it again with their new architecture numbers. The distance between the two dates is the product. **This number — "your million's date" — is the excitement engine v1 lacked, and it is pure math, zero hype.**

**The Day 3 closing move — The Re-Date.** Same calculation, new inputs (higher average deal via ICP tier, higher close rate via the Method, plus retention/referral yield via the Machine). The million moves from "2037" to "2029." Conservative assumptions only — coaches enforce this; an honest 5-year acceleration lands harder than a fantasy 2-year one. The wall photo of 30 Maps showing re-dated millions is the single best marketing asset the programme will ever produce.

# PART 3 — THE BRANDED DELIVERABLE STACK (with the three Signatures)

Every deliverable now has a name, a one-line promise, and a money meaning. The three **SIGNATURE** assets are the ones the marketing leads with and alumni talk about.

|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | **The Truth Audit** | The Business Audit reveal (real hourly rate, lead-source autopsy) | "What every hour you worked last year actually paid you" |
| 3 | **The One-Liner** | Advisory Positioning Statement, compressed to a spoken sentence + paragraph version | "The sentence that books advisory conversations instead of viewings" |
| 4 | **The Client X-Ray** ⭐ SIGNATURE | The 12-Level Discovery Framework (full question set, branded) | "Twelve levels deep in one conversation — clients feel diagnosed, not prospected" |

# PART 4 — THE GAME LAYER: THE BUILDER'S SCOREBOARD

Simple, visible, not childish. A scoreboard wall tracks every participant across the 3 days — one point per completed build, posted by the producer at each break. Completions only, no quality scores (quality is the coaches' job; the scoreboard's job is momentum).

- 10 scored builds across 3 days (audit re-read, dated million, sort, ICP, One-Liner, X-Ray drill, Five Doors, Snapshot live, sends, First 90).
- **Full Build status** (10/10) is announced by name at the Day 3 close — this cohort's Implementors. Mirrors and seeds the Inner Circle's annual Implementor Award.
- The scoreboard gives the producer a real-time read on who's stuck (coach dispatch) and gives Edmund qualification data for the Inner Circle shortlist without any extra process.

## DAY 2 — THE MILLION-DOLLAR CONVERSATION
*Day promise: "You will run the full advisory conversation yourself today — and you'll hear the difference in your own voice."*

Structure unchanged from v1 (it was the strongest day) with three ◆ adjustments:

1. ◆ Every framework taught is connected back to the Map's math on transition: "Levels 1–4 of the X-Ray raise close rate. Close rate from 20% to 35% moves your million 3 years closer. That's what this hour is worth." The money-math runs through every block transition — this is the discipline v1 lacked.
2. ◆ The Five Doors block ends with each participant writing their scripts into a single laminated card format (the "door card") — pocket asset, not workbook page.
3. ◆ Day 2 close adds the Stage 3 Map fill-in: participants write their 3 named pipeline clients INTO the Map (the Method zone), connecting tomorrow's machine to real names with real commission values. Total value of the 30 rooms' named pipelines is announced: "There is roughly $X million of commission sitting in this room's named pipelines. Tomorrow we build the machine that stops it leaking."

# PART 6 — CINDIOR'S BLOCK: THE DECISION

Recommendation: **the 45–60 minute version, Day 3 at 9:30, titled "A Day Inside the System."** Not her story arc, not an Inner Circle anchor — an operational walkthrough of how she actually runs the machine, hour by hour.

Why this beats the alternatives Edmund listed:

| Option | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Her agent→advisor journey story (Day 1) | **No** | Day 1 belongs to the participant's own numbers and Edmund's authority arc. Her story there makes the workshop about HER transformation; the design needs it to be about THEIRS. Also redundant: they know her results — that's why they came. |
| Presence/anchor for Inner Circle ("work with both of us") | **No, not explicitly** | At $35K the Inner Circle sells on restraint. Using her block as an IC anchor reads as setup. The walkthrough does this job IMPLICITLY and better: watching the system run IS the IC advertisement. |
| "She did it, anyone can" presence cameo (20 min) | **Weak** | Inspiration without mechanism. The v1 design already rejected motivation-content everywhere else; her block shouldn't be the exception. |
| ◆ **45–60 min operational showcase (Day 3, 9:30)** | **YES** | Day 3 morning is exactly when participants are about to install the machine. Seeing the machine run a real working day — what the CRM surfaces at 8am, which follow-ups fired overnight, what AI drafted, what she touched manually (and what she did NOT have to remember), how a discovery booking arrived while she slept — converts the Snapshot install from "tech setup" into "installing what I just saw." Maximum proof, zero hype, perfect placement. |

**Format (so it stays operational, not theatrical):** Edmund interviews her live while her actual screens are mirrored (sanitised data view). Skeleton: yesterday's 8am pipeline view → what the system did overnight → her 3 manual touches of the day → one live client thread end-to-end (first touch to strategy session, shown in the CRM timeline) → "what I stopped doing" list (the hours the machine bought back). Close with the one allowed authority beat, from her, unprompted: "I run the system. He builds and updates it. That difference is why this works." — which lands the Edmund-as-architect frame more credibly than any pitch could.

# PART 7 — THE CONTINUITY GAP (new decision required)

The Peng Joon deconstruction exposed a hole in the MAS ladder: between the $3K workshop and the $35K Inner Circle there is NOTHING — no recurring revenue, no software glue, no home for the 25 participants per cohort who don't enter the IC.

**Proposed (decision pending): THE MAS SYSTEM — ~S$197/month.** Contains: MAS Snapshot hosting + updates (sequences and prompts refreshed as Edmund's own system evolves), the Co-Pilot Library (growing monthly), one monthly group implementation clinic (tech team, not Edmund), and the alumni community. 

Why it matters: (a) retention — their CRM, sequences and AI prompts live inside MAS-maintained infrastructure (Peng Joon's switching-cost lesson, applied gently); (b) recurring revenue that scales with cohorts while the IC stays capped at 20; (c) it keeps non-IC alumni inside the ecosystem until they're ready to ascend; (d) it's the natural GHL affiliate/white-label vehicle. Guardrail: Edmund's personal time is NOT in this tier — the moment it is, the IC's scarcity collapses. Tech team capacity is the gating question.

# PART 8 — WHY THIS IS 10/10 NOW (the test)

A participant finishing Day 3 can say ALL of the following, truthfully:

1. "I know the date my million arrives — I calculated it twice, and I moved it five years." (Map — desire, dated)
2. "I have eight named assets, three of them I'd call signature weapons." (Stack — possession)
3. "I ran the full advisory conversation myself, out loud, and handled my worst objection." (Method — capability)
4. "The system is installed and it messaged clients before I left the building." (Snapshot — machinery, running)
5. "I booked a real advisory conversation from inside the room." (Booking Hour — revenue, already moving)
6. "I watched someone run this exact system as her normal Tuesday." (Cindior — proof it's livable)
7. "I'm MAS1, I went 10-for-10 on the scoreboard, and the group knows it." (Game + cohort — identity)
8. "I know exactly what my next 90 days look like, and what's beyond the workshop if I want Edmund staying in my business." (First 90 + IC — the path continues)

v1 delivered 2, 3, half of 4, and 8. v2 delivers all eight. Simplicity check: ONE artifact (the Map), FOUR stages, EIGHT named assets, ONE scoreboard. Everything else is delivery detail. Nothing here requires Edmund to teach more content than v1 — it requires the same content arranged around a date, a game, and a machine that's already built.
