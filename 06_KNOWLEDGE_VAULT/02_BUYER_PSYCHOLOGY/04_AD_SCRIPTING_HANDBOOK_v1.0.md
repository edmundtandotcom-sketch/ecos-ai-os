# **The Ad Scripting Handbook**

### **How to write ads that sell, using the same structure behind ads that have done millions**

## **Read this first**

The four ads in this handbook are Caleb's. They're here as examples, so you can see the structure working in real ads that performed.

**Don't copy his voice. Don't copy his stories.** Copy the structure. Then fill it with your business, your proof, your words.

Every winning ad, in any niche, runs on the same three parts:

## **HOOK → STORY → OFFER**

That's it. The words change. The structure doesn't. Learn the structure once and you can write an ad for anything you sell.

## **The building blocks (in plain English)**

An ad is made of small moves. Each move does one job. Here's the full set. You won't use all of them in every ad. Think of it as a menu.

**The hook (pick one)**

- **Call-Out** — name exactly who it's for and what they're doing right now. The wrong person scrolls. The right person stops.
- **Open Loop** — start a story or ask something they have to keep watching to answer.

**The story (the middle of the ad)**

- **The Mirror** — describe their problem. Paint the pain they're living with right now. Not feelings, the actual day: what their week looks like, what keeps going wrong. Make them feel seen.
- **The Lie** *(optional)* — say out loud the lie they tell themselves about the problem. This is the line that makes them feel understood.
- **The Hard Truth** — one blunt sentence they don't want to hear but can't argue with.
- **Reframe** — pre-frame their belief. Show the wrong way they think about the problem, then flip it to the right way. The gap between the two is your big idea.
- **Your Method** — name your way of doing it, and break it into simple steps. A named thing is easier to trust and remember.
- **Proof** — show it works. Pick ONE type:
  - *Your track record* — your results, your numbers.
  - *Someone else's win* — a client or someone unlikely who got a result.
  - *Your own story* — how you struggled, then figured it out.
  - *A warning* — what happened to someone who didn't have this. (Proves the cost of doing nothing.)

**The close**

- **The Offer** — promise the exact thing the hook called out. Price. Guarantee. Tell them to click.

**Two rules that run through all of it**

- **Real details.** Real names, real numbers, real specifics. "$45,000 in two calls" beats "great results" every time. Never make numbers up.
- **The method is the hero, not you.** Even your wins should prove the system works, so the reader believes it'll work for them too.

# **The one choice that matters**

The structure never changes. The only real decision is **how you prove it.** Four ways:

| **Proof type** | **What it proves** | **Use when** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Your track record** | It works, fast | Warm audience, you want speed |
| **Someone else's win** | It works for anyone, not just you | They think you're special and they're not |
| **Your own story** | It works again and again | They think you got lucky |
| **A warning** | The cost of doing nothing | They're at a decision point |

Pick one per ad. Don't try to use all four.

# **The checklist**

You do **not** need every building block in every ad. Here are the six that have to be there. The rest are bonus.

## **Must-haves (every ad needs these six)**

- [ ] **Hook** calls out who it's for, or opens a loop, in the first line
- [ ] **The Mirror** — you show them their real day, so they feel seen
- [ ] **One hard truth** — blunt, one sentence
- [ ] **Reframe** — the wrong way they think vs your way
- [ ] **Proof** — one type, with real numbers or names (never made up)
- [ ] **The Offer** — promises the opposite of the pain in your hook, with price and guarantee

## **Level it up (add these once the basics are solid)**

- [ ] A "lie they tell themselves" line inside the mirror
- [ ] Your method, named and broken into steps
- [ ] A memorable one-liner you can reuse across ads
- [ ] (For story ads) your failures before the win
- [ ] One short, quotable lesson at the end

If you've got the six must-haves, you've got an ad. The rest just make it stronger.
