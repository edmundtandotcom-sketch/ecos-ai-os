# 1. THE BUSINESS IN ONE PARAGRAPH

Peng Joon sells ONE framework — **Offer + Funnel + Traffic = a Sales Process** — endlessly rewrapped into different containers (5-day sprints, 3-day intensives, 1–2 day virtual workshops, challenges), at escalating price points, with a white-labeled GoHighLevel platform (SalesProcess.com) as both the delivery tool AND the recurring-revenue product. Every event teaches the framework, builds part of it live, and sells the next container plus the software. The genius is not the content — it is that the same asset is monetised five ways: ticket, system subscription, software margin/affiliate, high-tier event, and continuity.

# 3. THE SEVEN MECHANICS THAT MAKE IT WORK

**3.1 One framework, infinite wrappers.**
Offer + Funnel + Traffic never changes. "AI Multiplier," "AI Sales Engine," "Sales Process," "Breakthrough Blueprint" are the same curriculum with the year's hook applied. Content cost approaches zero after the first build; every new wrapper feels fresh because the HOOK is current (AI), not because the framework moved.

**3.2 The software IS the business model.**
SalesProcess.com is white-labeled GoHighLevel. Three revenue effects: (a) recurring margin/affiliate on every subscription, (b) retention — leaving him means losing your CRM, funnels, automations, (c) the tool deepens results, which feeds testimonials. The CRM is simultaneously the classroom, the product, and the cage (in the good sense — switching cost = loyalty).

**3.3 The trial-as-ticket gate.**
For the HighLevel workshops, the "ticket" is starting a free software trial with a card on file that auto-converts to $97–$297/month. He gets paid for filling someone else's funnel — and the attendee arrives already INSIDE the tool they're about to be taught. Implementation friction at the event = zero, because everyone has the same logged-in stack.

**3.4 Snapshots / share funnels — install, don't build.**
Participants don't build funnels from blank screens. Pre-built templates ("7-Figure Share Funnels," done-for-you systems "activated inside HighLevel") are loaded into their account in one click; the event time is spent CUSTOMISING, not constructing. This is why his "build it live" events work at scale with mixed-skill audiences — the floor is pre-installed.

**3.5 The "TO DONE" list.**
His language, verbatim, from the Intensive: not a to-do list — a TO DONE list. The promise of every container is that the thing is FINISHED inside the container ("Your Ads & Email Sequences Crafted, Implemented & Rolled Out... So That You Leave The Intensive Having It DONE"). This single promise separates him from every theory seminar and justifies the price.

**3.6 The staged-money narrative.**
The Gamechanger Intensive's 4 stages: (1) Get traction/first subscriber → (2) Generate First Sale → (3) Smart Scaling 5→6 figures → (4) "No Big Mistakes" 6→7 figures. Note what this is: a MONEY STAIRCASE. The participant always knows which dollar stage they're on, what the next stage pays, and which mistake at their stage is fatal. The curriculum is organised by the participant's bank account, not by topic. This is a large part of the emotional pull.

**3.7 Event-selling craft (stable across all containers).**
The value stack with per-item anchoring (totals ~$29,637 → "today only $997"); event-only offers ("we didn't even mention these at the event"); a real 30-day money-back guarantee with a work requirement; payment plans (4×$297); borrowed authority (Brunson, Myron Golden, Kiyosaki quotes); student results with names and exact figures ("Daniel closing RM71,973 via events with no ad spend"); FAQ section that pre-handles "I'm just starting" / "no niche" / "not technical" / "can't afford it."

# 5. THE SCALE LESSON (FOR MAS P2–P5 FUTURE)

Peng Joon's machine scales because: the framework never changes (zero re-development), delivery is containerised (a challenge can run monthly with a small team), software produces revenue while he sleeps, and each event manufactures the testimonials that fill the next one. MAS's equivalent end-state: MAS P1 runs quarterly as the standard container; the MAS Snapshot + System subscription compounds between cohorts; the Inner Circle stays capped at 20 as the scarce apex. The ladder scales; the apex never does — that asymmetry is what protects Edmund's positioning while the business grows.
