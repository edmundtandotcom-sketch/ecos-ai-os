## 1.2 · There are not enough car park lots

`TF p44`: **1,014 residential car park lots** (including 31 EV) plus 7
accessible = 1,021 total, for **1,268 units**.

**0.80 lots per unit. 254 units' worth of shortfall.** [derived: 1,014 ÷ 1,268]

Nobody buying a 1,367 sqft four-bedder with two cars has been told this. It is
on the developer's own project overview page. For a 5-hectare site next to an
MRT this is a deliberate planning choice, not an oversight — but it is a real
thing to know before you commit, and it is the kind of detail that makes a
viewer trust the rest of the video.

# PART 2 — THE LAYOUTS THEMSELVES

## 2.1 · The one structural idea: the dumbbell, and what it costs

Every 2-bedroom is a **dumbbell** — bedroom at each end, living and dining in
the middle, balcony off the living. The developer names it: `FP p02` callout 2,
"Highly-efficient dumbbell layout".

**What it buys you.** There is no corridor. In a conventional plan you walk
down a hallway to reach the bedrooms and that hallway is floor area you paid
for and cannot furnish. In a dumbbell the living room *is* the circulation.
That is why BPS1 accounts for 67.6 of its 68 sqm on `TF p29` — **0.4 sqm
unaccounted for.** [derived: sum of the printed room areas]

**What it costs you.** Three things, and they are real:

1. **Every bedroom door opens into the living room.** There is no buffer. Some-
   one watching TV at 11pm is audible in both bedrooms.
2. **No foyer.** On B1 and BP1 the entrance door opens straight into the dining
   / kitchen zone. You can see the whole apartment from the doorway, and there
   is nowhere to drop shoes and bags out of sight.
3. **Single aspect.** All habitable rooms sit on one façade. Good: every room
   gets the same view and the same light. Bad: **no cross-ventilation** — there
   is no opposite window to pull air through, so with the aircon off the unit
   relies on one face. And whatever that one face gets — morning sun, afternoon
   sun, the neighbouring block — every room in the unit gets.

**Point 3 is the bridge from the layout segment to the facing segment, and it
is the reason facing matters more here than in a dual-aspect plan.**

## 2.2 · The ladder — what each step up actually buys

Read off the plans themselves (`FP p01, p03, p05, p07, p08, p09` and the room
areas on `TF p29–p31`). This is the spine of the video.

| | 592 B1 | 678 BP1 | 732 BPS1 | 947 C1 | 1,055 CP1 | 1,152 CPS1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bedrooms | 2 | 2 | 2 + study | 3 | 3 | 3 + study |
| Baths | **1, shared** | 2, both en-suite | 2 | 2 | 2 + **WC** | 2 + **WC** |
| Kitchen | enclosable | **open** | enclosable | **enclosed** | enclosed | enclosed |
| Yard | — | — | — | — | **yes** | **yes** |
| Store | in kitchen | at entry | at entry | at entry | at kitchen | at kitchen |
| Household shelter | — | — | — | — | **utility / maid** | **utility / maid** |
| Dining seats drawn | 4 | 4 | 4 | **6** | 6 | 6 |
| Units | 100 | 416 | 120 | 120 | 170 | 60 |

Three step-changes matter more than the square footage:

**592 → 678: you gain a second bathroom, and lose the guest toilet.**
On B1 the single bath sits between the master and the living area and is
reachable from both — a jack-and-jill. On BP1 there are two baths but **both
are en-suite** (`FP p03` callout 5, "All bedrooms with ensuite bath"). Which
means a visitor has to go through a bedroom. **A 678 sqft two-bedder with two
bathrooms and no powder room is a worse host than a 592 with one shared bath.**
That is a genuine, checkable con and no one says it.

**678 → 947: the kitchen gets a door.**
`FP p03` callout 4 on BP1 says "**Open** kitchen"; `FP p07` callout 3 on C1
says "**Enclosed** kitchen … natural ventilation". BPS1 at 732 says
"**Enclosable**" (`TF p29` callout 4). So the three words are the developer's
own and they are different words. If you cook with a wok, the 678 will smell
of dinner in both bedrooms — because of the dumbbell, there is nowhere for the
smell to go.

**1,055 → 1,152: the study becomes a room.**
On BPS1 the study is *inside the master* — `TF p29` callout 5: "Integrated as
part of the Master Bedroom, the study offers flexibility". On CPS1 the study is
a **separate room on the façade with its own frontage**, and `FP p09` callout 4
calls it "Study room with full frontage with hackable wall for future expansion
of master bedroom". **One is a nook in your bedroom; the other is a room with a
window that can merge into the master.** Note the developer's own words: it is
designed to expand the master, not to become a fourth bedroom. Same word on the
price list, completely different asset.

## 2.3 · Reading the furniture the developer drew

The plans are drawn with furniture. That is the developer telling you what they
think fits, and it is a fair test.

- **B1 592 / BP1 678** — dining drawn as **4 seats**. Living drawn with a
  two-seat sofa and a coffee table. Master takes a queen with a wardrobe run
  along the inner wall; Bedroom 2 takes a queen with a smaller wardrobe. **Two
  adults and a small child. A family of four eats in shifts.**
- **C1 947 / CP1 1,055 / CPS1 1,152** — dining drawn as **6 seats**. That is
  the real jump, and it happens at the 3-bedroom, not at the 1,055.
- **CP1 1,055** — `TF p30` prints the living frontage as **~3.0 m**.
  **DP1 1,367** prints **~3.4 m**; **E1 1,808** prints **~6 m**. Frontage is
  what decides whether a sofa faces the view or the wall. Three metres takes a
  three-seater against the window and nothing else.
- **CP1 / CPS1** — `TF p30` callout 6 and `FP p09` callout 7: the **household
  shelter doubles as utility / storage / maid's room**. In the 1,152 the yard,
  the WC and the HS sit together at the back, which is a proper service zone.
  In the 678 there is no service zone at all — the washer/dryer is in the open
  kitchen, four steps from the dining table.

# PART 4 — PROS AND CONS, TYPE BY TYPE

Honest both ways. Everything here traces to Parts 2 and 3.

**592 · 2-Bedroom B1/B2 · 100 units · Classic Tier 1**
- **For:** the cheapest way in. Dumbbell, so no wasted corridor. Enclosable
  kitchen — better than the 678 on cooking. The single bath is reachable from
  the living side, so guests do not go through a bedroom.
- **Against:** one bathroom for two bedrooms. 4-seat dining. Vinyl bedroom
  floors, tiled living. Only 100 of them, so thin resale comparables *within*
  the project, which cuts both ways.

**678 · 2-Bedroom Premium BP1/BP3 · 416 units · Classic Tier 1**
- **For:** two bathrooms, both en-suite. The dumbbell at its best — 63 sqm with
  almost nothing wasted. The single biggest type, so the most liquid thing in
  the project: there is always a buyer at the right price.
- **Against:** **open kitchen** in a single-aspect unit — cooking smells have
  nowhere to go. **No powder room**, so visitors use a bedroom's bath. No foyer.
  Washer/dryer four steps from the dining table. And on exit, **416 identical
  units are your competition.** Vinyl bedrooms.

**732 · 2-Bed Premium + Study BPS1 · 120 units · Classic Tier 1**
- **For:** the study is carved out of the master, so you get a work corner
  without losing a bedroom. Kitchen is **enclosable** — a real upgrade on the
  678. 99.4% of the strata area is labelled rooms.
- **Against:** the study is *in your bedroom*. It is not a third room and it
  will not become one. Balcony is 7.1% — the highest share of the three
  dimensioned types.

**947 · 3-Bedroom C1 · 120 units · Classic Tier 1**
- **For:** the kitchen gets a door and natural ventilation. Dining goes to 6.
  Store at the entrance. Three bedrooms all on the façade with full frontage.
- **Against:** all three bedrooms on one face means the whole unit lives or
  dies on that one aspect. Two bathrooms for three bedrooms. No yard, no WC.
  Still vinyl bedrooms and a tiled living room.

**1,055 · 3-Bedroom Premium CP1 · 170 units · Classic Tier 1**
- **For:** separate **yard** and a **WC**, so the service zone finally exists
  and guests stop using a bedroom's bath. HS doubles as utility or a helper's
  room. Living/dining 32.1 sqm — bigger than some entire one-bedders. Balcony
  is the lowest share of the 2- and 3-bed range at 6.8%.
- **Against:** living frontage is **~3.0 m**, which is a three-seater against
  the window and not much else. 170 units is the third-largest type, so real
  in-project competition on exit.

**1,152 · 3-Bed Premium + Study CPS1 · 60 units · Classic Tier 1**
- **For:** the **study is a real room on the façade** — usable as a study, or
  knocked into the master via the hackable wall. Yard, WC, HS-as-maid's-room. Only 60 units in the whole project —
  the scarcest thing in the Classic Collection. And it is the only Thomson
  Reserve unit that is **exactly the same size as a Jadescape 3-bedroom
  (1,152 sqft)**, which makes it the cleanest comparison in the video.
- **Against:** you are still in Classic Tier 1 — vinyl bedrooms, tiled living,
  at what will be a 3-million-dollar price. That is the one place where the
  spec tier genuinely jars, and it should be said.
