# Thomson Reserve Webinar — 60-Minute Flow Overview (v1)
Written run-of-show mapped to the actual 35-slide deck Edmund has built.
Purpose: one document to see the whole shape at once — what happens, why
psychologically, and where to tighten. Not a new script — a lens to look
at the existing slides through, plus specific trim/reorder calls.

---

## THE ONE STRUCTURAL FIX THAT MATTERS MOST

**Move "THE R.E.I. METHOD™" reveal (current slide 34) to right after the
Two Questions reframe (current slide 12) — before the fair-price build,
not after it.**

Right now the method gets *named* only near the end, after the audience
has already sat through the fair-price build, the Lentor ladder, and the
multiplier comparison. That means all of that plays as "a lot of
analysis" and only retroactively gets labeled as "a system" in the last
few minutes. Flip the order and every single thing that follows —
fair-price, land-cost-predicts-price, exit, rentability — reads as "step
3 of the method, live" instead of a loose collection of charts. This one
reorder is what makes the whole 60 minutes feel like a system was
followed, rather than a system existing.

**Why this is the actual answer to "make it sharper without teaching
everything":** naming all 6 steps up front (Transformation, Land Cost,
Entry Price, Exit Strategy, Who To Sell To, Rentability) is pure Layer 1
— it costs nothing to give away and it's the thing that makes the room
feel like they're watching a method, not a monologue. Everything after
it can then be *shorter*, because the audience already has the map and
doesn't need each section re-justified as "why am I hearing about
this now."

## THE THROUGHLINE (say this explicitly, once, right after naming the method)

> "This is the exact same 6 steps I run for myself and for every client,
> on every launch — not something built for tonight. First I'll show you
> it already working, on a launch that's already played out. Then we run
> the same 6 steps on Thomson Reserve, live, with what we actually know
> today — including the one thing nobody can know yet."

That sentence is doing three jobs at once: it proves the method is real
(not invented for the pitch), it sets up the case-study-then-live-
application structure you asked for, and it pre-frames the honest price
gap so it lands as integrity later instead of a dodge.

## ACT-BY-ACT, MAPPED TO YOUR ACTUAL SLIDES

### ACT 0 — Open & Qualify · Slides 1–5 · ~6 min
Personal-stakes hook → "you are here because" → 4 objectives → poll →
"what tonight actually is." **Keep as-is — this sequence is already
tight and does real work**: the qualifier slide (2) makes almost every
viewer see themselves in the room within the first 90 seconds, and the
poll (4) is what lets the pitch personalize later. Don't touch this act.

### ACT 1 — Authority, Proven Not Claimed · Slides 6–10 · ~5 min
TR hero shot → 5-year benchmark chart → 10-year benchmark chart →
net-asset-value ladder graphic → property-journey graphic.

**Two things to tighten:**
- Slides 7 and 8 (5-year and 10-year benchmark) — only keep both if the
  spoken line explicitly contrasts them ("even over just 5 years the gap
  is real — stretch to 10 and it compounds"). If that line isn't there,
  cut to whichever one has the stronger number and move faster.
- Slides 9 and 10 (ladder graphic, journey graphic) — these are two
  different visual metaphors for the same idea. Pick one. Running both
  back to back dilutes rather than reinforces.

### ACT 2 — Why Now, The Big Reframe · Slides 11–12 · ~3 min
3 Forces → Two Questions. Keep exactly as-is — this is the pivot from
"why should I care" to "here's what I'm actually going to teach you,"
and it's already lean.

### ⭐ NEW POSITION — Name The Method · (move slide 34 here) · ~2 min
Introduce THE R.E.I. METHOD™, all 6 steps, fast — this is a naming
moment, not a teaching moment. Say the throughline line above. This
replaces the current slide 34 position entirely; don't show it twice.

### ACT 3 — Fast Facts + The Ballot Process, Previewed · Slides 13–14 · ~3 min
TR fast facts → New Launch Ballot Process infographic. **Reposition the
ballot infographic's role:** show it here as a fast preview ("here's the
end-to-end process — we'll come back to the middle of it later"), not a
full walkthrough. Its full explanation belongs later, paired with Step 4
(Exit Strategy) of the method, where "before/during/after the ballot"
actually lives. Showing it twice — once as a map, once in depth — is
good pacing; showing it once in full this early front-loads content the
audience isn't ready to use yet.

### ACT 4 — The Experience, Briefly · Slides 15–18 → cut to 1 slide · ~1 min
Four consecutive TR render/amenity shots break momentum right before
your best content. **Cut to one, maybe two.** One line over it: "this is
what's pulling everyone in — now let's find out if the numbers agree."
That's the entire job these slides need to do.

### ACT 5 — CASE STUDY: The Method, Already Proven · Slides 27–29, 35+ · ~7 min
This is the section that directly answers "demonstrate the process as a
case study." Position it as: *"Before I run this on a project with no
price yet, let me show you this same method on one that's already
played out."*

- **Lentor cluster / "1st Mover Advantage" ladder (27–28)** — this is
  your strongest proof for Step 2 (Land Cost): 8 real launches, land
  cost rising launch over launch, over several years. This is a
  genuinely great case study because it's *resolved* — you can show the
  prediction and the outcome in the same slide.
- **ProTrend charts (35+)** — you appear to have several near-identical
  versions of this chart. **Pick the single strongest one** (clearest
  entry-to-outcome story, biggest legible gain) and cut the rest — three
  similar bar charts in a row reads as padding, one sharp one reads as
  proof.
- **Amberwood / Lucerne Grand / Serra cards (29)** — use as a fast "and
  I've run this same method on these three live launches too" beat, not
  a re-analysis. One sentence each, max.

**Discipline check for this whole Act:** it's fine for this section to be
numbers-rich — you're not teaching the audience to replicate it, you're
proving the method works using a project that isn't Thomson Reserve.
That's illustration, not instruction — the line only gets crossed if you
start explaining *how* to build the Lentor ladder yourself rather than
just showing that it happened.

### ACT 6 — Apply Live To Thomson Reserve · Slides 19–26, 31 · ~15 min
Now the same method, on the project with no price yet.

- **"Let's build the actual number, live" (19)** → **Fair Price Built
  Live (20–23)** — you currently have what look like 3–4 near-duplicate
  versions of this slide. Collapse to a single progressive build (Land →
  Expenses → Profit → Benchmark accumulating on one visual sequence),
  not four separately-titled slides that each say "Fair Price — Built
  Live." Four identical headers in a row reads as a slide-count problem,
  not a system.
- **Lorong Puntong real news (24)** — strong, keep. This is live proof
  the land-cost-rising pattern named in the case-study act is happening
  in this exact pocket right now, not just historically elsewhere.
- **2.24x–2.38x multiplier comparison (25)** — keep as the benchmark
  check, but make sure the spoken line stays at "here's what this
  multiplier implies for Thomson Reserve" rather than "here's how you'd
  calculate this multiplier for any project" — same content, different
  frame, and it's the frame that keeps this Layer 3 instead of Layer 4.
- **The objection slide (26)** — keep exactly as built. This is your
  strongest trust-moment in the whole deck.
- **"Are there still undervalued developments?" (31)** — good tension-
  setter; use it to ask the open question ("where does Thomson Reserve
  sit on this chart once its number exists") rather than asserting an
  answer you don't have yet.

### ACT 7 — The Honest Reframe · Slide 32 · ~3 min
"It's not the project, it's how you decide" — 3-box contrast. Keep
exactly as-is; this is the pivot from teaching into selling, and it's
already sharp.

### ACT 8 — The Pitch · Slide 33 · ~10 min
"Map Your Next 3 Property Moves" CTA. Keep, but make sure the spoken
close explicitly calls back to the method: *"You just watched all 6
steps run on a real case, and 5 of them run on Thomson Reserve live. The
one step nobody can finish yet — Entry Price, because there's no price
list — is exactly the piece a real conversation closes. Not the whole
method. Just that one gap."* That's a sharper, more specific close than
a generic CTA, because it names precisely what's missing instead of
gesturing at "more help available."

## WHY THIS SEQUENCE, PSYCHOLOGICALLY

1. **Qualify first** (Act 0) — everyone sees themselves in the room
   before any teaching starts, so they stay for content that might not
   be about their exact situation.
2. **Prove the messenger** (Act 1) — the numbers only matter if the
   person showing them has skin in the game.
3. **Name the method before using it** (the ⭐ move) — this is the single
   biggest lever for "there's a real system here" versus "that was a lot
   of information."
4. **Prove the method on a resolved case before an unresolved one** (Act
   5) — the audience gets to see the method be *right* about something
   that already happened, which buys trust for Act 6, where the biggest
   number (entry price) is honestly still unknown.
5. **Apply it live, including the one honest gap** (Act 6) — this is
   where "I don't know yet" becomes a trust signal instead of a weakness,
   because the method's track record was already proven in Act 5.
6. **Reframe the villain, not the project** (Act 7) — converts "was
   Thomson Reserve good" into "do you have a process," which is the only
   frame that makes the pitch feel like an extension of the teaching
   instead of a tone shift.
7. **Close on the one specific gap, not a generic offer** (Act 8) — "5 of
   6 steps done, 1 step needs your numbers" is a sharper, more honest ask
   than "book a call to learn more."
