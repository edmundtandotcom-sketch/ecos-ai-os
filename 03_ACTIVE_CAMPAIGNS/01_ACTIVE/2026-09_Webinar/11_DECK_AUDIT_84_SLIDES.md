# Thomson Reserve Webinar — 84-Slide Deck Audit (v1)
Full slide-by-slide pass on `Thomson_Reserve_Webinar_Deck.pptx` as it
currently stands (84 slides, notes read via markitdown). For each slide:
what's there now, and the script direction — pre-frame / engagement /
CTA / interaction / open loop / excitement — needed to make it land live.

**Headline finding:** the raw material is strong — a real client case
study (67–69), several genuine open loops already planted, a permission-
based intro device. The gaps are mechanical, not creative: some open
loops don't have a confirmed payoff slide, several slides are bare
images with no script line at all, and one section (29–32) repeats
itself four times with only the notes changing.

---

## PRIORITY FIXES (read this before the slide-by-slide)

1. **Anonymize slide 67's case study.** Real budget ($1.9M), real ballot
   number (1247/380), a real unit-swap decision. Strip anything that
   could identify the client before this goes live — a false name and
   rounded numbers lose none of the story's power.
2. **Slides 29–32 are one slide repeated four times**, not a progressive
   build — same title, same four rows, same numbers, only the spoken
   note changes. In PowerPoint this reads as a mistake, not a reveal.
   Either build genuine progressive reveal (row 2 appears only on slide
   30, etc.) or collapse to one slide and let the *speech* carry the
   progression.
3. **Three open loops are planted with no visible payoff slide yet:**
   - Slide 32: "this is only one benchmark... I show you why shortly" —
     needs a slide that explicitly delivers the "why" (the multiplier/
     comparison content already exists around slide 34, but nothing
     currently calls back to slide 32's promise by name).
   - Slide 45 / 51: "at the end I'll give you my pricing guide" and "the
     walk-out story" — both get partially paid off (67–70 is the case
     study, 70 is the estimate) but neither slide explicitly says "here's
     what I promised you earlier," which is what actually closes an open
     loop psychologically. Add the callback line.
   - Slide 6: "MUST IMPROVE THE 3 POINTS MORE" — your own flagged TODO,
     addressed in the slide-by-slide below.
4. **Many image-only slides (8–12, 15–18, 41–42, 44, 46–49, 52–57, 59–60,
   71–74, 76–80) have zero notes** — meaning right now they'd be
   presented in silence or improvised cold. Each needs at minimum a
   one-line transition/caption so nothing goes unscripted.
5. **Two different "2 kinds of buyer" framings exist** (slide 39's
   90%-emotion/10%-logic, and the predictable-vs-hope-and-pray concept
   from earlier planning) — pick one, don't run both or it dilutes.

## ACT 0 — OPEN & QUALIFY (Slides 1–6)

**Slide 1 — Title / hook.** Notes already have good engagement cues
(excited, interact, type in chat, safe environment) — make sure these
are spoken as literal instructions, not just a vibe: *"Type 'yes' if you
can hear me — I want to see the chat moving before we start."* Pre-frame
line already strong ("this IS a live demonstration of the process").

**Slide 2 — "You are here because."** Full-screen, no spoken cue noted.
Add: *"Read through these — if two or three apply to you, you're in the
right room tonight."* Let it sit 10–15 seconds before moving on;
qualifier slides work by dwell time, not narration.

**Slide 3 — "Work With Us Privately" teaser.** No notes. This is
appearing very early (slide 3) as a bare CTA card with no spoken framing
— right now it likely reads as a premature pitch. Recommend: either move
this later (it's your actual close CTA, showing it this early undercuts
the "no pitch yet" promise from slide 1) or, if it's meant as a fast
flash-preview ("here's where we end up tonight"), say so explicitly:
*"Quick preview of where tonight can lead if it makes sense — we'll come
back to this properly at the end."*

**Slide 4 — Four Objectives.** Good note ("ask the room why you're here")
— make it two-way: *"Type in chat which one's closest to you before I
even show the poll."* Gets typing started early.

**Slide 5 — Quick Poll.** Notes are strong and already do real work
(sets Q&A expectations, personalizes the rest of the session). Keep
exactly as-is.

**Slide 6 — "What Tonight Actually Is."** Your own flagged note: "MUST
IMPROVE THE 3 POINTS MORE." Current three points are serviceable but
generic. Sharper version, tied to what's actually unique about tonight:
1. *"The two questions almost nobody asks before they ballot"* (keep)
2. *"A real case — a client who nearly bought wrong, and what we did
   instead"* (this foreshadows 67–69 by name, which is a real open loop
   worth planting explicitly here)
3. *"My own honest price estimate for Thomson Reserve, at the end — not
   a guess, a built number"* (foreshadows slide 70)
Also keep the "sound fair? type fair" commitment device — it's a strong,
underused technique; the room typing "fair" in chat creates real
buy-in before you've asked for anything.

## ACT 7 — THE REAL CLIENT CASE STUDY (Slides 67–70)

**This is the strongest material in the entire deck.** Real stakes, real
tension (missed launches, bad ballot numbers, the temptation to just buy
something), a real decision-discipline payoff (the 2B1B→2B2B swap). Three
things to fix before it goes live:

1. **Anonymize** — no identifying specifics beyond what's needed for the
   story to land (budget range, ballot number, and the swap decision are
   enough; strip anything more specific).
2. **Slide 67's note is essentially a full script already** — it just
   needs tightening for pacing (it currently reads as stream-of-
   consciousness notes, not delivery-ready lines). Structure it as: setup
   (his situation) → rising tension (missed launches, bad advice
   elsewhere) → the turn (he came to us, we mapped more than just this
   purchase) → keep going into 68/69.
3. **Slide 69's closing line is excellent, use it almost verbatim:**
   *"You're not looking for an agent to go along with you — you want one
   who'll dare to tell you to stop when it doesn't make sense."* This is
   one of the best lines in the whole deck. Consider repeating a version
   of it again near the pitch (slide 81's "Do Nothing / DIY / Work With
   Us") since it directly reinforces Track 2's value.

**Slide 70 — Your price estimate.** This is where slides 45 and 51's open
loops both pay off — say so explicitly: *"Here's the pricing guide I
promised you at the start, and the projection I mentioned a few minutes
ago — all in one place."* Closing two open loops in the same breath is
more satisfying than closing them separately.
