only ever existed in conversation, never got written back into this file.

1. **Structural rebuild:** "Two Questions" is retired. The script now
   opens on one Big Domino line, then busts three specific beliefs in
   order — Secret 1 (Vehicle): the old way of choosing property vs. a
   repeatable process. Secret 2 (Internal): "I want both own-stay and
   investment" is not a decision, it's an avoided one. Secret 3
   (External): real unknowns exist, which is why you prepare ahead
   instead of deciding live. Almost all of v6's actual content (the
   4-Factors build, the objection-handling, before/during/after, the
   floor-area mistake) survives — it's re-homed under the secret it
   actually belongs to, not rewritten from scratch.
2. **The R.E.I. METHOD™ naming collision, resolved by using both, in
   their own lane:** Edmund has two real, separately-published assets
   both branded "THE R.E.I. METHOD™" — the teaching method used in this
   webinar and both YouTube videos (4 Factors: Land/Expenses/Profit/
   Benchmark, plus the 6-step Transformation/Land Cost/Entry Price/Exit
   Strategy/Who To Sell To/Rentability), and a separate lead-magnet ebook
   that defines it as Review → Evaluate → Identify. Rather than merge
   these into one confusing hybrid, v7 keeps them in the two places they
   already, separately, genuinely belong: **the teaching section of the
   webinar keeps the 4-Factors/6-step version** (unchanged, it's what's

   real asset of Edmund's, and the offer/journey is exactly where the

3. **The back third (the pitch) is rebuilt**, using a proven direct-
   response skeleton: declarative recap → decision moment → official
   invitation → offer as a named journey → architecture breakdown →
   value justification → "if all this did was" stack → price reveal →

4. **"HomeVestor"** is adopted as new identity language in Secret 2 (own-

**Everything else that governed v6 still governs v7, unchanged:** Thomson

**[SCREEN: Title]**

Hey, welcome in. Drop a "yes I can hear you" — and tell me: what's
pulling you toward Thomson Reserve? The MRT, the school, the hype, or
just "everyone's talking about it"?

**[Wait 30–45 sec, read 3–4 responses]**

Here's exactly what the next hour is. You're going to get real, specific
information on Thomson Reserve — the numbers, the honest read, the parts
that genuinely work and the parts that genuinely give me pause. That's
not the bait before the pitch. That's the actual content.

But tonight is not about convincing you Thomson Reserve is good or bad.
It could be an amazing development and still be the wrong property for
you. By the end of tonight, I want you to know what must be true before
you commit — and just as important, when the right decision is to walk
away. Because at two or three million dollars, guessing is simply too

You won't walk out of here having made a final decision. Nobody does that

an actual process — and someone who runs it — for whichever launch you
end up looking at, this one or otherwise.

Let's get into it.

## 2 — AUTHORITY + WHY NOW (3:00–8:00)

**[SCREEN: Portfolio timeline]**

Quick word on why I'm qualified to run this. Started under $600K, as an
HDB. Since then — a portfolio realistically sitting at $6.6 to $9 million
in net asset value. Two real deals: The Tre Ver, bought $1.05M, exited
$1.28M. Parc Clematis, bought $1.15M, exited $1.525M.

**[SCREEN: S&P vs property — real benchmark numbers]**

Here's the bar I hold every launch to, including my own money. The S&P
500 has averaged roughly 10.5% a year historically. Singapore private
property averages closer to 4.8% a year in capital growth, plus about 3%
rental yield. On paper, stocks win. So why buy property at all?

**Leverage.** A mortgage means you're not putting up the full price —
you're putting up a fraction, and the growth applies to the whole asset.
Run the real math on my own deals: Parc Clematis, roughly 22.9% a year on
the capital I actually committed. The Tre Ver, about 12.9%. Both cleared
the S&P bar. Both did it through leverage, not luck.

**[SCREEN: "3 forces"]**

Quickly, why now specifically: capital flowing in from a strong stock
market looking for a home, where interest rates go next, and — the one
that predicts prices best — land cost. Every time a developer pays more
for land than the last guy, that's next year's price floor. Keep that in
mind, because it's exactly what we're building on in a few minutes.

## 5 — SECRET 1 (VEHICLE): THE LIVE FAIR-PRICE BUILD (12:30–27:00)

**[SCREEN: Thomson Reserve fact strip]**

Fast facts. District 20, Bright Hill Drive. 99-year lease. 1,268 units,
six towers. Joint venture of three established developers. Direct link
to Upper Thomson MRT. A well-known school inside the priority radius.
Land cost, publicly disclosed: **$1,178 psf ppr.** No official price
list yet.

**[SCREEN: "Stop choosing property the old way"]**

Here's the first belief I want to challenge tonight. Most buyers believe
the job is to find a good property. That's too simple. The old way
sounds like: "everybody's talking about it," "I know this neighbourhood,"
"the project looks amazing," "my friend bought," "the agent says demand
is strong." Those things might be relevant. They cannot be the decision
process. The vehicle that actually works is a repeatable build — Land,
Expenses, Profit, Benchmark. Not another tip. Not another prediction. A
sequence.

**[SCREEN: "Let's build the actual number, live"]**

So let's run it, live, on this project.

**Land — $1,178 psf ppr.** Reasonable relative to recent nearby land
sales.

**[SCREEN: "But here's the objection you should already be having"]**

And I want to name something directly, because if you've read anything
about this launch, you've probably already thought it: *"even if the
land was cheap, why would the developer pass those savings on to me?
They don't price off their own cost — they price off what the market
will bear."* That's a real, fair objection — I've seen it argued almost
word for word on property forums discussing this exact project. And it's
correct. Cheap land is not a promise of a cheap price. It's one input
into a number, not the number itself. That's exactly why we keep
building.

**Expenses** — construction, financing, marketing. Not disclosed
publicly at this stage, which is normal. This is where an estimated
breakeven gets built — the same four factors, every time.

**Profit** — the margin a developer needs to justify building at all.
Unknown until pricing drops.

**Benchmark** — what's actually proven nearby right now, not what a
showflat feels like it's worth. Public analyst estimates on Thomson
Reserve currently range widely — from the low $2,000s to around $3,100
psf, depending which comparable you use. **That's the honest answer, not
a dodge: I cannot give you a confident number tonight, and neither can
anyone else being straight with you.**

**[SCREEN: "Hold it against the real bar"]**

Here's what you can do with even an estimated range, though. Take the
middle of that public estimate — call it roughly $2,600 to $2,900 psf —
and run it through the same leverage math I showed you on my own deals
earlier. At a typical loan quantum, does the growth needed to clear the
S&P bar over a realistic hold period look achievable, or does it need the
market to do something it hasn't reliably done? I'm not running your
specific numbers live tonight — that depends on your loan, your quantum,
your hold period — but that's the exact test, and it's the same one I'm
applying to my own decision.

**[SCREEN: verdict by objective]**

So — this vehicle, applied honestly, by objective. **Stay-for-free:** the
location and school fundamentals are genuinely strong — reasonable fit,
if the final price doesn't outrun what "for free" actually requires.
**Kids/school:** genuinely strong, one of the better arguments for this
project specifically. **Legacy:** reasonable, with the 99-year lease
worth factoring into a multi-decade hold. **Pure investment:** needs real
scrutiny — until there's a confirmed price, I can't tell you this clears
the bar, and I won't pretend otherwise.

**[MICRO-CTA]** This is the vehicle — not guessing, not the loudest
analyst, a repeatable build you can run on any project, launched or not.
Which raises the next question: even with the right process, do you
actually know what you're optimising for?

## 6 — SECRET 2 (INTERNAL): GUESSING VS. DECIDING (27:00–34:00)

**[SCREEN: "I want both"]**

This next part has nothing to do with Thomson Reserve's price. It has to
do with a decision most buyers never consciously make — and it changes
how you read everything else tonight.

Earlier, a lot of you told me your objective was own-stay, or investment
— but if I'm honest, most people typing that are quietly thinking "both."
Familiar area, good for the kids, AND strong growth. I want to be really
clear: that's not wrong to want. But it's not one goal. It's two. And
tonight, almost nobody actually separates them.

**[SCREEN: "Every property decision has a priority — whether you name it
or not"]**

Here's the belief I want to bust. You don't get to have both, equally,
in the same unit, without ranking one above the other. If you don't
consciously choose which one wins, the decision doesn't stay balanced —
it defaults to whichever pulls hardest emotionally in the room, usually
the familiar, emotional pull, while the growth side quietly loses without
you ever deciding it should.

**[SCREEN: unit-mix proof]**

Let me prove this using Thomson Reserve itself. The bulk of its 1,268
units sit in the smaller 2- and 3-bedroom range. That split already tells
you something — this project, like every project, was structured with
two different kinds of buyer in mind, whether you noticed or not. If your
#1 priority is proximity and lifestyle, this location earns its place on
a shortlist. If your #1 priority is growth, you're now comparing it
against a completely different bar — the same S&P benchmark I showed you
earlier, not a feeling about the neighbourhood.

**[SCREEN: "A crowded ballot doesn't tell you which goal it's serving"]**

And here's the trap this feeds. If Thomson Reserve sells out fast on
launch weekend, everyone will read that as proof it's a great investment.
It isn't. It's proof other people showed up too. A queue doesn't tell you
which of your two goals it's actually satisfying — it just gives you
false permission to skip ranking them at all.

**[SCREEN: "Become a HomeVestor"]**

You don't have to become a hardcore investor, and you don't have to
ignore your lifestyle either. What I want you to become tonight is what I
call a **HomeVestor** — someone who knows their primary objective,
satisfies the lifestyle needs that genuinely matter, and then protects
the financial decision with data. Not "confused buyer who wants
everything." A HomeVestor with a ranked priority.

**[MICRO-CTA]** You don't need me to tell you whether to want family or
growth more. You need someone to make you name it — before you're
standing in a showflat designed to make you feel like you don't have to
choose.

## 7 — SECRET 3 (EXTERNAL): REAL UNKNOWNS, REAL ODDS (34:00–42:00)

**[SCREEN: "Thomson Reserve hasn't launched"]**

Everything so far assumed you've got the right process and a decided
priority. This last piece is the one nobody controls: timing. Thomson
Reserve hasn't launched. There's no official price list yet. Anyone who
tells you they already know the exact number is guessing. Tonight isn't
about knowing — it's about being ready before the unknowns resolve.

**[SCREEN: "Even if it's a fit — will you get it?"]**

Say Thomson Reserve genuinely fits your objective, and you've ranked your
priority. That still doesn't mean you get the unit you want, at a price
you're happy with. That's a different question — probability — and
almost nobody checks it before they ballot.

**[SCREEN: unit-mix odds]**

Your actual odds depend heavily on what you're buying. With the bulk of
1,268 units concentrated in the smaller 2- and 3-bedroom range, a buyer
chasing one of those types faces very different odds than someone after a
larger family unit. Same project, structurally different probability,
depending purely on what you need. Most people never check this before
they ballot — they find out live, standing in a room, when it's too late
to plan around it.

**[SCREEN: "Prepare ahead, don't decide live"]**

This is exactly why the process is prepare-ahead, not decide-live. The
one thing you can control isn't the price list. It's being ready before
it drops.

**[MICRO-CTA]** You can research this project for weeks, pick the right
objective, even get the price right — and still lose, because of the one
thing nobody told you to check.

## 8 — BEFORE / DURING / AFTER THE BALLOT (42:00–47:00)

**[SCREEN: "3 phases"]**

Here's the actual process, in three parts — because the ballot itself is
not the only decision point, and this is exactly what "prepared, not
guessing" looks like in practice.

**Before.** Your walk-away price, set weeks ahead, before you're standing
in a room. Your ranked list of stacks, decided at home. And — something
that belongs here and gets skipped constantly — your financing actually
sorted. If you're not financially ready to decide the moment your number
is called, the right move is to walk away and come back when you are. A
fair unit at a fair price will still exist somewhere. There is no version
of this where being unprepared works in your favour.

**During.** What you actually do live, in the room, if your first choice
is gone before your number comes up. This is discipline, not panic — and
it's the single hardest thing to do without a plan already decided in
advance, because the room is built to make you decide fast.

**After.** What changes once you're holding the unit — or didn't get it.
Either outcome needs a next move mapped, not just a pause.

Sometimes the best purchase is the one you don't make. Recently, we
guided a client through another major launch. Good location. Units still
available when his turn came. But when we compared the actual unit, the
price, and the alternatives available to him, the risk no longer
justified the decision — at around $1.9 million, for that specific unit.
So we told him: don't buy. Not "you already queued, just take it." We
said no. That's the difference between trying to buy a property and
trying to make the right property decision.

## 9 — THE FLOOR-AREA MISTAKE (BONUS INSIGHT) (47:00–49:00)

**[SCREEN: "Total floor area vs. what you actually live in"]**

One more thing almost every buyer gets wrong, across every launch, not
just this one — the gap between total floor area and usable, liveable
space. Developers list total floor area, which can include balconies and
aircon ledges. A few years back, the rules changed for how new launches
must measure this, so newer, harmonised layouts genuinely give you more
usable space per square foot than older stock quoting the same number.
Compare psf without knowing this, and an older unit can look "cheaper"
than it actually is, once you adjust for what you're really living in.
Worth checking on any unit you're comparing, Thomson Reserve included.
