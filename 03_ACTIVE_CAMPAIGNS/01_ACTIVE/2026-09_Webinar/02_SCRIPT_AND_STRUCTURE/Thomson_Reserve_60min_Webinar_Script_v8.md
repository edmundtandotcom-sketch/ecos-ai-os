## 5 — SECRET 1 (VEHICLE): THE LIVE FAIR-PRICE BUILD (23:00–39:00)

**[SCREEN: Thomson Reserve fact strip]**

Fast facts. District 20, Bright Hill Drive. 99-year lease. 1,268 units,
six towers. Joint venture of three established developers. Direct link
to Upper Thomson MRT. A well-known school inside the priority radius.
Land cost, publicly disclosed: **$1,178 psf ppr.** No official price
list yet.

**[SCREEN: "Stop choosing property the old way"]**

Here's the first belief I want to challenge tonight. Most buyers believe
the job is to find a good property. That's too simple. The old way
sounds like: "everybody's talking about it," "I know this neighbourhood,"
"the project looks amazing," "my friend bought," "the agent says demand
is strong." Those things might be relevant. They cannot be the decision
process. The vehicle that actually works is a repeatable build — Land,
Expenses, Profit, Benchmark. Not another tip. Not another prediction. A
sequence.

**[SCREEN: "Let's build the actual number, live"]**

So let's run it, live, on this project.

**Land — $1,178 psf ppr.** Reasonable relative to recent nearby land
sales.

**[SCREEN: "But here's the objection you should already be having"]**

And I want to name something directly, because if you've read anything
about this launch, you've probably already thought it: *"even if the
land was cheap, why would the developer pass those savings on to me?
They don't price off their own cost — they price off what the market
will bear."* That's a real, fair objection — I've seen it argued almost
word for word on property forums discussing this exact project. And it's
correct. Cheap land is not a promise of a cheap price. It's one input
into a number, not the number itself. That's exactly why we keep
building.

**[ENGAGE]** Quick gut-check before I continue — type your guess. Do you
think the developer prices this to reward buyers for the cheap land, or
to protect their own margin? I want your honest answer, not the "right"
one.

**Expenses** — construction, financing, marketing. Not disclosed
publicly at this stage, which is normal. This is where an estimated
breakeven gets built — the same four factors, every time.

**Profit** — the margin a developer needs to justify building at all.
Unknown until pricing drops.

**Benchmark** — what's actually proven nearby right now, not what a
showflat feels like it's worth. Public analyst estimates on Thomson
Reserve currently range widely — from the low $2,000s to around $3,100
psf, depending which comparable you use. **That's the honest answer, not
a dodge: I cannot give you a confident number tonight, and neither can
anyone else being straight with you.**

**[SCREEN: "The multiplier gut-check"]**

Here's a fast way to sanity-check any launch, including this one.
Historically, developers have priced launches at roughly 2 times their
land cost, per square foot. Run that here: $1,178 times 2 is roughly
$2,350 to $2,400. But that's the OLD rule. Construction, financing and
land betterment costs have all climbed, and the multiplier's drifted
closer to 2.3 times more recently. Run it again: $1,178 times 2.3 lands
you around $2,700. Notice that sits right at the top of the benchmark
range I just showed you — two different ways of checking the same
number, landing in the same neighbourhood. That agreement is what makes
a range worth trusting, instead of one guess.

**[SCREEN: "Hold it against the real bar"]**

Here's what you can do with even an estimated range, though. Take the
middle of that public estimate — call it roughly $2,600 to $2,900 psf —
and run it through the same leverage math I showed you on my own deals
earlier. At a typical loan quantum, does the growth needed to clear the
S&P bar over a realistic hold period look achievable, or does it need the
market to do something it hasn't reliably done? I'm not running your
specific numbers live tonight — that depends on your loan, your quantum,
your hold period — but that's the exact test, and it's the same one I'm
applying to my own decision.

**[SCREEN: verdict by objective]**

So — this vehicle, applied honestly, by objective. **Stay-for-free:** the
location and school fundamentals are genuinely strong — reasonable fit,
if the final price doesn't outrun what "for free" actually requires.
**Kids/school:** genuinely strong, one of the better arguments for this
project specifically. **Legacy:** reasonable, with the 99-year lease

the bar, and I won't pretend otherwise.

**[MICRO-CTA]** This is the vehicle — not guessing, not the loudest

**[SCREEN: "Every property decision has a priority — whether you name it

Here's the belief I want to bust. You don't get to have both, equally,
in the same unit, without ranking one above the other. If you don't
consciously choose which one wins, the decision doesn't stay balanced —
it defaults to whichever pulls hardest emotionally in the room, usually
the familiar, emotional pull, while the growth side quietly loses without

**[SCREEN: unit-mix proof]**

## 8 — BEFORE / DURING / AFTER THE BALLOT (61:00–67:00)

**[SCREEN: "3 phases"]**

Here's the actual process, in three parts — because the ballot itself is
not the only decision point, and this is exactly what "prepared, not
guessing" looks like in practice.

**Before.** Your walk-away price, set weeks ahead, before you're standing
in a room. Your ranked list of stacks, decided at home. And — something
that belongs here and gets skipped constantly — your financing actually
sorted. If you're not financially ready to decide the moment your number
is called, the right move is to walk away and come back when you are. A
fair unit at a fair price will still exist somewhere. There is no version
of this where being unprepared works in your favour.

**During.** What you actually do live, in the room, if your first choice
is gone before your number comes up. This is discipline, not panic — and
it's the single hardest thing to do without a plan already decided in
advance, because the room is built to make you decide fast.

**After.** What changes once you're holding the unit — or didn't get it.
Either outcome needs a next move mapped, not just a pause.

Sometimes the best purchase is the one you don't make. Recently, we
guided a client through another major launch. Good location. Units still
available when his turn came. But when we compared the actual unit, the
price, and the alternatives available to him, the risk no longer
justified the decision — at around $1.9 million, for that specific unit.
So we told him: don't buy. Not "you already queued, just take it." We
said no. That's the difference between trying to buy a property and
trying to make the right property decision.

## 9 — THE FLOOR-AREA MISTAKE (BONUS INSIGHT) (67:00–69:00)

**[SCREEN: "Total floor area vs. what you actually live in"]**

One more thing almost every buyer gets wrong, across every launch, not
just this one — the gap between total floor area and usable, liveable
space. Developers list total floor area, which can include balconies and
aircon ledges. A few years back, the rules changed for how new launches
must measure this, so newer, harmonised layouts genuinely give you more
usable space per square foot than older stock quoting the same number.
Compare psf without knowing this, and an older unit can look "cheaper"
than it actually is, once you adjust for what you're really living in.
Worth checking on any unit you're comparing, Thomson Reserve included.
