## WHAT CHANGED AND WHY

Three rounds of feedback shaped this version, in order:

1. **v4 → v5:** replaced an invented framework with Edmund's real,
   consistently-used methodology (Land/Expenses/Profit/Benchmark + Exit +
   a What-Works/Why-Pause verdict), discovered in his own already-
   published Amberwood/Serra reviews.
2. **v5 → this version, structurally:** dropped the Amberwood/Serra
   comparison — it diluted focus. Replaced it with **Two Questions**
   that reorganize the entire 60 minutes: **is Thomson Reserve right for
   YOU (Fit), and if it is, can you actually GET it (Probability).** Most
   buyers only ever ask the first one.
3. **This version, content:** added a **tangible, numbers-driven
   centerpiece** — a live fair-price estimate for Thomson Reserve,
   reverse-engineered from its real disclosed land cost, held against a
   real beat-the-market hurdle — because Fit and Probability alone read
   as concepts, not proof. Also wove in **real buyer objections and
   myths**, sourced from actual property forums and buyer-education
   content (not invented), so the webinar addresses what people are
   already thinking, not a strawman.

**The standing instruction behind all of it, restated because it governs
every line below:** Thomson Reserve stays the hook and the spine, not a
prop for an abstract lecture. Every concept — Fit, Probability, the fair-
price build — resolves immediately into real Thomson Reserve specifics.
Nobody should leave feeling like they sat through a generic framework
that happened to mention this project a few times.

**The honest promise the whole session is built to deliver, stated
explicitly in the open and the close:** nobody makes a full decision in
60 minutes. What they leave with is real information on Thomson Reserve
specifically, *and* the knowledge that a structured process — and someone
who runs it — exists to walk them through this project, or any other
launch, from objective, to financing structure, to portfolio fit, through
to after the ballot.

**Sourcing discipline, unchanged from prior versions:** nothing from the
Thomson Reserve YouTube script, nothing from another agent's proprietary
framework or case studies. New this version: real market research (public
forum sentiment, public buyer-education sources) used to anchor trap-
busting content in genuine buyer psychology — sources logged in Appendix
B.

## 1 — OPEN: THE HOOK, THE PROMISE (0:00–3:00)

**[SCREEN: Title]**

Hey, welcome in. Drop a "yes I can hear you" — and tell me: what's
pulling you toward Thomson Reserve? The MRT, the school, the hype, or
just "everyone's talking about it"?

**[Wait 30–45 sec, read 3–4 responses]**

Here's exactly what the next hour is. You're going to get real, specific
information on Thomson Reserve — the numbers, the honest read, the parts
that genuinely work and the parts that genuinely give me pause. That's
not the bait before the pitch. That's the actual content.

But I'm also going to show you something most people never get shown:
the two questions that actually decide whether a launch like this is a
good move — not just "is it a good project," but "is it right for you,"
and "will you actually get it." Almost nobody asks that second one, and
it's the one that decides whether you win or lose on ballot day.

You won't walk out of here having made a final decision. Nobody does that
in an hour, and I'd be lying if I said otherwise. What you'll walk out
with is real information on this project, and the knowledge that there's
an actual process — and someone who runs it — for whichever launch you
end up looking at, this one or otherwise.

Let's get into it.

## 2 — AUTHORITY + WHY NOW (3:00–8:00)

**[SCREEN: Portfolio timeline]**

Quick word on why I'm qualified to run this. Started under $600K, as an
HDB. Since then — a portfolio realistically sitting at $6.6 to $9 million
in net asset value. Two real deals: The Tre Ver, bought $1.05M, exited
$1.28M. Parc Clematis, bought $1.15M, exited $1.525M.

**[SCREEN: S&P vs property — real benchmark numbers]**

Here's the bar I hold every launch to, including my own money. The S&P
500 has averaged roughly 10.5% a year historically. Singapore private
property averages closer to 4.8% a year in capital growth, plus about 3%
rental yield. On paper, stocks win. So why buy property at all?

**Leverage.** A mortgage means you're not putting up the full price —
you're putting up a fraction, and the growth applies to the whole asset.
Run the real math on my own deals: Parc Clematis, roughly 22.9% a year on
the capital I actually committed. The Tre Ver, about 12.9%. Both cleared
the S&P bar. Both did it through leverage, not luck. That's the actual
question for tonight — not "will Thomson Reserve go up," but "will it
clear this bar, on the capital you'd actually commit."

**[SCREEN: "3 forces"]**

Quickly, why now specifically: capital flowing in from a strong stock
market looking for a home, where interest rates go next, and — the one
that predicts prices best — land cost. Every time a developer pays more
for land than the last guy, that's next year's price floor. Keep that in
mind, because it's exactly what we're building on in a few minutes.

## 5 — QUESTION 1: FIT — THE LIVE FAIR-PRICE BUILD (13:00–28:00)

**[SCREEN: Thomson Reserve fact strip]**

Fast facts. District 20, Bright Hill Drive. 99-year lease. 1,268 units,
six towers. Joint venture of three established developers. Direct link
to Upper Thomson MRT. A well-known school inside the priority radius.
Land cost, publicly disclosed: **$1,178 psf ppr.** No official price
list yet.

**[SCREEN: "Let's build the actual number, live"]**

Here's what I actually want to do with you right now — not describe the
process, run it. Land, Expenses, Profit, Benchmark. That's how a fair
price gets built, and I'll show you exactly how it applies here.

**Land — $1,178 psf ppr.** Reasonable relative to recent nearby land
sales.

**[SCREEN: "But here's the objection you should already be having"]**

And I want to name something directly, because if you've been reading
anything about this launch, you've probably already thought it, or seen
someone say it: *"even if the land was cheap, why would the developer
pass those savings on to me? They don't price off their own cost — they
price off what the market will bear."* That's a real, fair objection —
I've seen it argued almost word for word on property forums discussing
this exact project. And it's correct. Cheap land is not a promise of a
cheap price. It's one input into a number, not the number itself. That's
exactly why we keep building.

**Expenses** — construction, financing, marketing. Not disclosed
publicly at this stage, which is normal. This is where an estimated
breakeven gets built — the same four factors, every time.

**Profit** — the margin a developer needs to justify building at all.
Unknown until pricing drops.

**Benchmark** — what's actually proven nearby right now, not what a
showflat feels like it's worth. Public analyst estimates on Thomson
Reserve currently range widely — from the low $2,000s to around $3,100
psf, depending which comparable you use. That range is wide because
nobody actually knows yet. **That's the honest answer, not a dodge: I
cannot give you a confident number tonight, and neither can anyone else
being straight with you.**

**[SCREEN: "Hold it against the real bar"]**

Here's what you can do with even an estimated range, though. Take the
middle of that public estimate — call it roughly $2,600 to $2,900 psf —
and run it through the same leverage math I showed you on my own deals a
few minutes ago. At a typical loan quantum, does the growth needed to
clear the S&P bar over a realistic hold period look achievable, or does
it need the market to do something it hasn't reliably done? I'm not
running your specific numbers live tonight — that depends on your loan,
your quantum, your hold period — but that's the exact test, and it's the
same one I'm applying to my own decision.

**[SCREEN: verdict by objective]**

So — Question 1, honestly, by objective. **Stay-for-free:** the location
and school fundamentals are genuinely strong — reasonable fit, if the
final price doesn't outrun what "for free" actually requires. **Kids/
school:** genuinely strong, this is one of the better arguments for this
project specifically. **Legacy:** reasonable, with the 99-year lease
worth factoring into a multi-decade hold. **Pure investment:** needs real
scrutiny — until there's a confirmed price, I can't tell you this clears
the bar, and I won't pretend otherwise.

## 6 — QUESTION 2: PROBABILITY (28:00–35:00)

**[SCREEN: "Even if it's a fit — will you get it?"]**

Here's the question almost nobody asks. Say Thomson Reserve genuinely
fits your objective. That doesn't mean you get the unit you want, at a
price you're happy with.

**[SCREEN: "A crowded ballot proves demand. It doesn't prove value."]**

I want to be direct about something, because it's a real trap. If
Thomson Reserve sells out fast on launch weekend, that will get
reported everywhere as proof it's a great investment. It isn't proof of
that. It's proof the ballot was crowded. Plenty of launches that sold out
fast have gone on to underperform after completion, because sellout
speed reflects pricing and hype on the day — not what happens to the
project over the next five to ten years. Don't let a queue confirm a
decision your own numbers haven't confirmed yet.

**[SCREEN: unit-mix odds]**

And your actual odds depend heavily on what you're buying. With the bulk
of 1,268 units concentrated in the smaller 2- and 3-bedroom range, a
buyer chasing one of those types faces very different odds than someone
after a larger family unit. Same project, structurally different
probability, depending purely on what you need. Most people never check
this before they ballot — they find out live, standing in a room, when
it's too late to plan around it.

## 7 — BEFORE / DURING / AFTER THE BALLOT (35:00–42:00)

**[SCREEN: "3 phases"]**

Here's the actual process, in three parts — because the ballot itself is
not the only decision point.

**Before.** Your walk-away price, set weeks ahead, before you're standing
in a room. Your ranked list of stacks, decided at home. And — something
that belongs here and gets skipped constantly — your financing actually
sorted. Real balloting advice, and I'd tell you the same thing: if
you're not financially ready to decide the moment your number is called,
the right move is to walk away and come back when you are. A fair unit
at a fair price will still exist somewhere. There is no version of this
where being unprepared works in your favour.

**During.** What you actually do live, in the room, if your first choice
is gone before your number comes up. This is discipline, not panic — and
it's the single hardest thing to do without a plan already decided in
advance, because the room is built to make you decide fast.

**After.** What changes once you're holding the unit — or didn't get it.
Either outcome needs a next move mapped, not just a pause.

## 8 — THE THING ALMOST EVERYONE GETS WRONG (42:00–45:00)

**[SCREEN: "Total floor area vs. what you actually live in"]**

One of the most common things buyers get wrong across every launch, not
just this one — the gap between total floor area and usable, liveable
space. Developers list total floor area, which can include balconies and
aircon ledges. A few years back, the rules changed for how new launches
must measure this, so newer, harmonised layouts genuinely give you more
usable space per square foot than older stock quoting the same number.
Compare psf without knowing this, and an older unit can look "cheaper"
than it actually is, once you adjust for what you're really living in.
Worth checking on any unit you're comparing, Thomson Reserve included.

## 9 — THE REFRAME: GAMBLE VS. PREDICTABLE (45:00–48:00)

**[SCREEN: the choice]**

Here's the actual decision in front of everyone watching. You can wait
for the ballot and hope your number and the price both land in your
favour — a gamble on two things you don't control. Or you can walk in
with a plan already priced out, already knows your walk-away, already
knows your real odds. Not a gamble. Prepared.

Every client who works with us gets this same process — even the ones
who end up balloting for Thomson Reserve anyway. It's not about talking
anyone out of anything. It's about not finding out the hard way that
being unprepared cost you the unit, or the price, or both.
