## MODULE 2 — AUTHORITY + WHY NOW (3:00–8:00) · Slides 3–5

**Say:**
> Quick word on why I'm qualified to run this. Started under $600K, as
> an HDB. Since then — a portfolio realistically sitting at $6.6 to $9
> million in net asset value. Two real deals: The Tre Ver, bought
> $1.05M, exited $1.28M. Parc Clematis, bought $1.15M, exited $1.525M.
>
> Here's the bar I hold every launch to, including my own money. The S&P
> 500 has averaged roughly 10.5% a year historically. Singapore private
> property averages closer to 4.8% a year in capital growth, plus about
> 3% rental yield. On paper, stocks win. So why buy property at all?
>
> **Leverage.** A mortgage means you're not putting up the full price —
> you're putting up a fraction, and the growth applies to the whole
> asset. Run the real math on my own deals: Parc Clematis, roughly 22.9%
> a year on the capital I actually committed. The Tre Ver, about 12.9%.
> Both cleared the S&P bar. Both did it through leverage, not luck.
> That's the actual question for tonight — not "will Thomson Reserve go
> up," but "will it clear this bar, on the capital you'd actually
> commit."
>
> Quickly, why now specifically: capital flowing in from a strong stock
> market looking for a home, where interest rates go next, and — the one
> that predicts prices best — land cost. Every time a developer pays
> more for land than the last guy, that's next year's price floor. Keep
> that in mind, because it's exactly what we're building on in a few
> minutes.

## MODULE 5 — QUESTION 1: FIT — THE LIVE FAIR-PRICE BUILD (13:00–28:00) · Slides 9–17

**Say:**

> units, six towers. Joint venture of three established developers.
> Direct link to Upper Thomson MRT. A well-known school inside the
> priority radius. Land cost, publicly disclosed: **$1,178 psf ppr.** No
> official price list yet.
>
> Here's what I actually want to do with you right now — not describe
> the process, run it. Land, Expenses, Profit, Benchmark. That's how a
> fair price gets built, and I'll show you exactly how it applies here.
>
> **Land — $1,178 psf ppr.** Reasonable relative to recent nearby land
> sales.
>
> And I want to name something directly, because if you've been reading
> anything about this launch, you've probably already thought it, or
> seen someone say it: *"even if the land was cheap, why would the
> developer pass those savings on to me? They don't price off their own
> cost — they price off what the market will bear."* That's a real, fair
> objection — I've seen it argued almost word for word on property
> forums discussing this exact project. And it's correct. Cheap land is
> not a promise of a cheap price. It's one input into a number, not the
> number itself. That's exactly why we keep building.
>
> **Expenses** — construction, financing, marketing. Not disclosed
> publicly at this stage, which is normal. This is where an estimated
> breakeven gets built — the same four factors, every time.
>
> **Profit** — the margin a developer needs to justify building at all.
> Unknown until pricing drops.
>
> **Benchmark** — what's actually proven nearby right now, not what a
> showflat feels like it's worth. Public analyst estimates on Thomson
> Reserve currently range widely — from the low $2,000s to around $3,100
> psf, depending which comparable you use. That range is wide because
> nobody actually knows yet. **That's the honest answer, not a dodge: I
> cannot give you a confident number tonight, and neither can anyone
> else being straight with you.**
>
> Here's what you can do with even an estimated range, though. Take the
> middle of that public estimate — call it roughly $2,600 to $2,900 psf
> — and run it through the same leverage math I showed you on my own
> deals a few minutes ago. At a typical loan quantum, does the growth
> needed to clear the S&P bar over a realistic hold period look
> achievable, or does it need the market to do something it hasn't
> reliably done? I'm not running your specific numbers live tonight —
> that depends on your loan, your quantum, your hold period — but that's
> the exact test, and it's the same one I'm applying to my own decision.
>
> So — Question 1, honestly, by objective. **Stay-for-free:** the
> location and school fundamentals are genuinely strong — reasonable
> fit, if the final price doesn't outrun what "for free" actually
> requires. **Kids/school:** genuinely strong, this is one of the better
> arguments for this project specifically. **Legacy:** reasonable, with
> the 99-year lease worth factoring into a multi-decade hold. **Pure
> investment:** needs real scrutiny — until there's a confirmed price, I
> can't tell you this clears the bar, and I won't pretend otherwise.

> different probability, depending purely on what you need. Most people
> never check this before they ballot — they find out live, standing in
> a room, when it's too late to plan around it.

## MODULE 8 — THE THING ALMOST EVERYONE GETS WRONG (42:00–45:00) · Slide 22

**Say:**
> One of the most common things buyers get wrong across every launch,
> not just this one — the gap between total floor area and usable,
> liveable space. Developers list total floor area, which can include
> balconies and aircon ledges. A few years back, the rules changed for
> how new launches must measure this, so newer, harmonised layouts
> genuinely give you more usable space per square foot than older stock
> quoting the same number. Compare psf without knowing this, and an
> older unit can look "cheaper" than it actually is, once you adjust for
> what you're really living in. Worth checking on any unit you're
> comparing, Thomson Reserve included.
