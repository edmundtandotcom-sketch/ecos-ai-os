# Thomson Reserve Webinar — Delivery Cheat Sheet (v1)
Two tools for delivery, not more script: a phrase bank to keep the
session conversational instead of lecture-y, and a case-study placement
map so real client stories land at the moments they actually teach
something — not as a standalone highlight reel.

Companion to `02_SCRIPT_AND_STRUCTURE/Thomson_Reserve_60min_Webinar_Script_v1.md`
(v6). Module numbers below match that script.

---

## PART 1 — PRE-FRAMING PHRASE BANK

Read this before you go live, or keep it open on a second screen. These
are the lines that do the psychological work — presupposition, rhetorical
questions, "notice that" framing — rather than direct statements. Don't
read them verbatim as a script; let them remind you of the *move* to
make at that moment.

**Module 1 — Open**
- "Tell me in the chat — what's actually pulling you toward this?"
- "You won't walk out of here with a final decision. I want to be upfront
  about that before we start."
- "This isn't the bait before the pitch. This is the actual content."

**Module 2 — Authority + Why Now**
- "Anyone can say property went up. The only question that means
  anything is whether it beat the alternative."
- "I hold my own money to the same bar I'm about to hold this project to."
- "Notice I'm not telling you property beats stocks outright. I'm telling
  you *why* it can, when it does."

**Module 3 — The Two Questions**
- "Most people watching tonight only ever ask one question. We're asking
  two."
- "Hold both of these in your head — we're answering both, live."

**Module 4 — Objective**
- "Don't tell me what sounds sensible. Tell me what's actually true for
  you."
- "Notice which one you picked. We're coming back to it twice more
  tonight."

**Module 5 — Fit / the live fair-price build**
- "Let's not describe the process. Let's actually run it, right now."
- "If you've read anything about this launch, you've probably already had
  this exact objection — let's deal with it head-on instead of pretending
  it doesn't exist."
- "That's the honest answer. Not a dodge."
- "I'm not going to pretend to know a number nobody actually knows yet —
  and neither does anyone telling you otherwise."

**Module 6 — Probability**
- "Here's the question almost nobody in this market ever asks."
- "A crowded ballot proves demand. It does not prove value. Say that back
  to yourself the next time a launch sells out in a weekend."
- "Same project. Structurally different odds — depending purely on what
  you actually need."

**Module 7 — Before / During / After**
- "The ballot itself is not the only decision point tonight — it's just
  the loudest one."
- "This is discipline, not panic. And it's the hardest thing to do
  without a plan already decided somewhere quiet."
- "There is no version of this where being unprepared works in your
  favour."

**Module 8 — The floor-area "didn't know that" beat**
- "Here's something almost nobody's been told straight."
- "Worth checking on any unit you're comparing — this one included."

**Module 9 — Gamble vs. predictable**
- "Notice I didn't say don't buy it. I said don't gamble on it. Those are
  different things."
- "This isn't about talking anyone out of anything."

**Module 10 — Bridge**
- "Everything tonight was general — mine, or an average scenario. Yours
  is different."

**Module 11 — The Pitch**
- "You're not deciding today. You're deciding whether you want a real
  process behind you when you do."
- "If your objective tonight was [X] — here's exactly what I want to do
  for you."

**Module 12 — Q&A**
- "Ask me anything — about this project, the process, or where you're
  at right now."
- "Good question. What does that actually look like against [Fit /
  Probability]?" — then teach the thinking, not the verdict, per the
  standing Q&A rule in the main script.

## PART 2 — CASE STUDY PLACEMENT MAP

**The rule governing every slot below:** a case study earns its place by
demonstrating *the thinking process* — the mistake someone was walking
into, the moment it got caught, and what changed because of it. It is
never there just to prove Edmund has happy clients. If a story doesn't
show a diagnostic moment, it doesn't belong in this webinar — it belongs
in a testimonial reel instead.

Five slots, spread across the 60 minutes so the webinar never runs two
in a row. Each has: where it goes, what it needs to prove, the shape to
tell it in, and a **placeholder template only** — bracketed figures, not
invented numbers, so nothing here reads as a real claim. Swap in a real,
consented case before this goes live; do not deliver these placeholders
as-is.

### Slot 1 — "The Misdiagnosed Objective" (in or right after Module 4)
**Proves:** most people have never actually named their real objective —
the diagnostic conversation itself changes the outcome, before any
numbers get run.
**Shape:** client states an objective → a few questions in, the *real*
driver turns out to be something else → the shortlist changes completely
once it's named correctly.
**Template:**
> "Had a [couple / client] come in telling me they wanted [stated
> objective — e.g. 'a good investment']. Few questions in, it became
> clear what they actually needed was [the real driver — e.g. 'a place
> big enough for both sets of parents to eventually move in']. Totally
> different shortlist once we named that properly — and they'd never sat
> down and said it out loud before, not even to each other."

### Slot 2 — "The Client Who Almost Bought The Hype" (in Module 5, right after the fair-price build)
**Proves:** the fair-price process catches a mistake *in progress*, not
just after the fact — this is the thinking process working live.
**Shape:** client excited about a hyped launch → we ran the actual
numbers together → the numbers didn't support the price everyone assumed
was fine → they paused before committing.
**Template:**
> "Ran this exact process with a client on [a different hyped launch]
> [timeframe] ago. They were ready to ballot at [assumed fair price] just
> because everyone around them was. We built the real number together —
> land, expenses, margin, benchmark — and it came out closer to
> [lower estimate]. That gap alone was worth pausing for."

### Slot 3 — "The Unit-Type Mismatch" (in Module 6, right after the odds point)
**Proves:** probability is a real, checkable number — not a vague
caution — and most people find out the hard way instead of in advance.
**Shape:** client wanted a specific unit type → didn't realise the mix
meant thin odds for that type → we flagged it before the ballot, not
after → they adjusted strategy (different stack, different project, or
went in with eyes open) instead of finding out live.
**Template:**
> "A client wanted [a specific unit type] at a launch with a similar mix
> to what we just looked at. Ran the actual math on how many of that
> exact type existed versus expected demand — odds were genuinely thin.
> We caught that before ballot day, not during it, and [what changed as
> a result — adjusted stack choice / went in prepared for a fallback /
> chose differently]."

### Slot 4 — "The Walk-Away Story" (in Module 7, in the "During" beat)
**Proves:** discipline under real, live pressure is the actual product —
this is the single highest-trust story type available, use it well.
**Shape:** client had a walk-away price set weeks ahead → on the actual
day, in the room, with the hype/queue pressure real → they were tempted
to go above it → held the line (or we held it with them) → what that
protected them from.
**Template:**
> "Client had their walk-away set at [$X] weeks before preview. Ballot
> day, their first-choice stack was gone, energy in the room was pushing
> everyone to just take whatever was left. They almost went to [$X +
> premium]. We'd set the number together in advance for exactly this
> moment — held it, walked, and [what happened next — got a better unit
> elsewhere / came back to a later phase / etc.]."

### Slot 5 — "We Said Wait" (in Module 9, gamble vs. predictable)
**Proves:** contrarian credibility — telling someone *not* to move is the
single most trust-building story type in the whole webinar, because it's
the opposite of what a sales pitch would ever say.
**Shape:** client wanted to buy now → the honest numbers said the timing
or the project wasn't right yet → advised them to wait → outcome
vindicated the call (or at minimum, protected them from a bad entry).
**Template:**
> "Had a client dead set on buying [timeframe] ago. Ran the numbers — the
> honest answer was 'not yet,' not 'yes.' Told them exactly that, even
> though it meant no deal that month. [What happened as a result — they
> came back later at a better entry / avoided a launch that underperformed
> / etc.]. That's the same standard tonight gets held to."
