## 1. Verdict

The session already converts better per attendee than Sept 16 (CPA $700 → $515). The teaching is strong: the fair-price build, Lorong Puntong / sit-in-the-developer's-chair, the Lentor cheap-land-high-price proof, the 716/350 unit-mix reveal and the $1.9M "we said no" story are all genuine belief-changers. What stops it being a high-converting webinar is not the content — it is that the content is not ARRANGED as a sales argument:

1. **No Big Domino.** A high-converting webinar drives one belief the whole way down: "if I believe X, I have to act." Yours has the material for it — *"a popular project can still be the wrong purchase, and nobody in the room is paid to tell you to stop"* — but it is said in passing five times instead of installed once, then proved three times, then closed on.
2. **The title question is never answered.** "$3M budget — am I gonna buy Thomson Reserve?" gets "are we going for Thomson? yes" at 01:15:04 mid-sentence. The promised built price estimate and the layout bonus were both skipped. A webinar that does not deliver its own headline promise cannot ask the room to trust the offer.
3. **45 minutes before the first Thomson number.** Minute 67 of the meeting. The most motivated segment (Thomson buyers) waits 45 minutes for what they came for; the least motivated segment (general learners) gets a seminar.
4. **The pitch is 30 minutes and describes the SERVICE four times instead of selling the SESSION once.** The free strategy session is the only thing on sale tonight. Defect checks, construction photos, key collection and 4-year reviews are what an agent does after a purchase — they dilute the session and answer "are you an agent?" before anyone asked.
5. **Scarcity and guarantee both misfired.** "That's a lot of you — opening from 7 to 10" was said to 16 visible participants; the decision-integrity guarantee was inverted live ("it will not be a no or not-yet situation").

Fix those five and the 12.5% live application rate on attendees should move toward 25–30% — the range this format produces when the content is arranged as an argument and the offer is the obvious next step.

## 3. Block-by-block diagnosis

### A. Open (00:00–00:04) — keep, trim to 2 min
**What you said:** Q&A button instructions, "type yes if you can hear me", "we're going very specific into Thomson Reserve — the numbers, the honest part, the areas where it could be a trap."
**Works:** the promise is specific and honest-toned. "Could be a trap for us as homeowners" is a fear hook.
**Leaks:** two minutes of housekeeping before any stake.
**Say instead (min 0–2):** "Type where you're joining from — I read every name. [read 3–4 names]. In the next 90 minutes I'm going to build Thomson Reserve's fair price live, show you the unit-mix number the developer released this week that changed my own plan, and by the end I'll tell you exactly what I'd do with a $3 million budget — buy, wait, or walk. Q&A button is at the bottom; I answer every question at the end."
**Screen:** camera full, title slide (slide 1) for 20 s only.

### B. Hook (00:04–00:06) — your strongest 2 minutes; make it the Big Domino
**What you said:** "I've just sold one of my properties that gained about $340,000 in under four years, and I'm hunting for another between $3 to $4 million… is Thomson Reserve ideal for us to purchase as the next family property?"
**Works:** authority + skin in the game + the title question in one breath. This is the reason a Thomson buyer stays.
**Leaks:** it is never closed. The audience is told you are deciding; they never hear the decision.
**Say instead (min 2–4):** keep the line, then add the Big Domino: "Here's the one thing tonight rests on. A popular project can be the wrong purchase — and the reason people buy wrong isn't bad information, it's that nobody in that room is paid to tell them to stop. If that's true, then the question isn't 'is Thomson good' — it's 'who is checking my blind spot when the queue number is called'. Everything I show you tonight is me proving that to you with numbers."
**Screen:** slide 20 (THE BIGGEST RISK…) full screen for the domino line, then back to camera. Slide 20 currently appears at minute 38; move it here and bring it back at the transition (min 57) and the close (min 68).

### C. "You are here because" + $1.9M teaser (00:06–00:09) — keep

## 6. Verbatim script bank for the moments that decide the close

**6.1 Big Domino (min 3):**
"A popular project can be the wrong purchase. And the reason people buy wrong isn't bad information — it's that nobody in the room is paid to tell them to stop. Hold that. Everything tonight is me proving it with numbers."

**6.2 Pre-frame (min 4, 30 s):** see block D.

**6.3 The price reveal + $3M verdict (min 40):** see block S.

**6.4 Transition (min 57):** see block X.

**6.5 The offer, named once (min 58):**
"So here's what I'm inviting a small number of you into. It's called the Next Move Strategy Session — one-to-one, with me, 60 to 90 minutes, before the 17 October preview. It is not a class, not a workshop, not a course. It's the Before/During/After you just watched, run on your numbers instead of Thomson Reserve's."

**6.6 What happens in it (min 59):**
"Three things. Review — your objective ranked, your financing structured properly, not just what the bank will lend. Evaluate — the fair-price build on the projects on YOUR shortlist, Thomson or otherwise. Identify — your walk-away price, your ranked stack list, and whether the honest answer is buy, wait, or walk. You leave with those three written down."

**6.7 Who it's for / not for (min 60):**
"It's for you if you're deciding on a purchase or a sale in the next 12 months and you'd rather hear 'not this one' from me now than from the market in five years. It's not for you if you want someone to confirm what you've already decided — I'll waste your time and mine."

**6.8 Proof (min 61, 90 s):** portfolio line (block I) + ONE client story told, with the number (Adler the banker or Paul with three kids — one, not both).

**6.9 Anchor + why free (min 62):**
"Clients pay $1,997 for this depth of work. Tonight it's complimentary — and here's the honest reason: it's how both of us find out if there's a fit. I make money on transactions. That's exactly why the session exists — because if I only got paid when you buy, you should want the person across the table to have shown you, on record, that he tells people not to. You watched that tonight. What's one winning property worth to you — $90k, $250k, $500k? That's the number to weigh a 90-minute session against."

**6.10 The stack (min 63, build line by line on one slide):**
1. Your objective ranked and written — own-stay vs growth decided before the room decides it for you
2. Your fair-price range on your shortlist — Thomson and two alternatives
3. Your walk-away price and ranked stack list for 17 October
4. Financing structured for the next move, not just this one
5. An honest verdict: buy, wait, or walk — in writing
Bonus 1: the full Thomson Reserve layout pack with my notes (already coming to everyone who stayed).
Bonus 2: preview-weekend registration and a walked showflat with us on the day.

**6.11 Guarantee (min 65) — say it exactly:**
"Three promises. I will not tell you to buy because a unit is available. 'No' and 'not yet' are valid outcomes — you heard one tonight. And the final decision is always yours."

**6.12 Scarcity + urgency, real (min 66):**
"I can do [N] of these before the preview weekend — that's the number of evenings between now and the 17th, not a marketing number. Applications close Friday 9pm so I can read them properly. If you apply and I don't think I can help, I'll tell you that too." (Set N honestly — 4 to 6.)

**6.13 CTA + how (min 67):**
"Scan or click the link in the chat. Three steps: your details, six questions about your situation — the more specific, the better I prepare — then pick a slot. Incomplete applications aren't reviewed, not because I'm strict but because a call without your numbers is a waste of your evening."

**6.14 Three closes before Q&A (min 69):**
Logic: "You now know the range, the mix, and the odds. What you don't have is your own numbers against them."
Emotion: "The client with $1.9 million wasn't saved by information. He was saved by someone in the room whose job was to say no."
Urgency: "The preview is the 17th. Selection day is built to make you decide fast. The only thing you control is being decided before you walk in."

**6.15 Q&A open (min 73):**
"QR stays on screen. Ask me anything — the project, the process, your situation. If you've applied, type 'applied' so I can say hi." (Names of appliers read out = social proof for the undecided.)
