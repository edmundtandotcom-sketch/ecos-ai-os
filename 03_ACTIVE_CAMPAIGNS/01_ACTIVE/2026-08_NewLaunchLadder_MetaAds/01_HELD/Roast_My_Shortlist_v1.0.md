# Roast My Shortlist

**Version:** v1.0
**Date:** 2026-08-24
**Status:** HELD — depends on the offer-rename decision (§1 of the consolidation master)

---

## Angle definition

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Audience moment | Has 2–3 launches shortlisted, has been comparing for weeks, cannot decide |
| Hidden cause | Every adviser they've asked said yes to everything, so nothing narrows |
| Belief attacked | "I need more information to decide" |
| Reframe | You don't need another recommendation. You need someone willing to disqualify one. |
| Bridge | The Shortlist Review — walkaway price per project |

---

## Structural dependency

This angle only works if the offer changes:

1. Rename **"Strategy Session"** → **"The Shortlist Review"**.
2. Add a required landing-page field: *"Which project(s) are you considering?"*

That field does four things at once: self-qualifies to active shoppers, kills pitch-resistance (you're reviewing, not selling), hands over the call agenda before the call, and turns every project typed into it into the next NL03 ad. It is the only mechanic in this campaign that gets **stronger** the longer it runs.

**Lead with the negative promise.** "I'll tell you which one to buy" is a sales pitch. "I'll tell you which one I'd walk away from" is a service. The second converts.

## Hooks

**H1 — Send Me Your Shortlist**
> "Send me the three new launches you're deciding between. I'll tell you which one I'd walk away from — and why."

**H2 — The Free Verdict**
> "You've been comparing the same 3 projects for two months. Give me 30 minutes and you'll be comparing one."

**H3 — I'll Talk You Out Of It**
> "Tell me the project you're looking at and I'll try to talk you out of it. If I can't, that's probably the one you should buy."

**H4 — Your Agent Won't Say No**
> "Ask your agent which of your three shortlisted projects you should skip. Watch how fast the answer becomes 'all of them can buy.'"

**H5 — The Two Numbers**
> "Name the project. I'll give you two numbers back: the entry price where it still works, and the price where you walk away."

**H6 — Before Launch Day**
> "Decide on the project now, or decide under pressure on launch day when your first two choices are gone. One of those goes better than the other."

**H7 — The Screenshot**
> "Screenshot the projects sitting in your notes app right now. That's the shortlist. Send it to me and I'll tell you what's actually in it."

**H8 — Say No For Me**
> "Most buyers don't need someone to find them a project. They need someone willing to say no to one."

## Body — "Send Me Your Shortlist"

> You've got two or three new launches you keep going back to.
>
> You've walked the showflats. You've got the floor plans on your phone. You've probably got a spreadsheet.
>
> And you're still stuck.
>
> Not because you don't have information — you have too much. Every project has a brochure that makes it sound like the obvious choice, and every agent you've asked has told you the same thing:
>
> "Confirm can buy."
>
> "Place a cheque first."
>
> "Every new launch also can make money."
>
> Of course they say that. Nobody gets paid to tell you to skip one.
>
> So here's what I'll do instead.
>
> **Send me your shortlist. I'll tell you which one I'd walk away from.**
>
> Not based on the brochure. Based on the four things that actually decide whether you make money:
>
> **Your entry price** — the number above which the deal stops working, no matter how good the project is.
>
> **Your future buyer** — who is buying this specific unit from you in four to six years, and whether there are enough of them at the price you'll need.
>
> **Your exit** — the realistic resale price, against what's launching around you between now and then.
>
> **Your next move** — how much usable equity this releases, and whether that's enough to get you into the property after this one.
>
> Then I'll give you two numbers for each project on your list.
>
> The price where it still makes sense.
>
> And your **walkaway price** — the number where you stand up and leave, decided now, in a quiet room, instead of on launch day when your first two choices are gone and someone is telling you the last available stack is fine.
>
> That's how I've bought my own. Tre Ver at $1.05M on about $292K committed, out at $1.28M — roughly 12.9% a year. Parc Clematis at $1.15M on about $321K committed, out at $1.525M — about $282K net, roughly 22.9% a year on the capital I actually put in.
>
> `[CS-3 — MATCH: someone who came in with a shortlist, was talked OUT of their own top pick, bought the one they'd ranked second or third, and did better. Needs: which project they wanted, why it failed the test, what they bought instead, result. This is the single most important case study in the campaign — it proves saying no is the product.]`
>
> And it's how I've told clients to walk away from projects everyone else was balloting for.
>
> That's the **New Launch Ladder™**. We disqualify most of the list before we ever discuss which unit you like.
>
> So before you place another cheque:
>
> **Click the link below and tell me which projects you're deciding between.**
>
> I'll review your shortlist, tell you which one I'd walk away from, and give you the walkaway price for the ones that survive.
>
> Worst case, you leave with one less project to worry about.
>
> **You are one property away.**
