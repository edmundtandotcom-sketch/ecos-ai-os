## Register conflict — read before using

The Second Property Ladder Canonical Angle Control Register **already contains two angles in this territory**:

- `B05 — The Comfortable Home Trap` — owns inertia caused by present comfort
- `B16 — The Forever-Home Fallacy` — owns over-specification and permanence

Under the governing rule, an angle may not duplicate a registered angle's belief. Before anything here runs, Edmund must rule one of:

1. This is a **new-launch investor** execution of the same belief → it becomes a format beneath B16, not a new angle.
2. It is genuinely distinct (attacks *sequencing* rather than *permanence*) → it earns an `NL` ID.
3. It is folded into B16 as a variation.

**This is a possible partial explanation for why the angle previously underperformed**: it may have been run against an audience already covered by other registered angles, or without the register's non-duplication discipline applied.

---

## Diagnosis — why it lost to NL05 / NL06

Not the angle. The execution. Four errors:

**1. It attacks them; NL05 and NL06 attack someone else.**
NL05 lets them blame hype, agents and ballot luck. NL06 lets them blame the crowd. Forever-home says *your decision was wrong* — about their **home**. That is an identity defence, not a marketing objection. Defended people do not click.

**2. It triggers "so where do I live?" and never answers it.**
The killer. The moment that thought fires, they are gone. NL05 and NL06 have no equivalent unanswered objection.

**3. No deadline.** "Don't stay in it" is a philosophy. NL05 has a ballot season; NL06 has launch day.

**4. "You'll make more" is a claim without a number.** The gut counters with "but I need somewhere to live," and the gut wins any unquantified argument.

**The fix:** stop telling them not to live somewhere. Tell them the forever home is **move #4, not move #1**, and put a dollar figure on what waiting costs. Same idea, no insult, aspirational instead of restrictive.

**H1 — Move #4**
> "Your forever home is not your next property. It's your fourth one."
> Because if you buy it now, you're buying the best home your current money can afford — instead of the best home your money could have become.

> "The moment you buy your forever home, you've decided the maximum home you'll ever own."
> At 38, with the capital you have today. That's a big decision to make this early.

**H3 — Not Never. Not Yet.**
> "I'm not telling you never to buy your dream home. I'm telling you not to buy it third."

**H4 — The Nicer House**
> "There's a version of your dream home that's 40% bigger, and you're currently 6 years away from it. Most people buy the smaller one now."

## Entry point 2 — One property, two jobs

**H5 — Two Jobs**
> "A home has one job: be a nice place to live. An investment has one job: grow. Ask one property to do both and it usually does neither well."

**H6 — Which One Do You Love?**
> "Which property do you love, and which property pays you? If the answer is the same property, one of those two is a lie you're telling yourself."
