## 0. Standing workflow — before scripting any video
1. Scan real trending/market data (search volume, competitor breakouts, current SG property news) for a live angle.
2. **Verify against primary sources, not secondary aggregator summaries.** For regulatory/policy changes specifically: find the actual government press release (MND/HDB/IRAS/URA) or a professional research house's analysis that cites it directly (e.g. PropNex, a bank's research desk) — not a blog or news-aggregator paraphrase, which can flatten or misstate tiered rules, thresholds, and effective dates. If Edmund has supplied a source document, treat it as the ground truth and cross-check any web-search claims against it, not the other way round. **Make the sourcing visible to the viewer, not just true in the background** — name the source on screen or verbally for hard numbers ("according to MND's press release," "URA caveat data shows"), and where two independent sources land on the same figure, say so — that's a real credibility signal, not a nice-to-have. A number that's accurate but silently sourced does less trust-building work than one the viewer can see was checked.
3. Check the verified facts against the locked blueprint (villain, audience, category, mechanism) — use the angle only where it genuinely fits; don't force it, and say so plainly if the fit is narrow rather than overstating it.
4. Derive the teaching modules fresh from what the verified research actually shows, per §2 — never reuse a prior video's specific module set.
5. If the video features a real project, pull verified current data (developer, land cost, units, pricing, timeline) — never invent numbers.
6. Build the script on the framework below.
7. Self-grade against the video's 5 most relevant criteria, explicitly including factual accuracy against the primary source; revise before finalizing.
8. Check the finished script against §8 (The Depth Ceiling) before finalizing — every video needs the credibility layer (§0.2, §6a, §3a) without crossing into a DIY manual.

## 1. The core skeleton (one framework, flexes by pillar and by depth — see §3)

| Section | What happens | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Promise match (0:00–0:05) | Deliver exactly what title+thumbnail promised, immediately | Documented MrBeast 5-second rule |
| Hook + open loop (0:05–0:25) | Concrete, stakes-bearing claim + an unresolved loop | Open-loop research: 32% watch-time lift |
| Context bridge (0:25–1:00ish) | Why this matters, brief, not a monologue | Standard retention-architecture research |
| Core value — numbered teaching modules | Each opens with a progress marker, teaches a REAL transferable method (not just facts about one example), closes on a mini-payoff | Ali Abdaal's Value block; see §2 on teaching vs. informing |
| Pattern interrupt every 30-45s throughout | Cut / background change / on-screen text callout | Aggregated retention research |
| Midpoint re-hook (~40-50% mark) | Explicitly re-open a loop | MrBeast's documented 3rd-minute re-engagement beat |
| Worked example | Apply the taught method(s) to a real/anonymized case, thriller-paced (setback → reveal) | Mark Rober's narrative-investment principle |
| Recap | Close every loop opened earlier, by name | Open-loop research's own rule: only open what you close |
| Soft bridge / CTA | Personalization gap (their specific numbers), not a hard pitch | Established teach-vs-ad correction |

**Micro-CTAs and callouts, threaded through, not just at the end:** one mid-video comment-bait line per video; on-screen text callout at every progress marker and at the named-rule/villain name-drop; pinned comment carries the actual link.

## 2. Teach a transferable method, not just information about one example
The single biggest quality bar: does the viewer walk away with something they can apply to a DIFFERENT project or situation next month, or only with an opinion about the one thing this video covered? Every core teaching module should produce a **named, reusable rule**.

**Critical — do not skip this, found as a live mistake 2026-07-30:** the modules and their names must be DERIVED FRESH every time from two things — (a) what real research actually shows is notable/true/useful about THIS specific topic, and (b) which parts of the blueprint (audience, villain, Move options) genuinely connect to it, without forcing a connection that isn't really there. "The Headroom Test / Land-Cost Doubling Rule / 3-Signal Job Test" (Ownership → Loan Runway → Purpose) was the right set of three for a new-launch project specifically — it is NOT a universal template. Reusing those same three slots for a topic that isn't about a project (a regulatory change, a market-condition story, an en bloc development) produces recycled, hollow modules that only wear new names. Before building any script, ask: if I stripped away the labels, would these teaching points still be specific to this topic, or could I paste them into any other video unchanged? If the latter, they haven't actually been derived — start over from the real research.

The number of modules is not fixed at three either — use however many the topic genuinely supports (could be two, could be five), per the depth-scaling guidance in §3.

## 3. Dynamic depth — how to scale from ~10 min to 15-30 min without padding
Length is earned by real teaching depth, never assumed. The skeleton above is fixed; what flexes is how many teaching modules/named rules it carries and how much the worked example expands:
- **~10-12 min**: 3 teaching modules/rules, one worked example — standard single-topic or single-project video
- **~15-20 min**: 4-5 modules, OR 3 modules applied across 2 comparable projects/cases (contrast strengthens teaching, isn't padding), plus an expanded worked example with more than one buyer-type verdict
- **~20-30 min**: reserve for genuinely deep topics — a multi-project comparison, a "state of the market" style breakdown, or a topic with real regulatory/financial complexity (e.g., a full walk-through of ABSD + TDSR + decoupling together) — never stretch a 10-minute topic to 25 minutes by re-explaining the same point
- **Rule of thumb**: if trimming a section changes nothing about what the viewer can now do themselves, it was padding, not depth. Add another real module/comparison instead of restating the current one.
- Extending length is only advisable once the channel's actual YouTube Studio retention graph shows viewers are staying through the current shorter format — see §5.

## 3a. Comparison as a teaching device
Comparing two or more named, real things — projects, districts, a policy's before/after, two strategies applied to the same buyer profile — is one of the strongest credibility and retention tools available, not just a length-extension option (§3 already allows it at the 15-20 min tier; this is the how). Rules:
- **Compare on a small number of buyer-relevant dimensions, named explicitly** — e.g. price/psf, size, location/MRT proximity, TOP/launch timeline, developer track record — not an exhaustive spec-sheet dump. Pick the 3-5 dimensions that actually drive the decision this video is about.
- **The comparison must resolve into a nuanced, situation-dependent verdict, never a flat ranking.** "Project A wins" is a buying guide — it invites the viewer to stop watching and go decide. "Project A fits a DINK couple prioritizing rental yield; Project B fits a family prioritizing school proximity, even though A is cheaper per square foot" is teaching plus authority, and it still leaves the viewer's own situation as the open question. This is the same discipline as §6's nuanced verdict rule, applied to comparison specifically.
- Real numbers only, verified per §0.2, sourced visibly.

## 4. Interactive question beats — keeping it multi-dimensional, not a lecture
Thread genuine questions through the video at these points, not just the closing CTA:
- **One direct self-check question per teaching module** — spoken to camera, e.g. "Ask yourself right now: does your current loan already sit near that 55% ceiling?" — makes the viewer participate mentally instead of passively absorbing facts.
- **One mid-video engagement/comment-bait question** — e.g. "Comment HEADROOM if you want the checklist for this" — drives a real algorithm signal (comments), separate from the final CTA.
- **One rhetorical stakes question at the midpoint re-hook** — reopens attention exactly where natural drop-off happens.
- **Optional: a short audience-Q&A beat** near the recap for videos running 15+ minutes — pull 1-2 real viewer questions from comments on a prior video and answer them in 30-45 seconds each; this both rewards engagement and adds genuine multi-voice texture instead of one person talking the whole time.

## 5. Why this stays "long-lasting" — the self-correcting loop
After each video publishes, pull the actual YouTube Studio retention graph. Wherever retention drops sharply, that timestamp is what gets fixed in the next episode's equivalent section — same discipline MrBeast's team runs. Extend runtime (per §3) only once retention data supports it. This framework should sharpen every 2-3 episodes, not stay frozen as first written — if you upgrade it, upgrade THIS file; the skill that generates scripts reads it fresh each time, so upgrades take effect on the next video automatically, no re-prompting needed.

## 6. Project integration (real new-launch projects, throughout the video)
Use a real, currently-relevant project as the vehicle for the worked example — never a bolt-on plug at the end. Retitle as "I Ran [Named Rule/Framework] on [Project] — Here's Who It Actually Fits." Weave the project's real, verified numbers into each teaching module. Land a nuanced, buyer-type-dependent verdict (passes for X, fails for Y) rather than a blanket recommendation — the nuance is what keeps it from reading as an ad. **Disclosure is non-negotiable**: any referral/commission/agency relationship to a featured project needs an explicit stated disclosure line, every time.

## 6a. Named-project data checklist
Any real project referenced — whether it's the main worked example or a comparator dropped in for a policy/market video that isn't project-centric — should carry, wherever available and verified: **name, location/district, developer, unit count and size range, price/psf and quantum range, launch or TOP date.** Half-remembered or approximate figures read as filler and cost credibility; a specific, sourced number is what makes a video feel authoritative instead of generic. Never invent a figure to fill a gap — state plainly that a number isn't available yet rather than estimate without labeling it as an estimate (per §0.5).

## 7. Scope
This framework applies to any topic, trending news item, or project for the Second Property Ladder series — not just the Amberwood at Holland example it was built against. Amberwood was the worked demonstration; every future long-form video should be built by running the standing workflow (§0) against this framework, whatever the specific topic turns out to be.

## 8. The Depth Ceiling — insight and authority, not a DIY manual
Real data, named projects, and honest comparisons (§0.2, §3a, §6a) exist to make a video feel genuinely valuable — information alone doesn't earn attention or trust, and a video that's just facts recited at the viewer won't convert. But there is a governing ceiling, and it isn't new to this framework: it's blueprint §12's "Market the destination. Sell the plan. Deliver the depth," and it's §2's existing rule that a module teaches a transferable *method*, not a finished personalized answer.

Concretely: teach the real criteria, the real framework, the real way to evaluate a decision — that's what makes the video worth 12+ minutes of someone's time and what proves REI Method actually knows this market. Stop short of running the viewer's own numbers through that framework for them. Their specific ownership structure, financing position, spouse alignment, and goals are the genuine unknowns a consult exists to resolve — that gap is real, not manufactured, and it's precisely why "watch the video and now you can do it yourself" isn't actually true even when the video is excellent. The test before publishing: does this video teach someone how to *evaluate* correctly, or does it hand them a completed answer that removes their reason to book a session? The first is the goal on every video. The second is a giveaway, not content.
