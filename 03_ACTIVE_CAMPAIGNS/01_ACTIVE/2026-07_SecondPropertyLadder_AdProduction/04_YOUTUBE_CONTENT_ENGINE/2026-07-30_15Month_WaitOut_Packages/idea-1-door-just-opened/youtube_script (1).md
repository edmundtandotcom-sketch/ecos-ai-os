## 4:30–8:30 — RULE 1: THE EXPIRED-RULEBOOK RULE

`[DELIVER: Teaching rhythm. The "three years and ten months" number should sound almost surprising — say it like it surprised you too.]`

`[SCREEN: "Rule 1 — The Expired-Rulebook Rule"]`

Rule one. In one line: never plan a property move on a rulebook older than the last policy cycle. Plan on today's rules — or don't plan.

Here's what just happened, stripped of the drama. A rule that felt permanent lasted three years and ten months. In September 2022, out again in July 2026.

`[SCREEN: "A 'permanent' rule lasted 3 years, 10 months"] [CITE: Article, EdgeProp Singapore, https://www.edgeprop.sg/property-news/government-removes-15-month-wait-out-period-private-property-owners-buy-hdb-resale-flats, accessed 2026-07-30]`

`[DELIVER: Reframe — this is the emotional core of the rule. Direct, a little bit gentle.]`

Think about what that means for a second. If back in 2023 you sat down with your spouse, looked at that fifteen-month wait, and quietly concluded "a move like that isn't realistic for us — let's just stay put"... your conclusion was right then. And it's wrong now. Nothing about your money changed. The rulebook moved underneath you. And if you didn't notice, you're still living by a rule that doesn't exist anymore.

That's the quiet mechanism behind staying stuck at one property. People assume it's laziness, or fear. Usually it's neither. It's a sensible decision made under old rules that nobody ever went back and re-checked. You didn't choose to stay stuck. You just never re-opened the file.

`[DELIVER: The habit — make it doable.]`

So the habit this gives you: once a year, and every single time you see a cooling-measure headline, you re-run your next move against *today's* rulebook. Not to act on the news. Just to check whether a door you'd assumed was locked has quietly opened. This week is the loudest possible proof that they do.

## 8:30–13:00 — RULE 2: THE TRIGGER-NOT-TARGET RULE

`[DELIVER: A bit more energy — this is a "let's sort it live" module. Make the three boxes feel like a tool they can hold. Then get very straight on the honest sorting.]`

`[SCREEN: "Rule 2 — The Trigger-Not-Target Rule"]`

Rule two. A headline is only useful to you if it changes *your* position. So before you feel a single thing about any property news, you sort it into one of three boxes.

`[SCREEN: three boxes build as he names them]`

Box one — it moves *you*. It changes an option you personally have. This is the only box you act on, and only after checking your numbers. Box two — it moves the *market*. It shifts prices or sentiment broadly, but your specific options didn't change. That's context, not instruction. Box three — it moves *nobody you know*. Noise dressed up as news.

`[DELIVER: Now run this week through it, live. Get straighter than the headlines were.]`

So let's actually sort this week's news. Does the removal change an option *you* have? Here's the honest version. The wait-out came off only for private owners buying a non-subsidised resale flat *without* an HDB loan. Need the HDB loan — still fifteen months. Eyeing a BTO — still thirty. And either way, sell the private property within six months.

`[CITE: Article, HDB (official), https://www.hdb.gov.sg/hdb-pulse/news/2026/removal-of-the-15-month-wait-out-period-for-private-residential-property-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]`

So this is a Box-one event — genuinely moves you — for a narrow profile: a financially-ready owner who can buy that resale flat in cash-and-CPF without an HDB loan, and is willing to let the private property go. For a lot of people who cheered this week, it's honestly Box two. Interesting. Not theirs.

`[DELIVER: The data-as-education beat — walk the analyst split, don't just cite it.]`

`[DELIVER: 3-questions beat.]`

Is it easy? Very — it's one question before you let your heart rate move. "Does this change an option I actually have?"

Can you do it? You just did, on the biggest headline of the week. If your honest answer was "no, my option didn't change," that's not disappointing — that's you not getting played by a Box-two story.

Will it work for you? It works precisely *because* it forces the news to be about you or admit that it isn't. Either answer saves you money.

## 14:00–18:30 — RULE 3: THE SEQUENCE-BEFORE-SIGNATURE RULE

`[DELIVER: This is the sharpest teaching in the video. State the rule, then reveal the six-month clock as the thing that flips the whole plan. Slow on "you are not adding a flat — you are replacing one home with another on a timer."]`

`[SCREEN: "Rule 3 — The Sequence-Before-Signature Rule"]`

Rule three. The feasibility of a property move is set by its *sequencing* — the order and the timing — not by the asking price. Map the sequence before you fall in love with the destination.

Most people evaluate a move by asking "can I afford it?" Wrong first question. The right one is "in what *order* do the pieces have to happen, and does that order actually work with my life?"

Look at this move specifically. Even with the wait gone, the sequence has hard edges. There's a loan fork — buy without an HDB loan and you move now; need the loan and you're back to fifteen months. That one fork decides whether this is instant or a year away.

`[CITE: Article, 99.co, https://www.99.co/singapore/insider/15-month-period-removed-for-private-property-owners-buying-hdb-resale/, accessed 2026-07-30]`

And then the clock. You must sell your private property within *six months* of completing the resale purchase.

`[SCREEN: "6-month clock: sell your private property after you buy the flat"] [CITE: Article, HDB (official), https://www.hdb.gov.sg/hdb-pulse/news/2026/removal-of-the-15-month-wait-out-period-for-private-residential-property-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]`

`[DELIVER: The reframe — this is the "aha". Land it slowly.]`

Read that clock closely, because it quietly reverses the whole plan. You're not "keeping your private place and adding a flat." You're *replacing* one with the other, on a six-month timer. Six months to sell a private property — in a market where the HDB resale index actually slipped, down a touch in each of the first two quarters of this year.

`[CITE: Article, The Edge Singapore, https://www.theedgesingapore.com/cityandcountry/residential/analysts-cheer-removal-15-month-wait-out-period-private-home-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]`

Sell too slow, you're forced into a weak price. Sell too fast without your next roof sorted, you've got a gap. The price of the flat was never the hard part. The order and the timer are.

`[DELIVER: The habit — make it visual.]`

So the habit: before you sign anything, draw the sequence as a line. What sells, what buys, what gets disposed, and by when. Then stress-test the tightest date. If the sequence survives, the move is real. If the timer breaks, no price makes it work.

`[DELIVER: 3-questions beat — be honest that this one takes effort.]`

Is it easy? The drawing is easy — it's a line on paper. Being honest about the tightest date is the hard part, because that's the bit people wave away.

Can you do it yourself? The map, yes. Where a second set of eyes earns its keep is spotting the date *you* are too optimistic about — usually how fast the private place really sells.

Will it work for your situation? This is the check that saves people the most money, because it catches the move that looks affordable and is actually un-sequenceable. If your timer holds, you can move with real confidence.

## 18:30–22:30 — RULE 4: THE TWO-NAMES RULE

`[DELIVER: Slightly quieter, "let me tell you the thing couples miss" energy. This is where you protect the Depth Ceiling — teach the lever, refuse to quote personal numbers, and say why plainly.]`

`[SCREEN: "Rule 4 — The Two-Names Rule"]`

Rule four, and this is the one couples and dual-income owners consistently underrate. For a couple, which name holds which asset sets the *ceiling* on your future options. Positioning comes before purchasing.

In Singapore a married couple isn't one buyer — you're potentially two positions. How your current property is held, whose name it's in, who gets "counted" against a future purchase — that quietly decides how many doors stay open to you later, and what each one costs in stamp duty and financing.

`[DELIVER: The mini-example — make it concrete and a little bit stinging.]`

Two couples. Identical income, identical savings. Couple A holds everything jointly and never gave it a thought. Couple B structured their names deliberately, years ago. A door opens — a policy change, exactly like this week — and Couple B can move on it. Couple A discovers their own structure is the thing standing in their way. Same money. Different ceiling. The only difference was positioning done *before* it was needed.

`[DELIVER: The Depth-Ceiling honesty beat — this is on-brand and genuinely the right thing to say.]`

Now — I'm deliberately not going to throw ABSD percentages and loan ratios at you here. And I want to be honest about why. Whether a two-name move helps you or hurts you depends entirely on your current holdings, your CPF, your income split, your timeline. Anyone quoting you a one-size-fits-all number on YouTube is selling you certainty they don't have. The method is universal. The numbers are personal — and yours are different from that couple's.

So the habit: before you plan *what* to buy next, get clear on *how you're currently positioned* — because positioning is the lever, and it's almost always the cheapest one to pull early and the most expensive one to fix late.

`[DELIVER: Pull the four together — see the whole machine.]`

And look at what these four do together. Rule one made sure you're planning on today's rules. Rule two made sure the news is actually about you. Rule three made sure the move can physically be sequenced. Rule four made sure your structure is a launchpad, not a ceiling. Not one of them needed a price forecast. That's the point.

`[DELIVER: 3-questions beat.]`

Is it easy? The awareness is easy and free. Getting the structure genuinely optimal takes planning — but even knowing it's a lever puts you ahead of most owners.

Can you do it? You can start today by simply knowing how you're held and whose name carries what. That alone changes decisions.

Will it work for you? This is the lever with the longest reach — it decides not one move but every move after it. Which is exactly why you look at it before you need it, not after.
