## LinkedIn (professional, insight-led, first-person authority)

Every property policy change in Singapore produces the same reflex: a scramble to predict prices.

On 28 July, the 15-month wait-out period for private owners buying an HDB resale flat without an HDB loan was removed [Source: HDB official, 2026-07-30]. Coverage split almost immediately into "prices will jump" vs "prices will stay flat," with analysts themselves divided [Source: The Edge Singapore, 2026-07-30].

I want to make a contrarian case: for a financially-ready owner, "will resale prices jump?" is not a hard question — it's the wrong question.

A price forecast is a description of the market. It contains no information about your timeline, your financing, or your life-stage — and those are the only variables that determine whether a move is right for you. A correct forecast can still lead you into a trapped position if your runway is thin and your exits are closed. A wrong forecast is survivable if you built the move to survive being wrong.

So I teach a four-part decision method that never requires a forecast:

1. The Forecast Filter — a prediction only counts if it changes your timeline, financing, or life-stage.
2. The Runway Test — measure the months you can hold a move under a stressed rate and an income gap, not the market's direction.
3. The Two-Clock Rule — your MOP, ABSD window and life-stage carry real dates; the market's "right time" does not. Plan to the clock you control.
4. The Reversibility Check — every move earns a safe exit before you commit.

The throughline: stop outsourcing a million-dollar decision to a prediction no one can stand behind. Know your position, decide the move, own the outcome.
