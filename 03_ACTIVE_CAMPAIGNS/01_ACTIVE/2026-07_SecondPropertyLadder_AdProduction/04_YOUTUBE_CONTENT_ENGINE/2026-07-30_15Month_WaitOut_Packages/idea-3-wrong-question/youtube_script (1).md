## 11:00–16:30 — CHECK 2: THE RUNWAY TEST (the number I promised)

`[DELIVER: This is the centrepiece. Slow the open right down — you're about to deliver on the promise from 0:20. Build it. Then teach the method carefully, and be honest about the Depth Ceiling — you teach the method, you don't do their sums.]`

`[SCREEN: "Check 2 — The Runway Test"]`

Okay. The number I promised you at the start. The one that isn't on anybody's forecast slide, because it was never the market's number — it's yours.

Check two. The Runway Test. Stop measuring which way the market's going. Measure your runway — the number of months you could comfortably carry your next move if things went a bit wrong. Not catastrophically wrong. Just realistically-bad wrong.

Here's how you build it — and I'm going to teach you the method, not do your sums, because your actual sums are the thing we sit down and protect properly in a session. But the method is yours to keep.

`[DELIVER: Whiteboard this. Three moves. Deliberate.]`

Take the move you're considering. Now the trick most people get wrong: don't model it at today's best-case. Don't use today's friendliest interest rate and assume everything goes smoothly. Model it *worse* than today. A higher rate than you're seeing now. A gap in income or rental. Then ask one question: how many months can I carry this — mortgage, cash flow, life — before something has to give? Before I'd be forced to sell, or forced to borrow, or forced to change my life?

That number of months — that's your runway.

`[SCREEN: "Runway = months you can carry the move under a realistically-bad scenario"]`

`[DELIVER: The "why this beats a forecast" turn — this is the intellectual payoff. Calm conviction.]`

And here's why this quietly beats every price forecast on the internet. A forecast tells you what *might* happen to the market. Your runway tells you what happens to *you* if the forecast is wrong. And you can't control the market — but you can absolutely control your runway. You can size the move, hold more buffer, pick a safer structure.

Watch how it dissolves this week's whole argument. Two owners looking at the same move. One has twenty-four months of runway. One has three. Prices surge? They both do fine. Prices keep cooling? The twenty-four-month owner is completely fine and the three-month owner is in trouble. Notice — I never had to know which way prices went. The runway made the forecast irrelevant. The three-month owner isn't making a move, they're making a bet, and they're betting on a prediction nobody can actually give them.

`[DELIVER: 3-questions beat — but be more honest here, because this one takes a bit of effort. Don't oversell "easy".]`

So — the three things you're thinking.

Is it easy? Honestly, this one takes a bit more than ten seconds. You need your real numbers and you need the discipline to model the bad case instead of the pretty one. But it's arithmetic, not magic. If you can fill in a mortgage calculator, you can build a runway — you're just feeding it uglier inputs on purpose.

Can you do it yourself? The method, yes, completely. Where people slip is being too kind to themselves on the inputs — using today's low rate, assuming no gaps. The honest version is uncomfortable, and that discomfort is exactly the point. That's also the part where a second set of eyes earns its keep.

Will it work for your situation? This is the one check that works *because* it's built around your situation and nobody else's. Whatever the market does, a move that survives your bad case is a move you can stand behind. That's not a market bet. That's a plan.

## 17:30–21:30 — CHECK 3: THE TWO-CLOCK RULE

`[DELIVER: Teacher mode, but with feeling on the "three years" line — you've genuinely seen people lose time this way. That one should sound like it costs you something to say.]`

`[SCREEN: "Check 3 — The Two-Clock Rule"]`

Check three. The Two-Clock Rule. There are always two clocks running on a property move. The market's clock — which you cannot read, no matter how many videos you watch. And your clock — which you can read exactly. When they disagree, you plan to the one you can actually see.

Your clock is made of things that have real dates on them. When does your MOP end? When does an ABSD-relevant window open or close for how you'd structure this? Where are you in life — is there an age, a financing runway, a family event you want this done before? Those have *dates*. Real ones you could write on a calendar. The market's clock — "when will prices move" — has no date. Nobody can hand you one honestly.

`[DELIVER: Straight to camera, direct question, then the gut-punch line slower.]`

So ask yourself, right now: are you timing your next move to *your* calendar — or to a market calendar that doesn't exist?

Because I meet owners who've been "waiting for the right time" for three years. And when I ask them what event they're actually waiting for — what has to happen — they can't name it. That's not patience. That's the market's imaginary clock quietly eating years off your real one. Three years you don't get back.

`[DELIVER: Now tie it hard to THIS topic — the private-vs-HDB distinction from earlier. This is the callback that pays off the setup at 4:30.]`

And this is exactly why I told you to hold on to who this rule is for. Remember — the wait-out removal is for people moving *out of* private and *into* a flat. So which owner are you?

If you're planning to sell your condo and move into a resale flat — maybe to unlock cash, maybe to right-size for retirement — then this rule change is directly your move, and your clock just got a lot more freedom. You're no longer stuck waiting fifteen months.

But if your plan is to *add* a second property — keep what you have and build — then here's the honest truth most channels won't tell you this week: this specific rule change might not be your move at all. And that's completely fine. The Two-Clock Rule still runs exactly the same. The headline is just the market's clock making noise. Your MOP, your ABSD window, your life stage — that's still the clock you plan to.

`[DELIVER: 3-questions beat, quick and confident now — the viewer's warmed up.]`

Is it easy? Very. You're just writing down the dates you already have and refusing to invent one for the market.

Can you do it? Tonight. Pull up your own timeline and mark the real dates. If the market's date is blank — and it always is — you've got your answer about which clock to trust.

Will it work for you? It works differently depending on which owner you are — and that's the point. It tells you whether this week's news is your green light or just background noise. Either answer is useful. Both beat guessing.

## 21:30–24:30 — CHECK 4: THE REVERSIBILITY CHECK

`[DELIVER: This is the "keeps you safe" check — protective, almost fatherly. Lower, steadier. The tone is: I want you to win, and I want you to still be standing if you don't.]`

`[SCREEN: "Check 4 — The Reversibility Check"]`

Check four. And this is the one that keeps you safe when — not if — you turn out to be a little bit wrong. Because you will be, sometimes. Everyone is.

The Reversibility Check. Before any move, you ask: if this goes against me, what's my way out? A move you can undo safely beats a "perfect" move you'd be trapped inside.

So for whatever you're weighing — buying that resale flat now, or holding, or adding — you ask: if prices or my circumstances turn the wrong way, can I hold? Can I rent it out? Can I sell without taking a forced loss? Can I restructure? A move with two or three clean exits is a move you can make *without* needing the forecast to be right. And a move with no exit is one you don't make — no matter how confident that surge prediction sounds — precisely because no forecast is reliable enough to trap yourself on.

`[DELIVER: Pull the four together — this is the "see the whole machine" moment. Build it, one hand per check, then land it.]`

And look at what these four do together, because this is the whole design. The Forecast Filter took the prediction off the table. The Runway Test replaced it with your holding power. The Two-Clock Rule replaced market timing with your timing. And the Reversibility Check made sure that even if you're wrong, you're not stuck. Not one of them needed you to know where prices go. That's not an accident. That's the point — a way to make a million-dollar decision that doesn't rest on a guess.

`[DELIVER: 3-questions beat.]`

Is it easy? The question is easy — "what's my exit?" Making sure the answer is a *good* one takes a little structuring, but even asking it out loud will stop most of the worst moves.

Can you do it? You can ask it on every single decision starting today. Getting the exits genuinely clean is where planning helps — but the habit is free.

Will it work for you? This is the one that works hardest exactly when you're wrong — which, let's be honest, is when you most need it to.
