## 1:30–4:00 — MODULE 1: THE FORECAST FILTER

**[ON-SCREEN: "Rule 1 — The Forecast Filter"]**

Rule one. The Forecast Filter. Here it is in one line: **a forecast is only useful if it changes something you can actually do. If it doesn't touch your timeline, your financing, or your life-stage, it is weather — not a decision.**

Let me show you how to run it. Take any prediction you heard this week — "resale prices will re-accelerate," "resale prices will stay flat." Ask three questions. Does knowing this change *when* I can move? Does it change *how much I can borrow or afford*? Does it change *what stage of life this move is for*? If the honest answer to all three is no — and for a market-wide forecast it almost always is no — then that forecast has zero authority over your decision. You are allowed to put it down.

**[PATTERN INTERRUPT — mini-example]**

Here's why this rule earns its keep this week specifically. The analysts are genuinely split on whether removing the wait-out re-accelerates prices [Source: Article, The Edge Singapore, https://www.theedgesingapore.com/cityandcountry/residential/analysts-cheer-removal-15-month-wait-out-period-private-home-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]. Split. That means if you were waiting for the experts to agree before you moved, you are waiting for something that does not exist. When smart people disagree on a forecast, that's your signal that the forecast is not where your answer lives.

**[PATTERN INTERRUPT — tone shift, quieter]**

And I want to be straight with you, because "hard truth, soft heart" is the whole job here: **I cannot tell you where prices are going. Nobody can. Not the analysts, not the channels that posted this week, not me.** Anyone who promises you the direction of the market is selling you certainty they do not own. What I *can* do is teach you to make a move that holds up whether prices rise, fall, or go sideways. That's the entire point of the Filter — it stops you outsourcing your million-dollar decision to a prediction nobody can back.

So Rule 1, close the loop on it in your head: run every forecast through the Filter. If it doesn't move your timeline, your financing, or your life-stage — set it down and keep walking.

## 4:00–6:30 — MODULE 2: THE RUNWAY TEST

**[ON-SCREEN: "Rule 2 — The Runway Test"]**

Now the number I promised you at the very start. The one that's not on any forecast slide.

Rule two. The Runway Test. **Stop measuring the market's direction. Measure your runway — the number of months you can comfortably carry your next move under a bad, but realistic, scenario.**

Here's how you build it, and I'm going to teach you the method, not do your sums for you — because your sums are exactly what we protect in a proper session. You take the move you're considering. You don't model it at today's best-case interest rate and full occupancy. You model it at a *worse* rate than today, with a gap in rental or income, and you ask one question: **how many months can I hold this without stress before something has to give?** That number of months is your runway.

**[PATTERN INTERRUPT — reframe]**

Why does this beat a price forecast every time? Because a forecast tells you what *might* happen to the market. Your runway tells you what happens to *you* if the forecast is wrong. And you can control your runway. You cannot control the market. This week's data makes the point — HDB resale price *growth* has cooled hard, from 10.4% in 2022 down to 2.9% in 2025, with the resale price index actually dipping slightly quarter-on-quarter in the first two quarters of this year [Source: Article, EdgeProp Singapore, https://www.edgeprop.sg/property-news/government-removes-15-month-wait-out-period-private-property-owners-buy-hdb-resale-flats, accessed 2026-07-30]. Now — is that the start of a rebound because the wait-out is gone, or a market that keeps cooling? The analysts don't agree [Source: Article, The Edge Singapore, https://www.theedgesingapore.com/cityandcountry/residential/analysts-cheer-removal-15-month-wait-out-period-private-home-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]. But watch this: your runway makes that argument irrelevant to your decision. A move with twenty-four months of runway survives either outcome. A move with three months of runway is a gamble on the forecast being right — and you already know nobody can promise you that.

So Rule 2 loop, closed: the number that decides your move is your runway, in months, under stress. Not the market's direction. Yours.

## 7:15–9:15 — MODULE 3: THE TWO-CLOCK RULE

**[ON-SCREEN: "Rule 3 — The Two-Clock Rule"]**

Rule three. The Two-Clock Rule. **There are always two clocks running on a property move: the market's clock, which you cannot read, and your clock, which you can. When they conflict, plan to the clock you control.**

Your clock is made of things that are actually knowable. When does your MOP end? When does an ABSD-relevant window open or close for your structuring? Where are you in your life-stage — is this a move you want done before a certain age, before a financing runway shortens, before a life event? Those have *dates*. Real ones. The market's clock — "when will prices move" — has no date anyone can give you honestly.

**[PATTERN INTERRUPT — question to camera]**

So ask yourself right now: are you timing your next move to *your* calendar, or to a market calendar that doesn't exist? Because I meet so many owners who've been "waiting for the right time" for three years — and when I ask them what event they're waiting for, they can't name one. That's not patience. That's the market's imaginary clock quietly eating years off your real one.

And this is exactly why I flagged that detail earlier — that this week's rule is for people moving *from* private *into* HDB, disposing of their private home in the process [Source: Article, HDB (official), https://www.hdb.gov.sg/hdb-pulse/news/2026/removal-of-the-15-month-wait-out-period-for-private-residential-property-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]. If your plan is to *add* a second property rather than swap down into a flat, then this specific rule change may not even be your move — and that's fine. The Two-Clock Rule still runs identically. The headline is the market's clock making noise. Your clock — your MOP, your ABSD window, your life-stage — is still the one you plan to.

Rule 3, loop closed: two clocks, plan to yours.

## 9:15–10:45 — MODULE 4: THE REVERSIBILITY CHECK

**[ON-SCREEN: "Rule 4 — The Reversibility Check"]**

Rule four, and this is the one that keeps you safe when — not if — you turn out to be a little bit wrong. The Reversibility Check. **Before any next move, ask: if this goes against me, what is my exit? A move you can undo safely beats a "perfect" move you'd be trapped inside.**

This is the Exit-Safe idea, applied to the headline. Every property decision should leave you a way out. So for the move you're weighing, you ask: if prices or my circumstances move the wrong way, can I hold, can I rent, can I sell without a forced loss, can I restructure? A move that has two or three safe exits is a move you can make *without* needing the forecast to be right. And a move with no exit is one you should not make no matter *how* confident the forecast sounds — precisely because no forecast can be trusted to be right.

**[PATTERN INTERRUPT — reframe]**

Do you see what these four rules have done together? The Forecast Filter took the prediction off the table. The Runway Test replaced it with your holding power. The Two-Clock Rule replaced market timing with your timing. And the Reversibility Check made sure that even if you're wrong, you're not trapped. Not one of them required you to know where prices go. That's the whole design.

Rule 4, closed: every move gets an exit before you make it.
