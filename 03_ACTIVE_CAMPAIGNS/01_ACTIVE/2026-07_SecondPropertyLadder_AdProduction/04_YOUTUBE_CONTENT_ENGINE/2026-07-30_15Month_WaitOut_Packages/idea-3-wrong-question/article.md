# "Will Resale Prices Jump Now?" Is the Wrong Question

*By Coach Edmund Tan · 30 July 2026*

There is a particular kind of noise that follows every property policy change in Singapore, and this week it arrived right on schedule. On 28 July 2026, the government removed the fifteen-month wait-out period for private property owners buying an HDB resale flat without an HDB housing loan [Source: Article, HDB (official), https://www.hdb.gov.sg/hdb-pulse/news/2026/removal-of-the-15-month-wait-out-period-for-private-residential-property-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]. Within two days, my feed — and probably yours — filled up with the same question, asked in a dozen thumbnails and headlines: *will HDB resale prices jump now?*

I want to make an argument that will sound strange coming from someone whose job is property. That question is the wrong one. Not "difficult to answer," not "too early to tell" — wrong. It points a financially-ready owner's attention at the one thing they cannot control and away from the four things they can. And I've watched that misdirection cost people real money, in both directions: the ones who panic-rush a move, and the ones who freeze and lose another year of their window. This piece is about the question that actually pays you, and a four-part method for answering it.

## First, the facts — because you have to see the noise clearly before you can ignore it correctly

Let me lay out exactly what changed, because the reframe only works if the facts are straight. From 28 July, a private property owner — or a former owner, at any age — who buys a non-subsidised HDB resale flat *without* taking an HDB housing loan faces no wait-out period and no flat-size restriction [Source: Article, HDB (official), https://www.hdb.gov.sg/hdb-pulse/news/2026/removal-of-the-15-month-wait-out-period-for-private-residential-property-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]. Take an HDB loan instead, and the fifteen-month wait still applies [Source: Article, HDB (official), https://www.hdb.gov.sg/hdb-pulse/news/2026/removal-of-the-15-month-wait-out-period-for-private-residential-property-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]. The thirty-month wait-out for subsidised flats, including new BTO, is unchanged [Source: Article, HDB (official), https://www.hdb.gov.sg/hdb-pulse/news/2026/removal-of-the-15-month-wait-out-period-for-private-residential-property-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]. And crucially, buyers must still dispose of their private property, in Singapore or overseas, within six months of completing the resale purchase [Source: Article, HDB (official), https://www.hdb.gov.sg/hdb-pulse/news/2026/removal-of-the-15-month-wait-out-period-for-private-residential-property-owners, accessed 2026-07-30].

The rule was introduced on 30 September 2022 as a cooling measure to prioritise first-time buyers, and National Development Minister Chee Hong Tat said this week that it had "served its purpose" [Source: Article, The Star (Straits Times report), https://www.thestar.com.my/aseanplus/aseanplus-news/2026/07/28/singapore-scraps-15-month-wait-for-private-homeowners-buying-hdb-resale-flats, accessed 2026-07-30]. Analysts broadly welcomed the move — but they are genuinely split on whether it re-accelerates resale prices [Source: Article, The Edge Singapore, https://www.theedgesingapore.com/cityandcountry/residential/analysts-cheer-removal-15-month-wait-out-period-private-home-owners, accessed 2026-07-30].

Hold onto one detail there, because most of the coverage skated past it: this rule is for people moving *from* private *into* HDB, disposing of their private home to do it. It is not a rule about holding two properties. I'll come back to why that matters even if that is not your move at all.

## Why "will prices jump" fails you — and the honest thing I have to say

Here is the honest thing, and I'd rather say it up front than bury it. **I cannot tell you where prices are going. Neither can the analysts — they don't agree with each other [Source: Article, The Edge Singapore, https://www.theedgesingapore.com/cityandcountry/residential/analysts-cheer-removal-15-month-wait-out-period-private-home-owners, accessed 2026-07-30] — and neither can any channel that posted a confident number this week.** Anyone who promises you the direction of the market is selling certainty they do not own.

That is not a reason to do nothing. It is a reason to stop building your decision on a foundation nobody can pour. Because notice what a price forecast actually is: it's a description of *the market*. It says nothing about *your* timeline, *your* financing, or *your* life-stage — and those are the only inputs that decide whether a move is right for you. A forecast can be perfectly correct and still lead you into a move that traps you, if your runway was thin and your exits were closed. And it can be wrong while your move is completely fine, because you built the move to survive being wrong. The market's number and your decision live in two different places. Most people spend all their energy on the first and almost none on the second. That is the mistake this article is designed to end.

The name I give the underlying trap is the One-Property Trap: staying stuck at a single property despite being financially and situationally ready to move — not because you can't, but because you have no plan, so the loudest headline takes the wheel. The cure isn't a better prediction. It's a method that makes the prediction irrelevant.

## The Personal Runway Method — four rules that never need a forecast

I teach this as four named rules. Each one is reusable on every headline for the rest of your life, and none of them requires you to know where prices go.

**Rule 1 — The Forecast Filter.** A forecast is only useful if it changes something you can act on. Run any prediction through three questions: does knowing this change *when* I can move, *how much* I can afford, or *what life-stage* this move is for? For a market-wide forecast, the honest answer is almost always no on all three — which means it has no authority over your decision. When smart analysts openly disagree on the direction [Source: Article, The Edge Singapore, https://www.theedgesingapore.com/cityandcountry/residential/analysts-cheer-removal-15-month-wait-out-period-private-home-owners, accessed 2026-07-30], that disagreement is itself the signal that the answer you need is not in the forecast at all. Filter it out and keep walking.

**Rule 2 — The Runway Test.** This is the number that matters more than any forecast, and it's the one no analyst can give you, because it's yours. Take the move you're weighing and don't model it at today's best-case interest rate with everything going smoothly. Model it at a *worse* rate, with a gap in rental or income, and ask: *how many months can I carry this without stress before something has to give?* That number of months is your runway. It beats a price forecast every time, because a forecast tells you what might happen to the market, while your runway tells you what happens to you if the forecast is wrong — and you control your runway, not the market. Consider the backdrop: HDB resale price growth has cooled sharply, from 10.4% in 2022 to 2.9% in 2025, with the resale price index dipping 0.1% quarter-on-quarter in the first quarter of 2026 and 0.3% in the second [Source: Article, EdgeProp Singapore, https://www.edgeprop.sg/property-news/government-removes-15-month-wait-out-period-private-property-owners-buy-hdb-resale-flats, accessed 2026-07-30]. Is that a floor before a rebound, or a cooling that continues? The analysts don't agree [Source: Article, The Edge Singapore, https://www.theedgesingapore.com/cityandcountry/residential/analysts-cheer-removal-15-month-wait-out-period-private-home-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]. A move with twenty-four months of runway survives either answer. A move with three months of runway is a bet that the forecast comes true.

**Rule 3 — The Two-Clock Rule.** Every property move runs on two clocks: the market's, which you cannot read, and yours, which you can. Your clock is made of knowable dates — when your MOP ends, when an ABSD-relevant window opens or closes for your structuring, where you are in a life-stage you want the move completed by. The market's clock — "when will prices move" — has no date anyone can honestly give you. When the two conflict, plan to the clock you control. I meet owners who have been "waiting for the right time" for three years and cannot name a single event they are waiting for. That is not patience; it is the market's imaginary clock quietly eating years off their real one. This is also where that earlier detail earns its place: because the new rule is for owners moving from private into HDB and disposing of the private home [Source: Article, HDB (official), https://www.hdb.gov.sg/hdb-pulse/news/2026/removal-of-the-15-month-wait-out-period-for-private-residential-property-owners, accessed 2026-07-30], an owner whose plan is to *add* a second property rather than swap down into a flat may find this specific change is not their move at all — and that's fine. The Two-Clock Rule runs identically. The headline is just the market's clock making noise; you still plan to yours.

**Rule 4 — The Reversibility Check.** Before any move, ask: if this goes against me, what is my exit? This is the Exit-Safe principle applied to a headline. Can I hold, rent, sell without a forced loss, or restructure if circumstances turn? A move with two or three safe exits can be made *without* needing the forecast to be right. A move with no exit should not be made no matter how confident the forecast sounds — precisely because no forecast can be trusted to be right.

Look at what the four rules do together. The Filter takes the prediction off the table. The Runway Test replaces it with your holding power. The Two-Clock Rule replaces market timing with your timing. The Reversibility Check ensures that even when you're wrong, you're not trapped. It is one system, and not one part of it depends on knowing where prices go.

## What this looks like when a real, capable couple runs it

Let me show you the method in motion, anonymised, with the details changed but the shape intact — because I saw a version of this the very week the rule dropped.

A couple, both in their early forties, no children by choice — a DINK household with a condo they're comfortable in and a genuinely strong cash and CPF position. The headlines hit, the "will resale jump" videos start, and they split: one wants to rush a move this quarter before prices supposedly run, the other wants to freeze and wait for the market to settle.

Here is the part that nearly cost them, and it's the part that looks *responsible*. They opened a mortgage calculator, entered today's rate at best case, and the numbers looked fine. On that basis, the rush-it partner was ready to commit. It looked rigorous. But every input in that model was a bet that the market forecast would come true — best-case rate, smooth timing, prices behaving. They had built a plan that only worked if a prediction nobody could make turned out right.

Here is where it turned. We didn't argue about prices at all. We ran the four rules. The Forecast Filter took "prices might jump" off the table, because it changed none of their real inputs. The Runway Test, re-run at a worse rate with an income gap, revealed that the comfortable-looking move had a far thinner runway than they'd assumed — while a slightly different structure had far more. The Two-Clock Rule showed their real deadline was never the market; it was a financing-and-structuring window tied to their own timeline, with an actual date on it. And the Reversibility Check showed one version of the move had a clean exit and the other essentially didn't. The moment they stopped asking "will prices jump" and started asking "what's my runway and my clock," a decision that had been a two-week argument became obvious in about an hour. Not because we predicted the market — because we no longer needed to.

I'll be deliberate about what I have *not* told you here: their exact runway in months, their exact rate, their exact structure. Those are their numbers, and yours are different — different position, different clock, different exits. The method is what's shareable. The numbers are what you build in a room, with your real figures in front of you.
