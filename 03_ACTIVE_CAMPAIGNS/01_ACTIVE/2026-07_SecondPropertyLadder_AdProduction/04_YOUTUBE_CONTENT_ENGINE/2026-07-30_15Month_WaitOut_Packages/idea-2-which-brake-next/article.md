# One Brake Came Off. Smart Owners Are Already Asking Which Is Next.

*How a financially-ready Singapore owner should read the removal of the 15-month wait-out — as a signal to position, not a signal to predict.*

On the 28th of July 2026, one of the brakes on Singapore's property market quietly came off. A private residential property owner — or even a former private owner, at any age — can now buy a non-subsidised HDB resale flat with no wait-out period at all, of any flat size, provided they do not take an HDB housing loan. The change was immediate. `[Source: Article, HDB (official), https://www.hdb.gov.sg/hdb-pulse/news/2026/removal-of-the-15-month-wait-out-period-for-private-residential-property-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]`

That brake was fitted on the 30th of September 2022, when the fifteen-month wait-out was introduced as a cooling measure to give first-time buyers priority. `[Source: Article, 99.co, https://www.99.co/singapore/insider/15-month-period-removed-for-private-property-owners-buying-hdb-resale/, accessed 2026-07-30]` Announcing its removal, National Development Minister Chee Hong Tat said the measure had "served its purpose." `[Source: Article, The Edge Singapore, https://www.theedgesingapore.com/cityandcountry/residential/analysts-cheer-removal-15-month-wait-out-period-private-home-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]`

Here is the part that matters, and it is not the part the headlines led with. This is the first notable *easing* of the 2022 cooling-measure suite. Not a tweak in the middle of a tightening cycle — a brake, coming off. And the instinct it triggers in a lot of owners is precisely the instinct that gets them into trouble: they read "brake comes off," they hear "go," and they reach for a move built for a market that may never arrive.

This piece is not a prediction. I don't make price predictions, and you should be wary of anyone who does right now. This is about how a financially-ready owner — someone with a condo, an EC, or a high-value HDB, real cash and CPF behind them, and a genuine six-to-twenty-four-month timeline — should actually read a moment like this. The honest answer is: not as a starting gun, but as a prompt to ask a better question. Not "will prices go up," but "which brake is next — and am I positioned to win either way?"

## First, map the brake — because this one is narrower than it sounds

Before anyone celebrates or panics, get precise about what actually changed, because the details do almost all the work.

The wait-out was removed only for private owners buying a non-subsidised resale flat *without an HDB housing loan*. If you take an HDB housing loan, the fifteen-month wait still applies. For a subsidised flat — including a new BTO — the thirty-month wait still applies. And you must still dispose of your private property, in Singapore or overseas, within six months of completing the resale purchase. `[Source: Article, HDB (official), https://www.hdb.gov.sg/hdb-pulse/news/2026/removal-of-the-15-month-wait-out-period-for-private-residential-property-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]` `[Source: Article, 99.co, https://www.99.co/singapore/insider/15-month-period-removed-for-private-property-owners-buying-hdb-resale/, accessed 2026-07-30]`

And this is the line worth underlining, because it is the one most easily missed: no other cooling measure was touched. The Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty was not changed. Loan limits were not changed. This announcement removed one brake — the wait-out — and nothing else. `[Source: Article, EdgeProp Singapore, https://www.edgeprop.sg/property-news/government-removes-15-month-wait-out-period-private-property-owners-buy-hdb-resale-flats, accessed 2026-07-30]`

I teach owners to hold a simple mental picture I call the Brake Map. There are brakes that affect *timing* — how fast you can move between properties — and brakes that affect *cost and capacity* — how much a second property costs you in stamp duty and how much you can borrow. What came off on the 28th of July was one timing brake, on one narrow path. Every cost-and-capacity brake is still fully on. If you can't say in a single sentence which brake changed and whether it sits on the path your own next move takes, you don't yet know whether this news is even about you.

## A removed timing brake changes your order, not your budget

The most expensive mistake available this month is to treat a calendar change as a money change.

Before the 28th, if your next move involved selling your private property and buying a resale HDB flat — to rightsize, to release capital, to reposition which property sits in which name — you faced a fifteen-month gap. Fifteen months of renting, moving twice, and watching your capital sit idle. That gap didn't only cost money; it quietly killed a lot of otherwise sensible moves, because the in-between was simply too painful to run. Now, on the no-HDB-loan resale path, that front-end wait collapses, replaced by a six-month disposal requirement on the back end. `[Source: Article, HDB (official), https://www.hdb.gov.sg/hdb-pulse/news/2026/removal-of-the-15-month-wait-out-period-for-private-residential-property-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]`

That genuinely unlocks something. It makes certain *sequences* clean that used to be clumsy — any plan where an HDB resale leg was step one now runs with far less friction. But notice what it did not do. It did not make anything more affordable. Your ABSD exposure on a second property is unchanged. Your borrowing capacity is unchanged. `[Source: Article, EdgeProp Singapore, https://www.edgeprop.sg/property-news/government-removes-15-month-wait-out-period-private-property-owners-buy-hdb-resale-flats, accessed 2026-07-30]` The removal changed *when* and *in what order* you can move. It said nothing about whether the moves add up. That calculation is still yours, with real numbers — and it is exactly the calculation a headline can never do for you.

There is an honest caveat here, and I'd rather flag it than dress it up. This specific brake most directly helps owners whose next move includes an HDB resale leg — rightsizing, releasing capital, repositioning a name. If your next move is to add a second *private* property purely as an investment, this measure doesn't mechanically change your position at all. For you, its value is as a *signal* of where policy is heading, not a direct unlock. Read it accordingly.

## Don't pick the forecast — pick the position

This is the heart of it. And the reason it's the heart of it is that the people paid to forecast this cannot agree.

Analysts cheered the removal, and some expect it to add demand to the resale market. `[Source: Article, The Edge Singapore, https://www.theedgesingapore.com/cityandcountry/residential/analysts-cheer-removal-15-month-wait-out-period-private-home-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]` But look at where the market actually sits. HDB resale price growth cooled from 10.4% in 2022 to 2.9% in 2025. The Resale Price Index fell 0.1% quarter-on-quarter in the first quarter of 2026 and 0.3% in the second, after a stretch of slow-to-no growth from the fourth quarter of 2024 through 2025. `[Source: Article, EdgeProp Singapore, https://www.edgeprop.sg/property-news/government-removes-15-month-wait-out-period-private-property-owners-buy-hdb-resale-flats, accessed 2026-07-30]` `[Source: Article, The Edge Singapore, https://www.theedgesingapore.com/cityandcountry/residential/analysts-cheer-removal-15-month-wait-out-period-private-home-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]` One camp sees fresh demand; another sees a cooling market.

So who's right? It's the wrong question. The financially-ready owner's edge has never been a better forecast — it's a position that survives being wrong about the forecast.

Lay the plausible policy paths out as three scenarios. One: the brakes stay where they are, and this was a one-off cleanup of a measure that had served its purpose. Two: selective easing continues, and this was simply the first of several timing frictions to be unwound while the market stays soft. Three: tightening returns — if demand runs hot, the government has shown for years that it will step back in, and the cost-and-capacity brakes left untouched this round are exactly the levers it would reach for.

Now the move: don't choose the scenario, choose the position that doesn't get wrecked by any of them. A move that only wins under scenario two — "more easing is coming, so I'll stretch now" — is a bet. A move that holds up whether brakes stay, ease, or tighten is a plan. Write your intended next move as one sentence, then ask it three times — does this still make sense if brakes stay, if easing continues, if tightening returns? If it fails any one of those, you're holding a wager, not a position.

## The two-sided test that keeps you honest

The practical version of scenario thinking is a stress test with two sides, drawn straight from the argument the analysts are having.

Side one: prices re-accelerate. Does your move still work — or does it only work if you got in *before* the acceleration, meaning you're actually relying on timing you can't control? Side two: prices stay flat or soften further, which is what the most recent quarters actually did. `[Source: Article, EdgeProp Singapore, https://www.edgeprop.sg/property-news/government-removes-15-month-wait-out-period-private-property-owners-buy-hdb-resale-flats, accessed 2026-07-30]` Can your move absorb a flat market — the holding costs, the ABSD you've already paid, the slower exit — without forcing your hand?

A real plan is almost boring at this step. It works on both sides. It may do better on one, but it doesn't *break* on either. If your next move needs prices to move in a specific direction to be safe, you haven't found your next move yet — you've found your risk.

## What this looks like in a real living room

Let me make it concrete, anonymised and with details changed, but real in shape. A DINK couple, early forties, one private condo held comfortably in joint names, strong cash and CPF. When the news broke, they called within the week, genuinely excited, with a plan formed in about three days: sell the condo now while they still felt good about the price, buy a resale HDB immediately since the wait was gone, and "free up" to buy an investment property later.

On paper, it uses the Sequencing Window beautifully. But run it through the three scenarios and the two-sided test, and it comes apart. It worked in exactly one world — prices stay flat *and* they could re-enter the private market cheaply *and* no cost-and-capacity brake returned. Every leg leaned on the next. Worse, they'd anchored their excitement to a measure that never touched the most expensive part of their plan: their ABSD exposure on the future investment property was completely unchanged by this news. `[Source: Article, EdgeProp Singapore, https://www.edgeprop.sg/property-news/government-removes-15-month-wait-out-period-private-property-owners-buy-hdb-resale-flats, accessed 2026-07-30]` And selling their only property first, into a market that had just printed two negative quarters, `[Source: Article, The Edge Singapore, https://www.theedgesingapore.com/cityandcountry/residential/analysts-cheer-removal-15-month-wait-out-period-private-home-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]` would have made them a forced buyer on the way back in.

What changed wasn't the market. It was the question. The news was never their reason — it was just a cleaner calendar. Their real question was "what's the next move that gives us a second property and survives all three policy scenarios?" The wait-out removal was a footnote to that question, not the answer. Once they saw that, the urge to transact within a week evaporated. Same couple, same news — a completely different move, because they stopped predicting and started positioning.

## The gap only you can close

That couple's trap wasn't the market and it wasn't the policy. It was the One-Property Trap in its most modern form: financially ready, situationally ready, and about to react to a headline instead of moving from a plan. The most expensive place to sit isn't a soft market. It's one property with no clear next move, mistaking motion for progress.

Everything above is the method: map the brake, respect the difference between calendar and budget, position across scenarios rather than betting on one, and stress-test both sides. What the method can't do — what no article can do — is finish your numbers. Your name structure, your ABSD position, your real cash-and-CPF runway, and the one sequence that survives all three scenarios *for you*: that's the gap between a good framework and an actual next move.
