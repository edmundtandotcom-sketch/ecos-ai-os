## MODULE 1 — RULE: "The Brake Map" (map each measure as its own brake, not one blob)
*~1:00–3:15*

"Rule one. I call it **the Brake Map**. And the rule is simple: never treat 'cooling measures' as one big blob. Map each one as a separate brake, and know exactly which brakes touch *your* next move.

Here's why this matters right now. What actually changed on the 28th of July is narrow — and I need you to hear how narrow. The wait-out was removed **only** for private owners buying a non-subsidised resale flat **without an HDB loan**. `[Source: Article, HDB (official), https://www.hdb.gov.sg/hdb-pulse/news/2026/removal-of-the-15-month-wait-out-period-for-private-residential-property-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]`

**[PATTERN INTERRUPT — count on fingers]**

Watch the brakes that did *not* move. If you take an HDB housing loan, the fifteen-month wait still applies. `[Source: Article, HDB (official), https://www.hdb.gov.sg/hdb-pulse/news/2026/removal-of-the-15-month-wait-out-period-for-private-residential-property-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]` For a subsidised flat — including a new BTO — the thirty-month wait still applies. `[Source: Article, 99.co, https://www.99.co/singapore/insider/15-month-period-removed-for-private-property-owners-buying-hdb-resale/, accessed 2026-07-30]` And you still have to dispose of your private property, here or overseas, within six months of completing that resale purchase. `[Source: Article, HDB (official), https://www.hdb.gov.sg/hdb-pulse/news/2026/removal-of-the-15-month-wait-out-period-for-private-residential-property-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]`

And this is the part the headlines skip, so I'm going to say it plainly: **no other cooling measure was touched.** The Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty was not changed. Loan limits were not changed. This announcement was about one brake — the wait-out — and nothing else. `[Source: Article, EdgeProp Singapore, https://www.edgeprop.sg/property-news/government-removes-15-month-wait-out-period-private-property-owners-buy-hdb-resale-flats, accessed 2026-07-30]`

So the Brake Map for you looks like this. There are brakes that affect **timing** — how fast you can move between properties. And there are brakes that affect **cost and capacity** — how much a second property costs you in stamp duty, and how much you can borrow. The 28th of July removed one *timing* brake, on one *narrow path*. Every cost-and-capacity brake is still fully on.

**Self-check for Rule 1:** Before you get excited or anxious about any property headline, ask — *which specific brake changed, and does it sit on the path my next move actually takes?* If you can't answer that in one sentence, you don't yet know if the news is even about you."

## MODULE 2 — RULE: "The Sequencing Window" (a removed timing brake changes your move ORDER, not your budget)
*~3:15–5:45*

"Rule two. **The Sequencing Window.** A removed timing friction changes *when* and *in what order* you can move — it does not change *how much* you can afford. Confuse those two, and you'll make a budget decision off a calendar change.

Here's the concrete before-and-after. Before the 28th, if your next move involved selling your private property and buying a resale HDB flat — to rightsize, to release capital, to reposition one name in a couple — you had a fifteen-month gap to survive. `[Source: Article, 99.co, https://www.99.co/singapore/insider/15-month-period-removed-for-private-property-owners-buying-hdb-resale/, accessed 2026-07-30]` Fifteen months of renting, of moving twice, of your capital sitting in a bank instead of working. That gap didn't just cost money. It killed optionality. A lot of owners simply didn't make the move because the in-between was too painful.

**[PATTERN INTERRUPT — 'Here's what's different now']**

Now — on the no-HDB-loan resale path — that window collapses to essentially nothing, with a six-month disposal requirement on the back end instead of a fifteen-month wait on the front. `[Source: Article, HDB (official), https://www.hdb.gov.sg/hdb-pulse/news/2026/removal-of-the-15-month-wait-out-period-for-private-residential-property-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]`

So what does this actually unlock for a financially-ready owner? It unlocks *sequences* that were previously too clumsy to run. Rightsizing from a private property into a resale flat to free up capital for a separate investment move. Repositioning which property sits in which name. Any plan where an HDB resale leg was step one — that step just got a lot cleaner.

But — and this is the rule doing its work — **it did not make anything more affordable.** Your ABSD exposure on a second property is unchanged. Your borrowing capacity is unchanged. The Sequencing Window tells you the *order* of your moves can be tighter now. It says nothing about whether the moves add up. That's a separate calculation — and it's yours, with real numbers, not a headline's.

**Self-check for Rule 2:** *Did this news change my budget, or only my calendar?* If the honest answer is 'only my calendar,' then the news is an enabler, not a reason. The reason still has to come from your numbers."

## MODULE 3 — RULE: "Scenario Positioning over Prediction" (win under 2-3 plausible policy paths, don't bet on one forecast)
*~6:30–9:15* — **[STRONGEST MODULE]**

"Rule three, and if you remember only one thing from today, remember this one. **Scenario Positioning over Prediction.** You do not need to know what happens next. You need a position that wins across the two or three futures that are actually plausible.

Here's the honest state of play. The people whose job is to forecast this are split. Analysts cheered the removal — some expect it to add demand to the resale market. `[Source: Article, The Edge Singapore, https://www.theedgesingapore.com/cityandcountry/residential/analysts-cheer-removal-15-month-wait-out-period-private-home-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]` But look at where the market actually is. HDB resale price growth cooled from 10.4% in 2022 to 2.9% in 2025. `[Source: Article, EdgeProp Singapore, https://www.edgeprop.sg/property-news/government-removes-15-month-wait-out-period-private-property-owners-buy-hdb-resale-flats, accessed 2026-07-30]` The Resale Price Index actually fell — down 0.1% quarter-on-quarter in the first quarter of 2026, and down 0.3% in the second. `[Source: Article, The Edge Singapore, https://www.theedgesingapore.com/cityandcountry/residential/analysts-cheer-removal-15-month-wait-out-period-private-home-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]`

**[PATTERN INTERRUPT — 'So who's right?']**

So who's right — the ones who see fresh demand, or the ones who see a cooling market? Here's my answer: **it is the wrong question.** I am not going to predict a price for you, and you should be suspicious of anyone who does.

Instead, lay out the plausible policy paths as three scenarios:

Scenario one — **the brakes stay where they are.** This was a one-off cleanup of a measure that 'served its purpose,' `[Source: Article, The Edge Singapore, https://www.theedgesingapore.com/cityandcountry/residential/analysts-cheer-removal-15-month-wait-out-period-private-home-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]` and nothing else moves for a long time.

Scenario two — **selective easing continues.** This was the first brake, and over the coming cycle a few more timing-type frictions get unwound as the market stays soft.

Scenario three — **tightening returns.** If demand runs hot, the government has shown for years it will step back in. The cost-and-capacity brakes — the ones untouched this round — are exactly the levers it would reach for.

Now here's the move. **Don't pick the scenario. Pick the position that doesn't get wrecked by any of them.** A position that only wins in scenario two — 'more easing is coming, so I'll stretch now' — is a bet. A position that wins whether the brakes stay, ease, or tighten — that's a plan. The financially-ready owner's edge isn't a better forecast. It's building a next move that survives being wrong about the forecast.

**Self-check for Rule 3:** Write your intended next move as one sentence. Then ask it three times — *does this still make sense if brakes stay? If easing continues? If tightening returns?* If it fails any one of those, you don't have a position. You have a wager."

## MODULE 4 — RULE: "The Two-Sided Stress Test" (test the move against both 're-accelerate' and 'stay soft')
*~9:15–10:45*

"Rule four. **The Two-Sided Stress Test.** This is Rule three made practical. Take your specific next move and run it through both sides of the market that the analysts are arguing about.

Side one: prices re-accelerate. Does your move still work? Or does it only work if you got in *before* the acceleration — meaning you're actually relying on timing you can't control?

Side two: prices stay flat or soften further — which is what the last several quarters actually did, with slower or no growth from the fourth quarter of 2024 through 2025 and a mild decline into 2026. `[Source: Article, EdgeProp Singapore, https://www.edgeprop.sg/property-news/government-removes-15-month-wait-out-period-private-property-owners-buy-hdb-resale-flats, accessed 2026-07-30]` `[Source: Article, The Edge Singapore, https://www.theedgesingapore.com/cityandcountry/residential/analysts-cheer-removal-15-month-wait-out-period-private-home-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]` Can your move absorb a flat market — the holding costs, the ABSD you've already paid, the slower exit — without forcing your hand?

**[PATTERN INTERRUPT — 'A real plan is boring here']**

A real plan is almost boring at this step. It works on both sides — maybe better on one, but it doesn't *break* on either. If your move needs prices to move a specific direction to be safe, you haven't found your next move yet. You've found your risk.

**Self-check for Rule 4:** *Which side of the market does my plan need? If the answer is 'one specific side,' I'm exposed.*"
