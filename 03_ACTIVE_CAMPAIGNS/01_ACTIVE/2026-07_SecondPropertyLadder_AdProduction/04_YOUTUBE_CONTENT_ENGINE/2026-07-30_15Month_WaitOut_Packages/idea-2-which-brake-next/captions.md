## LINKEDIN (professional, analytical, thought-leadership)
On 28 July 2026, Singapore removed the 15-month wait-out period for private property owners buying non-subsidised HDB resale flats without an HDB loan. [Source: HDB official]

Most of the commentary I've read frames this as a demand story. I'd argue the more useful frame for a financially-ready owner is a regime-signal story.

Three things worth separating clearly:

1. What actually changed is narrow. The wait-out is removed only on the no-HDB-loan resale path. HDB-loan buyers still face 15 months; subsidised/BTO buyers still face 30; the 6-month private disposal rule remains. Critically, ABSD and loan-to-value limits were not touched. [Source: EdgeProp Singapore]

2. What it signals is broader. This is the first notable easing of the 2022 cooling-measure suite — a change in direction, not destination. The minister framed it as a measure that had "served its purpose." [Source: The Edge Singapore]

3. What it demands is discipline. The forecasters are split — analysts welcomed it, yet the HDB Resale Price Index fell 0.1% then 0.3% q-o-q in 1H2026. In that environment, the edge isn't a sharper prediction. It's a position robust across scenarios: brakes stay, brakes ease, or brakes tighten. A move that only pays off under one path is a bet, not a plan.

For owners weighing a second-property move, the practical takeaway is a two-sided stress test: does your move survive both a re-acceleration and a continued soft market? If it only works on one side, that's your risk, not your strategy.
