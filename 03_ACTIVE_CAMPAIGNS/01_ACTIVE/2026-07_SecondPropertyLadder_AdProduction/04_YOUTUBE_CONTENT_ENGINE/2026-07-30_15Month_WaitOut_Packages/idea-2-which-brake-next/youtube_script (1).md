## 4:30–8:30 — RULE 1: THE BRAKE MAP

`[DELIVER: Teaching rhythm. Make "brakes" a physical, visual idea. This module is about precision — reward them for thinking clearly.]`

`[SCREEN: "Rule 1 — The Brake Map"]`

Rule one — the Brake Map. Never treat "cooling measures" as one big blob. Map each one as a separate brake, and know exactly which brakes touch *your* next move.

Here's the map. Some brakes affect *timing* — how fast you can move between properties. Others affect *cost and capacity* — how much a second property costs you in stamp duty, and how much you can borrow. What came off on the 28th was one *timing* brake, on one *narrow* path. Every cost-and-capacity brake is still fully on.

`[SCREEN: two columns — "TIMING brakes" vs "COST & CAPACITY brakes"; the wait-out lifts out of the timing column]`

`[DELIVER: The reframe — why this precision pays.]`

Why does this matter so much? Because the owners who get hurt in these moments aren't the slow ones. They're the ones who react fast to the wrong thing. They read "brake comes off," they hear "go", and they make a move built for a market — and a rulebook — that isn't actually there. A clear map is what stops you doing that.

`[DELIVER: The habit.]`

So the habit: before you get excited or anxious about any property headline, ask one question — *which specific brake changed, and does it sit on the path my next move actually takes?* If you can't answer that in a sentence, you don't yet know whether the news is even about you.

`[DELIVER: 3-questions beat.]`

Is it easy? Yes — it's one question and a mental two-column map. No maths.

Can you do it yourself? Completely. The discipline is refusing to feel anything about a headline until you've placed the brake on your own map.

Will it work for your situation? This one works for everyone, because it's what tells you whether the rest of the video is even about you. For a lot of people this week, the honest map says "this brake isn't on my path" — and that clarity is worth more than any prediction.

## 8:30–13:00 — RULE 2: THE SEQUENCING WINDOW

`[DELIVER: Energy steady. This is a "before and after" module — paint the old fifteen-month pain vividly, then the new speed, then the hard turn: it changed your calendar, not your budget.]`

`[SCREEN: "Rule 2 — The Sequencing Window"]`

Rule two — the Sequencing Window. A removed *timing* brake changes *when* and *in what order* you can move. It does not change *how much* you can afford. Confuse those two, and you'll make a budget decision off a calendar change.

Picture the before. Until the 28th, if your next move meant selling your private property and buying a resale flat — to right-size, to release capital, to reposition a name — you had a fifteen-month gap to survive.

`[CITE: Article, 99.co, https://www.99.co/singapore/insider/15-month-period-removed-for-private-property-owners-buying-hdb-resale/, accessed 2026-07-30]`

Fifteen months of renting. Moving twice. Your capital parked in a bank instead of working. That gap didn't just cost money — it killed the move entirely for a lot of owners. Too painful to bother.

`[DELIVER: Now the after — brighter, faster.]`

Now, on the no-HDB-loan resale path, that window basically collapses — with a six-month disposal requirement on the back end instead of a fifteen-month wait on the front.

`[CITE: Article, HDB (official), https://www.hdb.gov.sg/hdb-pulse/news/2026/removal-of-the-15-month-wait-out-period-for-private-residential-property-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]`

So what does that unlock? Sequences that used to be too clumsy to run. Right-sizing from private into a resale flat to free capital for a separate move. Repositioning which property sits in which name. Any plan where a resale-HDB leg was step one — that step just got a lot cleaner.

`[DELIVER: The hard turn — this is the rule doing its work. Firm.]`

But — and this is the whole rule — it did *not* make anything more affordable. Your ABSD exposure on a second property, unchanged. Your borrowing capacity, unchanged. The Sequencing Window tells you the *order* of your moves can be tighter now. It says nothing about whether the moves actually add up. That's a separate sum — and it's yours, with real numbers, not a headline's.

`[DELIVER: 3-questions beat.]`

Is it easy? The question is dead easy — "did this change my budget, or only my calendar?" Sitting with the honest answer is the discipline.

Can you do it yourself? Yes — and this one check will stop the most common mistake of the week: treating a faster calendar as a bigger budget.

Will it work for your situation? If your honest answer is "only my calendar," then the news is an *enabler*, not a *reason*. The reason still has to come from your numbers — and that's true for everyone watching.

## 14:00–19:00 — RULE 3: SCENARIO POSITIONING OVER PREDICTION

`[DELIVER: This is the centrepiece — if they remember one thing, it's this. Slow, weighty. Lay the three scenarios out like you're dealing cards. Then the turn: don't pick one, pick the position that survives all three.]`

`[SCREEN: "Rule 3 — Scenario Positioning over Prediction"]`

Rule three, and if you remember only one thing today, remember this. Scenario Positioning over Prediction. You do not need to know what happens next. You need a position that wins across the two or three futures that are actually plausible.

Here's the honest state of play. The people paid to forecast this are split. Some analysts cheered the removal and expect it to add demand.

`[CITE: Article, The Edge Singapore, https://www.theedgesingapore.com/cityandcountry/residential/analysts-cheer-removal-15-month-wait-out-period-private-home-owners, accessed 2026-07-30]`

But look at where the market actually is. Resale price growth cooled from 10.4% in 2022 to 2.9% in 2025 — and the Resale Price Index actually *fell* a touch in each of the first two quarters of this year.

`[SCREEN: line trending down — "10.4% (2022) → 2.9% (2025) → slight dip 1Q–2Q 2026"] [CITE: Article, EdgeProp Singapore, https://www.edgeprop.sg/property-news/government-removes-15-month-wait-out-period-private-property-owners-buy-hdb-resale-flats, accessed 2026-07-30]`

`[DELIVER: The turn — "so who's right?" then reject the question.]`

So who's right — the ones who see fresh demand, or the ones who see a cooling market? My honest answer: it's the wrong question. I'm not going to predict a price for you, and you should be suspicious of anyone who does.

Instead, lay out the plausible policy paths as three scenarios.

`[SCREEN: three cards appear as he names them]`

Scenario one — the brakes stay put. This was a one-off cleanup of a measure that "served its purpose," and nothing else moves for a long while.

Scenario two — selective easing continues. This was the *first* brake, and over the next cycle a few more timing frictions get unwound while the market stays soft.

Scenario three — tightening returns. If demand runs hot, the government has shown for years it'll step back in — and the cost-and-capacity brakes it left untouched this round are exactly the levers it'd reach for.

`[DELIVER: The move — the intellectual payoff. Calm conviction.]`

Now here's the move. Don't pick the scenario. Pick the position that doesn't get wrecked by any of them. A position that only wins in scenario two — "more easing is coming, so I'll stretch now" — that's a bet. A position that wins whether the brakes stay, ease, *or* tighten — that's a plan. The financially-ready owner's edge was never a better forecast. It's a next move that survives being wrong about the forecast.

`[DELIVER: 3-questions beat.]`

Is it easy? The idea is easy. The honesty is the work — you have to be willing to give up the fantasy that you can call the market.

Can you do it yourself? Yes — write your move as one sentence, then ask it three times: does this still make sense if brakes stay, if easing continues, if tightening returns? If it fails any one, you've got a wager, not a position.

Will it work for your situation? This is the one that works *because* it doesn't depend on the future going your way. Whatever the market does, a position that survives all three is a position you can stand behind.

## 19:00–22:30 — RULE 4: THE TWO-SIDED STRESS TEST

`[DELIVER: This is Rule 3 made practical — brisker, hands-on. The "a real plan is boring here" line is the keeper; deliver it dry, almost with a smile.]`

`[SCREEN: "Rule 4 — The Two-Sided Stress Test"]`

Rule four — the Two-Sided Stress Test. This is Rule three you can actually run. Take your specific next move and push it through both sides of the argument the analysts are having.

Side one — prices re-accelerate. Does your move still work? Or does it only work if you got in *before* the run — meaning you're secretly relying on timing you can't control?

Side two — prices stay flat or soften further. Which, by the way, is what the last several quarters actually did.

`[CITE: Article, EdgeProp Singapore, https://www.edgeprop.sg/property-news/government-removes-15-month-wait-out-period-private-property-owners-buy-hdb-resale-flats, accessed 2026-07-30]`

Can your move absorb a flat market — the holding costs, the stamp duty you've already paid, the slower exit — without forcing your hand?

`[DELIVER: The keeper line — dry.]`

A real plan is almost boring at this step. It works on both sides. Maybe better on one, but it doesn't *break* on either. If your move needs prices to go a specific direction to be safe, you haven't found your next move yet. You've found your risk.

`[DELIVER: 3-questions beat.]`

Is it easy? Yes — two questions, both sides. You can do it on the back of an envelope.

Can you do it yourself? The test, absolutely. Where a second opinion helps is being honest about the flat-market side, because that's the one optimism likes to skip.

Will it work for your situation? This is the check that turns a good-feeling move into a proven one — because a move that survives both sides is a move that doesn't need the market's permission.
