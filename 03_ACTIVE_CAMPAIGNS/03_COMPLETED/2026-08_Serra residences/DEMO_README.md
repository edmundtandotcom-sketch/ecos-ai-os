## What's new here (vs Amberwood)

**Style Variant F — "Prime Editorial."** Deep ink ground, champagne gold
hairlines, electric cyan as the second data colour. It reads like a
financial broadsheet on air, where Amberwood read as a clean white deck.

**PrimeKit** — a new display set built on one rule: *animate the content,
don't fade in a card*. That was the "visual effects are still lacking" note.
In these two minutes you'll see:

| Beat | Component |
|---|---|
| "the last new launch in this exact pocket" | **FactSlate** — spec sheet, hairline grid, rows snapping in one at a time over a dimmed reference image |
| "even after 6 years" | **NumberSlam** — one giant number, chromatic-aberration impact on the hit frame, slow settle |
| "so that's the real question" | **LightLeak** wipe into a fullscreen question card |
| "the exact same project type… didn't perform" | **SplitCompare** — Neu vs Serra, animated divider, VS slam, verdict chip |
| "not the price, the gap" | **NumberSlam** again, as the walk-away tease |
| "here's what we're covering" | **AgendaBoard** — five rows, each landing on the beat he actually names it |
| throughout the cold open | **TickerBug** — persistent editorial section bug, clears before any fullscreen plate |

Not used in these two minutes but built and ready for the rest of the video:
**GapBar** (the hero graphic — two price columns with a bracket that visibly
closes from 2019's $900 gap to today's ~$487), **ProportionDots** (100 dots,

## What to judge

1. **1.1× or 1.15×.** 1.15× is what Amberwood and Lucerne shipped at. Serra's
   delivery is a little more deliberate than Amberwood's, so 1.1× may hold
   the nuance better — but it is your ear, not my measurement.
2. **The display language.** Does Prime Editorial read as an upgrade on the
   Amberwood look, or just different?
3. **Density.** The agenda plate holds for ~34 s because the rows land as he
   names them. If that feels long, I can release to camera between items.
