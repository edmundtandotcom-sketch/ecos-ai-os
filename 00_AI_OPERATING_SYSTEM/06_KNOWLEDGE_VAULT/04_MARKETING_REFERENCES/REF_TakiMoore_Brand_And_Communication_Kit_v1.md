> **What this is.** The transferable parts of his brand, written as rules our desks can apply. **It is a model of his method, not a copy of his identity.** No marks, palettes, typefaces or trade names are reproduced. Use it to build *our* identity in *his* structural style.

---

## 0. THE PREMISE

His brand is **verbal and structural**, not visual. The website is plain navy-and-white with headshots and icons; nothing in the design is trying to differentiate. The brand is carried entirely by:

1. a three-word spine,
2. a library of named, counted, drawable frameworks,
3. a specific voice.

That is why the design can be unremarkable and the brand still dominate its category. **Model the three things above. Design ours to our own taste.**

---

## 1. FRAMEWORK DESIGN RULES

Every framework we ship must pass all six:

1. **It is counted.** Three timeframes. Four forces. Nine steps. A number promises completion and tells the listener when they can stop worrying they missed something.
2. **It is drawable in 60 seconds.** On a napkin, on a whiteboard, mid-sentence. *If it cannot be drawn while talking, it is not finished.* This is the hardest test and the one most frameworks fail.
3. **It is named with a definite article.** `The Client X-Ray`, not "client analysis". The article makes it a thing that exists rather than a task you do.
4. **It answers one question.** One framework, one question. If it answers two, it is two frameworks.
5. **It has a shape.** Quadrant, ladder, funnel, map, loop, or numbered line. The shape carries the meaning without words.
6. **It ships with a template.** The framework explains; the template is what the client actually uses. Done-with-you means both.

**The four shapes that do most of the work:**

| Shape | Use it for | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Quadrant (2 axes) | Tension between two variables | Four Pressures — away/toward × now/future |
| Ladder / plateau | Where the buyer currently is | Three Plateaus |
| Numbered line | A sequence to run | The Gap Call's 9 steps |
| Nested windows | Making commitment safe | The Three Windows — year / quarter / month |

---

## 2. NAMING RULES

- Pattern: **`The` + 2–3 concrete words**. Nouns and verbs. No adjectives, no abstractions.
- Prefer a **physical metaphor**: X-Ray, Map, Engine, Doors, Climb, Windows. Physical words are drawable and memorable; conceptual words are neither.
- **Trademark everything** we intend to own. A named framework becomes an asset: ownable, teachable by clients, impossible to un-attribute.
- **Never adopt someone else's name.** Structure travels freely; names do not. See the deep dive §11.
- The name must survive being **said out loud in a lift** without explanation.

---

## 3. THE SPINE RULE

Three words. They must simultaneously serve as:

| Job | Test |
|---|---|
| Curriculum | Can each word be a day of the workshop? |
| Diagnostic | Can a prospect say "mine's broken at ___"? |
| Content taxonomy | Can every piece we publish file under exactly one? |
| Sales logic | Can the sales conversation be "which of the three?" |

**If a word fails any of the four, it is decoration, not spine.**

Ours: `Authority · Advisory · Amplify`. Currently used as a pillar list. Making it pass all four tests is Phase 1 of the rebuild.

## 4. VOICE SPECIFICATION

**Rhythm.** Short declaratives dominate. Fragments allowed. After any long explanatory sentence, a three-word one. *"It's super easy."*

**Transitions.** `So…` to open a section. `Here's the thing.` `Notice how…` `The reality is…`

**Formatting.**
- Numbered lists for steps.
- **Bold** for key phrases and script lines.
- *Italics* for dialogue speakers.
- Generous white space around role-play blocks.
- Sub-heads with em-dashes.

**Rhetoric.**
- Self-reflective questions: *"Who's the one with the power?"*
- One strong metaphor per piece, carried through.
- Repetition for reinforcement: *"Don't beg, don't plead."*
- Direct commands: *"Lead with 'no' before 'yes'."*

**Tone.** Authoritative without arrogance. Tactical over theoretical. Warm open, warm sign-off. **Transparent about his own motives** — he tells you why he structures something the way he does, which reads as honesty and disarms scepticism. This is the most under-copied element of his voice and the cheapest to adopt.

**Banned:** hype adjectives, unearned superlatives, "game-changing", motivational filler, anything that survives being deleted.

## 5. TEACHING & PRESENTING RULES

1. **Draw, don't slide.** Build the model live. Watching a framework get drawn is retained; a finished slide is not.
2. **Framework first, story second.** Model up, then hang a story on it. Never a story hunting for a point.
3. **Role-play, don't describe.** Perform both sides of the call. Demonstrate language patterns; never summarise them.
4. **Teach and sell in the same breath.** Every element must advance the sale, deliver value, hold engagement, and prove capability — simultaneously. There is no "and now, about my program" pivot.
5. **Belief before offer.** Three targets: what they must believe about *you*, about *the product*, about *themselves*. Plus pre-handled objections and pre-killed alternatives.
6. **Cap the teach by the build.** A teaching block is only as long as the build that follows it can justify.
7. **Every block ships an artefact.** Not an insight. An asset they leave with.
8. **Give the real tool away.** The free thing is the actual script, not a teaser. Reciprocity plus proof of depth.

## 6. VIDEO / CONTENT FORMAT

**Cadence.** ~5 pieces/week, ~30 min each — roughly 12 founder-hours/month.

**Sourcing — The Weekly Seven.** Scan seven categories, pick the five with genuine energy:
`calendar · clients · DMs · community · creations · consumption · circumstances`

**Selection rule — Dangerous + Prolific.** A strong opinion the founder actually wants to argue, published consistently. **Energy is the filter, not keyword volume.**

**The template — one framework per piece:**

| Beat | What happens |
|---|---|
| 1. Context | Where I am, who I'm with, what just happened |
| 2. Promise | What this tool does for you, specifically |
| 3. Name it | The framework's name, said clearly |
| 4. Draw it | Build the shape live |
| 5. Walk the parts | One at a time, counted |
| 6. Role-play | Perform the application in real language |
| 7. Hand it over | The template / the actual tool |
| 8. Proof | One named client, one specific result |
| 9. Two doors | Two conversion paths, warm personal sign-off |

**The two forces to hold in balance:**
- **Artist** — expression, originality, authenticity. Drives connection. Cannot be replicated or delegated.
- **Business** — consistency, volume, schedule. Drives reach.

Neither alone works.

**The documented failure mode:** outsource the founder voice → short-term relief → audience disengagement → sales decline → ad spend up 30–60% with no revenue lift. *This is the tension flagged as Decision D4 in the adoption blueprint — our content engine automates precisely what he warns against.*

## 7. VISUAL IDENTITY — what we take and what we don't

**Observable in his:** navy/white, clean, corporate-plain. Founder and client headshots carry the pages. Icon-based navigation for the three pillars. Book as the authority object. Deliberately unfashionable.

**Exact hex values and typefaces are not verified from public sources and have not been reproduced here — and it does not matter, because we should not copy them.**

**What actually transfers:**

| Rule | Why |
|---|---|
| Let the frameworks be the aesthetic | The drawn model is the memorable visual, not the palette |
| Faces, not stock | Founder + clients build the trust the design doesn't try to |
| One authority object | Book, canvas, or physical artefact that proves depth exists |
| Icons for the spine | Three pillars get three marks, used everywhere |
| Don't out-design — out-structure | Design is not where this category is won |

**Explicitly rejected:** cloning his palette, marks, or layout. That copies the one part of his brand doing the least work, while carrying real trademark risk.

---

## 8. THE COMPRESSION TEST

Before anything ships, it must pass:

1. Can I **draw** it in 60 seconds?
2. Can I **name** it with `The` + 2–3 words?
3. Can I **count** its parts?
4. Does it file under **exactly one** spine word?
5. Does it ship with a **template**?
6. Would I **say it out loud** in a lift without apologising for it?

Six yeses, or it is not finished.
