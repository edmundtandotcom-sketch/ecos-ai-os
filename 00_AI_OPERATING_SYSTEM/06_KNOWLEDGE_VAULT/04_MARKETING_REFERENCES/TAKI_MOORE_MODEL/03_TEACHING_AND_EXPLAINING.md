# TAKI MOORE — THE TEACHING & EXPLAINING METHOD
## Version: v1 | Date: 2026-08-13 | Type: REFERENCE RESOURCE
## Part 3 of 5 — `TAKI_MOORE_MODEL`

**This is the file to reach for when the job is: make something complicated land with one person, fast.** Consults, videos, demonstrations, workshop blocks, objection answers.

---

## 0. THE CORE PRINCIPLE

> **He does not explain ideas. He draws structures and hangs ideas on them.**

The difference matters. An explanation asks the listener to hold your words in working memory and assemble the meaning themselves. A drawn structure gives them a container first, so every subsequent sentence has somewhere to go.

This is why he can compress a one-hour coaching session into twenty minutes, and why his clients repeat his vocabulary back to him — they didn't remember what he *said*, they remembered the *shape*.

---

## 1. THE TEN MOVES

### Move 1 — Draw it, don't show it
Build the model **live**, while talking. Hand-drawn, in front of them.

A finished slide is received. A drawing being built is *followed* — the listener's attention tracks the pen and they assemble the idea in step with you. He describes it as "scribbling, drawing it as we speak."

*In a consult:* a blank sheet or iPad, drawn upside-down toward the client, beats any deck you've prepared.

### Move 2 — Count the parts before you name them
"There are three." "Nine steps." "Four forces."

Saying the number first does two things: it promises the explanation will end, and it tells the listener when they can stop worrying they've missed something. Anxiety about incompleteness is the main thing that blocks comprehension.

### Move 3 — `Why · What · How · Now`
The structure for any teaching block, at any length:

| Beat | Job | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| **Why** | Give them a reason to care before any content | Skipping this is the #1 reason good content doesn't land |
| **What** | The 3–5 things they need to know. **No more than five.** | If you have eight, you have two blocks |
| **How** | How it's actually done | Specific enough to act on |
| **Now** | The immediate next step | Without this, insight evaporates |

Works for a 90-second video, a 10-minute consult segment, or a 60-minute session. **The most portable thing in the entire library.**

### Move 4 — Framework first, story second
Put the model up, *then* hang a story on it. Never a story hunting for a point.

A story told before the framework is entertainment the listener has to re-file once they learn the point. A story told after is proof.

### Move 5 — Pop the insight
Don't argue someone out of a belief. **Reframe it with a model or a story** and let them arrive.

From his momentum coaching: locate the actual issue by mapping the process, then "pop the insight" — one image or one comparison that makes the old view untenable. One insight, not five.

### Move 6 — One pill at a time, then check in
From `The 6 Magic Pills™`: give **one** piece of information, then stop and check.

> *"Is this making sense so far?"*
> *"Are you comfortable with everything so far?"*

Never data-dump. The check-in is not politeness — it's a comprehension gate that stops you building on a foundation that didn't land. It also converts a monologue into a conversation without giving up control.

### Move 7 — Chunk up so everyone benefits
When one person asks a narrow question, **raise it one level of abstraction** so it applies to the room.

His "DJ party" principle: one person requests a song, the whole room dances. The opposite is the "deli counter" — serving people one at a time while everyone else waits and disengages.

*In a group consult, webinar, or a video answering one client's question:* answer the question **and** the category it belongs to.

### Move 8 — Shrink the scope
End on something completable in **hours or days**, never months.

His "Pringles" model: get them to do one small thing and the appetite follows. Three options maximum, each small enough to be obviously doable. A big correct action they won't start is worth less than a small one they will.

### Move 9 — Point to resources; don't re-teach
Recommendations → **Resources** → Actions. When something has already been explained well once, send the link, the worksheet, the recording. Live time is for diagnosis and decisions, not repetition.

This is what makes the 20-minute session possible.

### Move 10 — Start with wins, not with what's broken
Open by capturing what's gone right since last time — five things, then genuinely celebrate them.

His distinction: **past-focused accountability** (dwelling on unfinished homework) puts people in defence, where nothing lands. **Future-focused accountability** (acknowledging progress) puts them in a resourceful state, where they can actually think.

*This applies directly to a property consult.* Opening on what the client has done right — even if it's just "you got here before it got worse" — buys you the permission to be blunt later.

## 2. THE PRESENTING RULES

1. **Role-play, don't describe.** Perform both sides of the conversation. Never summarise a script — demonstrate it in real language. People copy what they've heard, not what they've been told about.
2. **Teach and sell in the same breath.** Every element must simultaneously advance the sale, deliver value, hold engagement, and prove capability. There is no "and now, let me tell you about my program" pivot — that pivot is where rooms go cold.
3. **Belief before offer.** Three targets: what they must believe about **you**, about **the product**, about **themselves**. The third is the most skipped and usually the real blocker. Pre-handle objections and pre-kill the alternatives they're silently comparing you to.
4. **Cap the teach by the build.** A teaching block is only as long as the exercise that follows can justify. Teach → do → teach → do.
5. **Every block ships an artefact.** They should leave holding a completed thing, not a page of notes.
6. **Give the real tool away.** The free thing is the actual script or template, not a teaser of it. Reciprocity plus proof that depth exists behind it.
7. **Speak slowly. Pause between questions.** His explicit tonality note on the Triage Call: rushing makes you sound scripted, which reads as sales pressure and kills trust. Deliberate pace reads as competence.
8. **Make sure they're seen.** In any group setting, acknowledge people by name throughout. *"Until you see me, I did not exist."*

## 3. APPLIED — A CLIENT CONSULT

The single most useful adaptation of his method for our advisory work.

| Stage | Move | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Open | Move 10 | Capture what they've done right. Two minutes. Gets them out of defence. |
| Frame | Move 2 + 3 | *"There are three things that decide this. Let me draw them."* |
| Diagnose | Move 1 | Draw the model live. Let them point at where they are. |
| Reframe | Move 5 | One insight, delivered as a picture or comparison — not an argument. |
| Present | Move 6 | One piece at a time. Check in after each. Never dump the whole plan. |
| Broaden | Move 7 | Answer the specific question **and** the principle underneath it. |
| Close | Move 8 | Three options maximum, each doable in days. |
| After | Move 9 | Send notes, a voice memo, and links. Don't re-explain next time. |

**Two structural borrows worth testing in consults:**

- **The gap-widening step** (Triage Call step 4): pick 4–5 dimensions, quantify current vs desired, convert to a single number. Done neutrally, it replaces persuasion with arithmetic — the client argues with the number, not with you.
- **Nested timeframes** (`The 3 Timeframes™`): frame a long decision as year / quarter / month so the client is only ever risking the innermost window. ⚠️ Must stay within our proof guardrails — no income or return promises.

## 4. APPLIED — A VIDEO

His nine-beat shape, one framework per video:

1. **Context** — where I am, what just happened
2. **Promise** — what this does for you, specifically
3. **Name it** — say the framework's name clearly
4. **Draw it** — build the shape on screen
5. **Walk the parts** — one at a time, counted
6. **Role-play** — perform the application in real language
7. **Hand it over** — the template or tool
8. **Proof** — one named example, one specific result
9. **Two doors** — two next steps, warm sign-off

**Rule: one framework per video.** Two frameworks means two videos.

---

## 5. APPLIED — A DEMONSTRATION

`Why · What · How · Now`, with the "how" performed rather than narrated:

1. **Why** — the cost of the current way, in their terms
2. **What** — name the thing and count its parts
3. **How** — *do it live.* Show the real screen, the real script, the real conversation. Interruptions and small failures increase credibility; a rehearsed clean run reduces it
4. **Now** — the one thing they do in the next 48 hours

Pair with Move 6 — pause and check in between segments rather than running the whole demo and asking for questions at the end.

## 7. THE VOICE

**Rhythm.** Short declaratives dominate. Fragments allowed. After any long sentence, a three-word one. *"It's super easy."*

**Transitions.** `So…` opens a section. `Here's the thing.` `Notice how…` `The reality is…`

**Formatting.** Numbered lists for steps · **bold** for key phrases and script lines · *italics* for dialogue speakers · generous white space around role-play · sub-heads with em-dashes.

**Rhetoric.** Self-reflective questions (*"Who's the one with the power?"*) · one strong metaphor per piece, carried through · repetition for reinforcement (*"Don't beg, don't plead"*) · direct commands (*"Lead with 'no' before 'yes'"*).

**Tone.** Authoritative without arrogance. Tactical over theoretical. Warm open, warm close.

**The most under-copied element:** he is **transparent about his own motives** — he tells you *why* he structures something the way he does, mid-teach. It reads as honesty and disarms scepticism far faster than any credibility claim.

**Banned:** hype adjectives, unearned superlatives, motivational filler, anything that survives being deleted.

---

## 8. THE TEST BEFORE ANYTHING SHIPS

1. Can I **draw** it in 60 seconds?
2. Can I **name** it with `The` + 2–3 words?
3. Can I **count** its parts?
4. Does it answer **one** question?
5. Does it ship with a **template**?
6. Would I **say it out loud** in a lift without apologising for it?

Six yeses, or it isn't finished.
