## 1. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH VERSION

Taki Moore sells **the business model a coach should be running**, not coaching skill. His buyer is a coach already earning — stuck at six figures, trading time for money, delivering 1:1 and bespoke. His fix is a business built on **systems and IP** instead of **time and effort**, producing **money, meaning and freedom**. Everything in the company hangs off one three-word spine — **Attract · Convert · Deliver** — which is simultaneously his curriculum, his blog taxonomy, his diagnostic question, and his sales logic. His durable moat is not a funnel; it is a **library of named, drawable, one-page frameworks** that make him the person who gave you the language you now think in.

---

## 2. THE TWO ERAS — READ THIS BEFORE MODELLING ANYTHING

His business has changed shape substantially. Modelling the book without knowing this means modelling a system **he himself abandoned**.

| | **Classic era** (book, ~2016–2022) | **Current era** (~2024–2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Lead gen | Webinars, funnels, appointment funnels | Content volume, especially YouTube. No funnels. |
| Qualification | The Triage Call (10-min phone screen) | **No sales calls at all** |
| Sales | Triage → 1-hour strategy call → close | **"Sell by Chat"** — DM and Google Docs |
| Sales team | 9 people | **0 people** |
| Core metric | Leads, conversion rate | **"Brandwidth"** — minutes of his content consumed |
| Planning | Quarterly / annual | **Six-week cycles** |
| Events | Webinar-led | 3 live events/yr Australia + 3 US |

**Self-reported scale (his own marketing claims — directional only, not verified):** $37M coaching business · 600+ coaches trained past seven figures · $50M+ combined client revenue · every buyer had consumed **at least 47 minutes** of his content before purchasing · ~12 founder-hours/month of content producing ~$1.2M/yr attributable · one workshop cycle at ~$80K tickets + ~$112K/month recurring added · ad spend cut from ~$60K/month to ~$3–4K/month when founder voice replaced produced content.

**Why both eras still matter to us:**
- The **classic era** is the better *teaching* system — the Triage Call, the webinar structure and the 9 Accelerators are concrete, drillable and easy to hand to someone else. Excellent raw material for a curriculum.
- The **current era** is the better *strategic read* — it says that once you have enough content depth, the funnel becomes unnecessary. That is a claim about **trust volume replacing conversion mechanics**.

## 3. THE PRODUCT LADDER

| Rung | Product | Price shape | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | Book, blog, podcast, YouTube, ~5 emails/week | Free / ~$20 | Build **brandwidth**. The book *is* the sales letter. |
| 1 | Free tool (classic: the Triage Call script) | Free opt-in | Captures at sharpest pain. Gives the **real** tool, not a teaser. |
| 2 | Workshop (3-day, e.g. `Clients 3.0`) | Low ticket | Monthly-ish profit centre **and** the enrolment room. |
| 3 | `Black Belt` — group coaching | High-ticket recurring | The core business. 6 figures → $1M. Done-with-you. |
| 4 | `Boardroom` — mastermind | Premium recurring | 7–8 figure operators. Status apex. |

**The shape that matters:** free tool → cheap workshop → recurring program → mastermind. **There is no course. Nothing terminal.** Every rung's job is to make the next rung obvious.

## 4. THE SPINE — ATTRACT · CONVERT · DELIVER

Three words doing four jobs at once. This is the single most copied-badly thing about him: most people take it as a pillar list; he uses it as an operating spine.

| Job | How the spine does it |
|---|---|
| Curriculum | 3 sections of the book, 3 parts of the program |
| Diagnostic | "Which of the three is broken for you?" — the prospect self-diagnoses, and **self-diagnosis is the sale** |
| Content taxonomy | Every blog post files under exactly one |
| Sales logic | The conversation is always about which of the three is costing them |

**The test for any spine:** if a word can't be a workshop day, a diagnosis, a content category and a sales question, it's decoration.

## 5. THE 9 ACCELERATORS

Three per pillar — depth without a new vocabulary to memorise.

**DELIVER** *(he puts this first — see §6)*
1. **Decide Your Model** — escape time-for-money 1:1
2. **Unpack Your IP** — lead groups proactively from your own content instead of reacting to individuals
3. **Harness Talent** — build a virtual team so you work in your strengths

**ATTRACT**
4. **Fill Your Funnel** — a lead system and traffic
5. **Build Authority** — "be a big deal in your space"; makes selling easier and conversion higher
6. **Install Automation** — marketing that runs without you

**CONVERT**
7. **Audition Clients** — assess fit rather than trying to impress
8. **Sell with Webinars** — one-to-many presenting
9. **Rock Events** — live events targeting 30–40% conversion

## 6. DELIVER-FIRST — THE SEQUENCING DECISION

The accelerators are numbered with **Deliver first**, not Attract. He fixes the *model* before turning on traffic, because scaling a broken model breaks it faster.

Practical consequence: a client who hasn't confronted their own economics can't usefully be taught positioning or lead-gen. The model fix is a **gate**, not a module.

## 7. THE THREE PLATEAUS

His diagnostic ladder: **Survival → Stability → Success**.

The buyer locates themselves on a rung *before* any offer appears. Naming the rung makes the gap felt without the coach having to argue for it.

## 8. OPERATING PHILOSOPHY

**Money · Meaning · Freedom.** "As your business gets bigger, your life should get better, not worse." He rejects the lifestyle-vs-empire trade-off.

**Money as "fun tickets."** Revenue reframed as tickets redeemable for experiences — makes profit-seeking feel like a game rather than greed.

**The swimming pool campaign.** Design a specific offer to fund a specific personal goal. (He ran 12-person workshops at his home at $6–8K a head to buy a Land Rover.) Named after John Lennon writing a song to pay for a pool.

**Calendar design.** Book the adventure and the revenue events **first** — the "big rocks" — then fit operations around them. Weekly, he assigns whole days to a single role ("Money Thursday", "Rockstar Friday") to avoid mixing incompatible energies. Target: four days a week, four hours a day.

**Energy over time.** He manages energy, not hours; mismatched work types in one day create friction.

**Unique ability gap.** One hour of founder work in his core competence funds 10–30 hours of team support. First hire was offshore, part-time, minimal criteria.

**Non-delegable:** founder voice, storytelling, point of view. Outsourcing these produces a documented decline curve — see `06_CONTENT_AND_VOICE.md`.

**Client selection as a growth lever.** Only accept perfect-fit clients; respectfully turn away the rest. Misfit clients eat the founder capacity growth requires.

**Price as "moat."** A high price funds ads, team and margin — which is what actually protects the business. Discounting to fill a room removes the protection.

## 9. THE FRAMEWORK-FACTORY MOAT

The real asset is the framework library. Everything is:
- **named** with a definite article (`The Gameplan`, `The Signature System`),
- **counted** (3 Timeframes, 4 Forces, 6 Pills, 9 steps),
- **drawable** in under 60 seconds,
- **trademarked**.

A named framework stops being advice and becomes an asset: ownable, teachable by clients on your behalf, and impossible to un-attribute. Clients repeat his vocabulary, which markets him.
