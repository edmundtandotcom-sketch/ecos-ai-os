# 1. THE BUYER JOURNEY — CLASSIC ERA

The version that is easiest to teach and drill, and the better raw material for a curriculum.

```
CONTENT → LEAD MAGNET → WEBINAR → TRIAGE CALL → STRATEGY CALL → PROGRAM → ASCENSION
```

| Stage | Framework in play | The job |
|---|---|---|
| Content | Four Forces · Weekly Seven | Speak to one quadrant of what actually moves them |
| Lead magnet | *Give the real tool* | The actual Triage Call script, not a teaser. Proof depth exists. |
| Webinar sign-up | The Four Campaigns → **Sign Up** | 4 emails: Problem, Promise, Proof, Reminder |
| Show-up | **Show Up** | Countdown emails, curiosity handout with open loops, teaser video, SMS |
| Webinar | **Pay Up** + Three Belief Targets | Teach and sell simultaneously. Shift belief about you, the product, themselves |
| Post-webinar | **Follow Up** | Four segments, four sequences: buyers · no-shows · early bailouts · watched-didn't-buy |
| Qualification | The Triage Call (9 steps) | ~10 min. Decide fit. Widen the gap. Sell only the *next conversation* |
| Sale | The 6 Magic Pills + The 3 Timeframes | 35–45 min on them first; then 3 emotional pills, 3 logical pills, one at a time; close with nested year/quarter/month |
| Onboard | The On-Ramp | Avatar → Signature System → Money Map → Extra Workday |
| Deliver | Done-With-You + the monthly rhythm | See §2 |

**The three chain-links people miss:**
1. **Show Up is a campaign, not a reminder.** Most events fail here, not in the presentation.
2. **Follow Up is four campaigns, not one.** The "watched but didn't buy" segment is the largest recoverable revenue in the system.
3. **The Triage Call sells a conversation, never the program.** Trying to close on the 10-minute call collapses the whole sequence.

# 2. THE BUYER JOURNEY — CURRENT ERA

```
HIGH-VOLUME CONTENT → BRANDWIDTH THRESHOLD → SELF-SELECTION → SELL BY CHAT → PROGRAM
```

No funnel. No sales calls. No sales team.

The load-bearing idea is **brandwidth**: his stated finding that buyers had consumed **≥47 minutes** of his content before purchasing. If that's the real gate, then the job of marketing isn't to optimise a conversion step — it's to **accumulate consumed minutes**. Everything follows:

- Long-form and binge-ability beat optimised short hooks
- YouTube becomes the centre of gravity
- The offer can sit in a Google Doc in a DM, because trust was built before the conversation started
- 9 salespeople become 0

**Honest read for us:** this is a *late-stage* model. It works because a decade of content and a book already exist. Adopting the current era without the accumulated brandwidth would mean removing the machinery that compensates for not being famous yet. **The classic era is the one to learn from while building; the current era is where it goes.**

# 3. THE DELIVERY YEAR

```
ON-RAMP → MONTHLY RHYTHM (×12, modular) → SESSION MENU → COMMUNITY
```

**The On-Ramp** — four gates before the main program, each a ~10-minute video plus a worksheet, then a 1:1 strategy session, then a first 90-day plan built with a guide:
1. Avatar / target market
2. Signature System (their offer)
3. Money Map (their funnel)
4. Extra Workday (reclaim 8–10 hrs/week)

Everyone enters the program from the same floor.

**The monthly rhythm** — four weeks, four session types:

| Week | Type | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Learn** | Teach something new |
| 2 | **Do** | Bootcamp / work session |
| 3 | **Meet** | Connection |
| 4 | **Mastermind or Plan** | Peer problem-solving or forward planning |

**Modularity is the unlock.** Every month stands alone; no prerequisites. That single design decision converts enrolment from cohort-gated to **continuous** — you can sell every week of the year without breaking anyone's experience. For most programs this is worth more than any marketing improvement.

**Inside any session:** `Connect · Congratulate · Coach`, with the format drawn from the eight-model menu (Teach Webinar, Skill Session, Mastermind, Sprint, Sensei Session, Implementation Session, Hot Seat, Sherpa).

**Inside any 1:1:** `The Gameplan™` — momentum → issues → game plan (recommendations, resources, actions). 15–20 minutes.

**The delivery doctrine underneath all of it:** Done-With-You. Teach the model, hand over the template. Every block ships an artefact.

# 4. THE CONTENT ENGINE

```
WEEKLY SEVEN → PICK 5 BY ENERGY → ONE FRAMEWORK EACH → PUBLISH → BRANDWIDTH
```

1. **Mine** the seven categories weekly: calendar · clients · DMs · community · creations · consumption · circumstances
2. **Select** the five with genuine energy — *energy is the filter, not keyword volume*
3. **Shape** each as one framework, nine beats (context → promise → name it → draw it → walk the parts → role-play → hand it over → proof → two doors)
4. **Publish** ~5×/week, ~30 min each — roughly 12 founder-hours/month
5. **Balance** Artist (voice, opinion — never delegated) against Business (consistency, volume)

**The documented failure mode**, in his words: outsource the founder voice → short-term relief → audience disengagement → sales decline → ad spend up 30–60% with no revenue lift.

⚠️ **This is the sharpest tension between his model and an AI-assisted content operation.** Worth resolving deliberately rather than by default. His position isn't "don't use tools" — it's that opinion, story and point of view are load-bearing and cannot be synthesised without the audience noticing.

# 5. THE BUSINESS OPERATING LAYER

```
BIG ROCKS → THEMED WEEKS → SIX-WEEK CYCLES → THE BIG EASY
```

- **Big rocks first** — the annual challenge and the revenue events go in the calendar before operations
- **Themed weeks** — whole days assigned to one role; don't mix incompatible energies. Target four days, four hours
- **Six-week cycles** replace quarterly planning — short horizons build momentum
- **The Big Easy** (impact × ease) decides what goes into each cycle
- **Unique ability gap** — hire to protect the founder's core hour
- **Client selection + price as moat** — refuse non-fits; price high enough to fund ads, team and margin

# 6. THE FIVE STRUCTURAL DECISIONS WORTH STEALING

Independent of his market, his niche or his era:

1. **Deliver before Attract.** Fix the model before adding traffic. Scaling a broken model breaks it faster.
2. **Modular months.** Kill prerequisites and enrolment becomes continuous.
3. **Sell the next conversation, not the program.** Every step's only job is to earn the next step.
4. **Done-with-you = teach + template.** The template is what makes group delivery produce 1:1 outcomes.
5. **One spine, four jobs.** Three words that serve as curriculum, diagnostic, content taxonomy and sales logic — or they're decoration.
