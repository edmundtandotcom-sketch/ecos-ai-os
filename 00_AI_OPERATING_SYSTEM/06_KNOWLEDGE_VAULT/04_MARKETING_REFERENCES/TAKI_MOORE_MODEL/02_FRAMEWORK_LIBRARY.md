# A. DIAGNOSE

## A1 · `Attract · Convert · Deliver` — the spine
**Shape:** three boxes, left to right.
**Answers:** "Which part of my business is actually broken?"
**Draw it:** three boxes in a row. Ask which one leaks. That's the diagnosis and the sale in one move.
**Why it works:** three is the maximum a person holds without notes. Self-diagnosis converts better than being told.

## A2 · The 9 Accelerators — 3×3
**Shape:** three columns, three rows.
**Parts:** DELIVER — Decide Your Model · Unpack Your IP · Harness Talent. ATTRACT — Fill Your Funnel · Build Authority · Install Automation. CONVERT — Audition Clients · Sell with Webinars · Rock Events.
**Answers:** "Where exactly inside the broken part?"
**Draw it:** the three spine boxes, then three bullets under each.
**Note:** Deliver is deliberately first. Fix the model before adding traffic.

## A3 · The Three Plateaus
**Shape:** a staircase of three steps.
**Parts:** Survival → Stability → Success.
**Answers:** "Where am I right now?"
**Draw it:** three rising steps, ask them to point. Naming the rung makes the gap felt without arguing for it.

## A4 · `The Big Easy` *(current era)*
**Shape:** 2×2 matrix — Impact (low/high) × Ease (hard/easy).
**Answers:** "Of everything I could do, what do I do first?"
**Draw it:** quadrant; plot every idea; work the high-impact/easy corner first.
**Use:** pairs with six-week cycles. Excellent generic prioritisation tool for any client.

# B. POSITION & OFFER

## B1 · `The Signature System™` — Problem + Promise + Process
**Shape:** left box → arrow with steps → right box.
**Parts:**
- **Problem** — every frustration and fear of the ideal client (he literally uses one post-it per item)
- **Promise** — where they want to be; short-term wants *and* long-term aspirations
- **Process** — **6–8 named steps** bridging the two
**Answers:** "What exactly do I sell?"
**Draw it:** Problem box, Promise box, then 6–8 numbered steps on the arrow between them.
**His line:** *"Stop selling conversations and logistics. Start selling promises to problems with a specific process."*
**Note:** this is arguably his most transferable single framework — it converts any expertise into a sellable, teachable system.

## B2 · `The Four Forces`
**Shape:** 2×2 quadrant. Vertical axis Away↔Toward; horizontal Immediate↔Future.
**Parts:** **Frustrations** (away/now) · **Fears** (away/future) · **Wants** (toward/now) · **Aspirations** (toward/future).
**Answers:** "What actually moves this person?"
**Draw it:** fold a page in four. Fill each quadrant in their words. Every hook, headline, ad and email comes from one quadrant.
**Note:** this is his research-to-copy pipeline in one square.

## B3 · `The Magic Model` *(current era)*
**Shape:** one diagram, client-specific.
**Rule:** every client builds **one** visual model that explains their unique value — then uses it across marketing, content and program design.
**Answers:** "What's the one picture that explains what I do?"
**Note:** the framework-factory principle applied to the client, not the coach.

## B4 · The On-Ramp
**Shape:** four sequential gates before the main program.
**Parts:** Avatar/Target Market → Signature System → Money Map (funnel/marketing) → Extra Workday (reclaim 8–10 hrs/week).
**Answers:** "What must be true before you can benefit from the program?"
**Note:** each is a 10-min video + worksheet, then a 1:1 strategy session. Guarantees every member starts from the same floor.

# C. ATTRACT

## C1 · `Brandwidth` *(current era — the strategic idea)*
**Definition:** the metric is not time on list or lead count — it is **minutes of your content consumed**. He found buyers had consumed **≥47 minutes** before purchasing.
**Answers:** "Why isn't my audience buying?" → they haven't consumed enough of you yet.
**Consequence:** the job of content is not to generate a click. It is to **accumulate consumed minutes**. Long-form and binge-ability beat optimised short hooks.
**This is the idea that let him delete his funnel and his sales team.**

## C2 · The Weekly Seven — content mining
**Shape:** seven-item checklist, run weekly.
**Parts:** calendar · clients · DMs · community · creations · consumption · circumstances.
**Answers:** "What do I make content about this week?"
**Rule:** scan all seven, pick the five with genuine energy. **Energy is the filter, not keyword volume.**

## C3 · Dangerous + Prolific
**Parts:** *Dangerous* = a strong opinion you actually want to argue. *Prolific* = published consistently.
**Answers:** "Why is my content invisible?" — usually because it is safe, not because it is rare.

## C4 · Artist vs Business
**Shape:** two forces in tension.
**Parts:** **Artist** — expression, originality, authenticity; drives connection; cannot be delegated. **Business** — consistency, volume, schedule; drives reach.
**Answers:** "Why did my content stop working when I systemised it?"

# D. CONVERT

## D1 · `The Triage Call™` — 9 steps *(classic era)*
**Shape:** numbered line, ~10 minutes.
**Purpose:** decide fit. **Not** to sell. Ends by booking the real conversation.
1. **Take control of the first minute** — confirm the booking, state the 10-minute limit, explain you'll ask questions then decide fit, promise to refer them elsewhere if it's a no
2. **Why now / why me** — why now is the critical one; probe vague answers
3. **Their business in 60 seconds** — who do you help, what problem, how delivered
4. **Widen the gap** — pick 4–5 categories (income, clients, hours), quantify current vs desired, convert to an annual dollar figure. *"That's $300,000 a year."*
5. **What's missing or broken?** — let them self-diagnose
6. **What do you need from me?** — hold them in the tension; don't reveal the solution
7. **When?** — "later thing or sooner thing?" Listen for tone/word mismatch. Rate 1–10 if unclear
8. **Problem check-in** — reflect back 2–3 problems until they say *"yes, that's me"*
9. **Show them the door** — a genuine no-fit referral, **or** book the 1-hour strategy call
**Tonality rule:** speak slowly, pause between questions, stay "chilled". Rushing makes it sound scripted and kills trust.
**Power framing:** *"You're the one with the ticket to Hollywood"* — they're in the audition seat.

## D2 · The First Question
**Rule:** open with something innocuous, simple, easy to answer, and informative enough to steer the conversation. His model: a restaurant asking *"Do you have any food allergies?"* — it signals care **and** qualifies at the same time.
**Answers:** "How do I open without it feeling like an interrogation?"

## D3 · `The 6 Magic Pills™`
**Shape:** six doses, delivered one at a time, with a check-in between each.
**Parts:** **3 pills on "why they need it"** (emotional, top) · **3 pills on "how it works"** (logical, below).
**Method:** give one piece of information about your offer, then check in — *"Is this making sense so far?"* / *"Are you comfortable with everything so far?"* Never data-dump.
**Answers:** "How do I present my offer without overwhelming them?"
**Sequencing:** spend 35–45 minutes on *them* first — lead them, stretch the gap between where they are and want to be, surface what's blocking them. Only then does the offer land, because now it's answering something.

## D4 · `The 3 Timeframes™`
**Shape:** three nested windows, largest to smallest.
**Parts:** **Year** = the commitment (12-month goals imply a 12-month engagement) · **Quarter** = the ROI (first 90 days repay the year) · **Month** = the risk reversal (30-day walk-away, "cancel, refund, walk away as friends").
**Answers:** "How do I make a big commitment feel safe?"
**Draw it:** three boxes nested inside each other. The buyer is only ever risking the innermost one.
⚠️ **Our guardrail:** the quarter-ROI move must never become an income promise in our market.

## D5 · The Four Campaigns — events/webinars
**Shape:** four sequential campaigns, not one event.
**Parts:**
- **Sign Up** — target via Four Forces; 4 emails: Problem, Promise, Proof, Reminder (short: Mon–Thu; long: staggered 10 days for higher registration)
- **Show Up** — personality-led countdowns, a curiosity handout with deliberate open loops, teaser video, SMS reminders (~+10% show rate)
- **Pay Up** — content that **teaches and sells simultaneously**; every element must advance the sale, deliver value, hold engagement and prove capability
- **Follow Up** — four segments, each with its own sequence: **buyers** · **no-shows** · **early bailouts** · **watched-but-didn't-buy** (objection-away videos on the top 3 concerns)
**Answers:** "Why did my event underperform?" — almost always Show Up or Follow Up, not the presentation.

## D6 · The Three Belief Targets
**Parts:** what they must believe about **you** · about **the product** · about **themselves**. Plus pre-handled objections and pre-killed alternatives.
**Answers:** "What has to be true in their head before they can buy?"
**Note:** "about themselves" is the most-skipped and usually the real blocker.

## D7 · `Sell by Chat` *(current era)*
**Method:** no scheduled calls. Conversation happens in DMs, with the offer laid out in a shared Google Doc. Prospects self-select. Zero sales team.
**Precondition:** only works on top of high brandwidth (C1). Without consumed minutes, this is just a slow way to get ignored.

# E. DELIVER

## E1 · Done-For-You / DIY / Done-With-You
**Shape:** three-point spectrum.
**Parts:** *Done-for-you* — great results, burns you out, won't scale. *DIY* — protects your time, weak results, no proof. *Done-with-you* — **teach the model + hand over the template.**
**His line:** *"It's not 'I do it' or 'you do it', it's let's do it together."*
**Operating rule:** every teaching block ships a template. That is what makes group delivery produce 1:1-grade outcomes.

## E2 · `The Gameplan™` — the coaching session, 15–20 min
**Shape:** three phases.
1. **Build on momentum** — *"Since we last talked, what wins have you had? What progress have you made?"* Future-focused accountability, not dwelling on missed homework.
2. **Unpack the issues** — clients surface 20–30 items; dig to the core few.
3. **Get a game plan** — three parts: **Recommendations** (your strategic call) · **Resources** (point to existing material — never re-teach live) · **Actions** — *"Between now and our next meeting, what are three actions you'd like to complete?"* then confirm feasibility and timing.
**Follow-up:** send notes, a voice memo, and the resource links.
**Answers:** "How do I run a session in 20 minutes instead of an hour?"

## E3 · Momentum-Based Coaching — Past → Present → Future
**Shape:** three stages, two steps each.
- **Past** — (1) capture wins, list five (2) celebrate them emotionally. *Goal: move them from problem-dwelling into a resourceful state.*
- **Present** — (1) locate the actual issue by mapping the process (2) **pop the insight** using a model or story to reframe. *Goal: find the one fixable problem.*
- **Future** — (1) simplify the path to three options (2) **shrink the scope** — tasks completable in hours or days, not months. *Goal: action.*
**The Pringles model:** get them to do one small thing; appetite follows.
**His critique of normal coaching:** letting clients tell too much of their story and burying them in resources.

## E4 · `Connect · Congratulate · Coach`
**Shape:** three beats under every group session.
- **Connect** — everyone feels safe, noticed, engaged
- **Congratulate** — wins and lessons shared
- **Coach** — teach one thing they can use going forward
**Answers:** "What's the minimum structure for any group session?"

## E5 · The Eight Group Coaching Models
A menu — different session types keep a long program from going flat.

| # | Model | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Teach Webinar** | Why it matters → What they need to know (3–5 points) → How to do it → Now (next step) |
| 2 | **Skill Session** | Intro, then 3 skill drills, each: set up → run → debrief |
| 3 | **Mastermind** | 10-min setup to find the right person + challenge → breakouts → full-group debrief |
| 4 | **Sprint** | 50 minutes of focused work on one thing, bookended by commitment and celebration |
| 5 | **Sensei Session** | Guest expert teaches 15–20 min → 45 min Q&A on real client problems |
| 6 | **Implementation Session** | 15 min teach → complete a worksheet together → discuss results |
| 7 | **Hot Seat** | Interview a successful client: frustrations → what they did → what's next; group observes |
| 8 | **Sherpa** | Community-led: connection, wins, coaching that expands to the whole group |

## E6 · `Why · What · How · Now` — the teach structure
**Parts:** **Why** does it matter (give them a reason to care) → **What** do they need to know (3–5 points, no more) → **How** to do it → **Now** what's the next step.
**Answers:** "How do I structure any teaching block?"
**Note:** the most portable framework in the whole library. Works for a consult segment, a video, a slide, an objection answer, a demo.

## E7 · The Sherpa Five Keys — running a group so nobody checks out
1. **Kick off with competence** — surface wins early; reframe non-winners as learners
2. **Get down to business** — *"What's the one thing you really want help with?"*
3. **Pop insights and actions** — one insight + three actions. Avoid the "deli counter" (serving clients one at a time while everyone else waits)
4. **Expand the application** — **chunk up** the question so the whole room benefits. The "DJ party" principle: one request, everyone dances
5. **Make sure they're seen** — acknowledge people throughout. *"Until you see me, I did not exist."*

## E8 · The Monthly Rhythm
**Shape:** four weeks, four session types.
**Parts:** **Learn** (teach something new) → **Do** (bootcamp / work session) → **Meet** (connection) → **Mastermind or Plan**.
**Answers:** "What's the cadence of a year-long program?"

## E9 · Modular 12-Month Design
**Rule:** every month stands alone. No prerequisites. Anyone can join in any month without disrupting the cohort or missing context.
**Why:** enrolment becomes continuous instead of cohort-gated — the single biggest constraint removal in program design.

## E10 · Six-Week Cycles *(current era)*
Replaces quarterly/annual planning. Short horizons build momentum and confidence; pairs with The Big Easy for choosing what goes in the cycle.

# F. OPERATE

## F1 · Big Rocks Calendar
Book the adventure and the revenue events **first**; fit operations around them. Yearly: one defining challenge (he uses the Japanese *masogi* idea — one big annual undertaking).

## F2 · The Themed Week
Assign whole days to a single role — "Money Thursday", "Rockstar Friday" — because mixing incompatible energies in one day creates friction. Target: four days a week, four hours a day.

## F3 · Unique Ability Gap
Hire to protect the founder's core competence. One founder-hour in that zone funds 10–30 hours of support.

## F4 · Fun Tickets + The Swimming Pool Campaign
Money = tickets redeemable for experiences. Design a specific offer to fund a specific personal goal, which makes the revenue target concrete and the work energising.

## F5 · Client Selection + Price as Moat
Only perfect-fit clients; respectfully refuse the rest. High price funds ads, team and margin — the actual protection. Discounting to fill a room removes it.

# G. THE SIX RULES BEHIND EVERY FRAMEWORK

1. **Counted** — a number promises completion and tells the listener when to stop worrying they missed something
2. **Drawable in 60 seconds** — if you can't draw it while talking, it isn't finished
3. **Named with a definite article** — `The Client X-Ray`, not "client analysis"
4. **Answers one question** — two questions means two frameworks
5. **Has a shape** — quadrant, ladder, spectrum, nested boxes, numbered line
6. **Ships with a template** — the framework explains; the template is what they actually use

**The four shapes that do most of the work:** quadrant (two competing variables) · ladder (where you are now) · numbered line (a sequence to run) · nested boxes (making commitment safe).
