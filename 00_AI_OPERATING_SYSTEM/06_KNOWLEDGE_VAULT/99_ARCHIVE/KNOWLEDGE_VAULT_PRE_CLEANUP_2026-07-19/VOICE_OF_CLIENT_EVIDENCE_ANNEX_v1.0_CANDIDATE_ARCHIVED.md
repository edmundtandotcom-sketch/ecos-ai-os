## PART D — Principles the field data CHALLENGES

> Where the 166-consult voice complicates or contradicts the library's model. Flagged for Edmund; the library is **not** edited by this annex.

1. **[CROSS-DOC CONFLICT] HDB audience is "retired" in theory but dominant in the field.** The library states MAS "no longer uses HDB-related pathways as a core audience" and removed HDB-upgrader archetypes. Yet the field data is saturated with HDB-origin owners and HDB-vs-condo deliberation (Joey, Dee & Andy, Felix, James, Rudresh, Adeline & Lewis). The `03_TRAPS...v2.1` doc still carries an HDB/EC upgrade-window trap and lists these households In-Audience. **Reconcile:** either formalise a "qualified HDB→private transition" carve-out, or accept the field mismatch and re-scope Trap 7. *Both live docs currently disagree.*

2. **Comfort Preserver ≠ complacent; often it's fear, not comfort.** The library frames CP-05 as stability-seeking and cautious ("current home is fine"). The field shows the same inaction is frequently driven by **acute fear** — monthly-commitment dread and crash anxiety — not contentment: ★ "if the property market crash... I will have a heart attack" (*Mindy*), ★ "$7,000 cash every single month, without fail... quite scary" (*Ming Xian & Xing Fang*). **Implication:** with many Comfort Preservers, lead with **downside-control and payment-safety**, not "your options are shrinking" (which can read as pressure the library itself warns against).

3. **Opportunity Seeker's real risk is often paralysis, not recklessness.** The library's stated CP-04 risk is "chases opportunities without enough risk discipline." In the field, the growth-oriented buyer is just as often **frozen by timing fear**: ★ "If I sell high, I also buy high, man" (*Ade*); "we're also thinking who can buy it at five mil" (*Bernard & Michelle*). **Implication:** the "control the downside" CTA is right, but the more common failure mode to address is inaction, not over-reach.

4. **The library is calm; the client is not.** The library models decision-style as a rational taxonomy. The dominant field register is **raw, somatic emotion** — "heart attack", "cannot sleep", "life savings, gone tomorrow", "one hit, one chance", "kill you". **Implication:** style-based calibration works *after* emotional safety is established. Add an emotional-acknowledgement step ahead of style-matched logic in Discovery/Diagnosis.

5. **"Too much information" is a decision style trigger AND a trap.** CP-01 Analytical is fed with data — but the field warns that more input actively *reduces* confidence: ★ "the more you read... the more indecisive you may be" (*Lucas*); ★ "I can look at PropertyGuru all day long, but still... I don't know what the reality" (*Brenda*). **Implication:** for Analytical buyers, supply a **filter/framework**, not more raw information — consistent with the library's own "do not drown them" note, but the field shows this is the primary failure, not a secondary risk.
