## 1B. The Villain: The One-Property Trap

Every category needs one enemy. Ours is not a rival agent — it is a belief the client already carries.

> 🟥 **THE VILLAIN:** The One-Property Trap — the inherited belief that buying one good home, staying comfortable, and slowly paying it down is the safest path — when for an ambitious climber it can quietly become the reason their net asset value stops climbing. It sounds responsible. It feels safe. It looks mature. But it turns the first property into the finish line instead of the first step.

The reframe we repeat until it sticks: **the most dangerous property move is not always buying wrong — sometimes it is stopping after one.**

### Five Supporting Villains
- **Buy-To-Stay Thinking** — deciding on comfort, layout, school, MRT while ignoring exit, equity, name-count, supply and next move. Fine for a home; dangerous for your biggest asset.
- **Agent-Led Transaction Thinking** — starting with projects and units. The agent shows you the next property; the Ladder shows you the next move.
- **The Just-Buy-Second Myth** — believing wealth comes from buying another property. Buying second is not the strategy; sequencing second is.
- **Exit-Later Buying** — buying first, thinking about the exit buyer only after the money is committed. If the exit is not planned before entry, it is a trap.
- **Project-First Advice** — believing the project is the strategy. A good project in the wrong sequence is still a bad move.

## 4. The Five Steps in Detail

### Step 1: Map the Position

**PURPOSE:** Before any move, we establish the client's true numbers through our full assessment — not their story. This mirrors the diagnostic worksheet we already run: current value, sale proceeds nett of legal & commission, cash and CPF split, total deployable funds, and loan capacity across routes.

**CAPABILITY SHIFT:** From *a rough sense of "my place is worth something"* → **a full numbers position: nett sale proceeds, cash + CPF funds, total funds, and loan capacity across purchase routes.**

**KEY QUESTIONS:**
- What does the property realistically sell for, and what are the cash proceeds nett of legal and commission?
- What are the total funds available — cash plus CPF, plus proceeds?
- What is the loan capacity, and what does it support at 75% across 30 years?
- What does each route (combined vs split-two) require in downpayment, balance funds, and instalment?

**KEY ACTIONS:**
- Run the full assessment sheet: value, nett proceeds, cash, CPF, total funds.
- Work out loan capacity and combined loan across scenarios.
- Cost each route: downpayment, balance funds, monthly instalment.
- State the position back in clean numbers the client recognises.

**OUTPUT:** **The Position Map** — the client's real funds, loan capacity, and costed routes on one view.

**COMPLETION STANDARD:** Complete when the client can see their nett funds, loan capacity, and what each route costs in downpayment and instalment — in real figures.

**COMMON FAILURE MODE:** *Advising on the story ("my place has gone up a lot") instead of the after-cost numbers. Optimism is not a position.*

---

### Step 2: Set the Direction

**PURPOSE:** A climb needs a defined direction — and the direction is option-value, not door-count. This step aligns the client's age, runway, affordability, risk level, objective and future plans to choose the route: one combined purchase above $3m, or split into two properties.

**CAPABILITY SHIFT:** From *a vague wish to "own more property"* → **a chosen route (combined vs split-two) aligned to age, runway, risk and objective — aimed at option-value, not property-count.**

**KEY QUESTIONS:**
- Given age and runway, how much time do we have to leverage loan tenure?
- Combined purchase (>$3m) or split into two — which fits their affordability and risk comfort?
- What is the real objective — retirement options, next-gen, or both?
- If they can own two immediately, do we execute both now or stagger one then another in a couple of years?

**KEY ACTIONS:**
- Align age, runway, affordability, risk level, objective, future plans through the diagnostic questions.
- Present both routes with their risk and safety trade-offs.
- Find the comfort ground where the client will actually proceed.
- Decide immediate-two vs staggered based on capacity and risk.

**OUTPUT:** **The Direction Statement** — chosen route + why it fits their age, runway and risk + the option-value target.

**COMPLETION STANDARD:** Complete when the client can state their route (combined or split-two), why it fits their situation, and the option-value outcome it aims at.

**COMMON FAILURE MODE:** *Defaulting to "buy two" because it earns more — when their age, risk or affordability points to a single stronger move first. The route must fit the climber.*

---

### Step 3: Sequence the Moves

**PURPOSE:** The step we own. The typical first move — when they hold HDB or an under-performing asset — is to sell and reposition into private, timed to their age. But the real first move is conceptual: we install the leverage principle — age, loan, income, tenure and time working together — so every move after compounds net asset value toward more options later.

**CAPABILITY SHIFT:** From *a single guessed transaction* → **an ordered move-list built on leverage of age, loan, income, tenure and time.**

**KEY QUESTIONS:**
- What is the first move — and does selling the HDB / under-performing asset unlock the climb?
- How do age, loan, income, tenure and time combine to grow net asset value from the first move?
- If owning two is possible now, do we execute immediately or stagger — one, then another in a couple of years?
- What sequence turns a higher asset base into more options at retirement and for the next generation?

**KEY ACTIONS:**
- Install the leverage concept first — age → loan → income → tenure → time.
- Identify the unlocking first move (typically sell HDB / under-performer → private).
- Order the moves and decide immediate-two vs staggered.
- Frame the endpoint as options — retirement, next-gen — not property count.

**OUTPUT:** **The Move Sequence** — the ordered moves, with the leverage logic and timing behind each. The signature artefact.

**COMPLETION STANDARD:** Complete when the client sees move 1, move 2 in order, understands the leverage logic, and knows why this order grows their options.

**COMMON FAILURE MODE:** *Presenting moves as a menu instead of an order. Options create paralysis; the sequence creates action. The order is the product.*

---

### Step 4: Research the Exit First

**PURPOSE:** Exit is not an afterthought — it is buy-side research. Before we buy, we secure as many selection points as possible so the exit is easier and the climb to the next level is smoother. This is the research that makes safe motion real.

**CAPABILITY SHIFT:** From *buying on hope that it works out* → **selecting each asset on exit-strength — transformation, entry price, exit strategy, buyer pool, rentability — before entry.**

**KEY QUESTIONS:**
- What transformation / growth story does this location and asset carry?
- Is the entry price right, and what is the realistic exit strategy?
- Who is the future audience we sell to — and is the unit rentable in the meantime?
- How does it map against market movement, income, population, resale vs new launch, freehold vs leasehold, and layout?

**KEY ACTIONS:**
- Research transformation changes, entry price and exit strategy for each candidate.
- Assess buyer pool and rentability.
- Map against market movement, population, resale/new-launch, tenure and layout.
- Use estimated holding period as a checkpoint only — never a hard stop.

**OUTPUT:** **The Selection Research** — the exit-strength case for each asset, decided before entry.

**COMPLETION STANDARD:** Complete when each intended purchase has a researched exit case — transformation, price, buyer pool, rentability — before we commit.

**COMMON FAILURE MODE:** *Researching the entry brilliantly and the exit never. Buying without an exit case is how people get stuck at the first step.*

---

### Step 5: Execute & Repeat

**PURPOSE:** A climb is a compounding sequence over the client's runway — not one move. This step turns a nervous one-off into a monitored relationship, so we know when the next move is due and the client keeps climbing toward more options.

**CAPABILITY SHIFT:** From *one nervous transaction, then silence* → **a monitored, repeatable climb where each move sets up the next.**

**KEY QUESTIONS:**
- Is this move executing on plan — or has the market or their situation shifted?
- What has changed in policy, rates or life that alters the sequence?
- When is the next move due, and what triggers it?
- Are we still on track toward the option-value endpoint?

**KEY ACTIONS:**
- Execute the current move against the sequence and selection research.
- Monitor policy, rate and personal-situation triggers.
- Review and re-sequence on a set cadence.
- Prepare the next move before it is forced.

**OUTPUT:** **The Climb Cadence** — the review rhythm that keeps the sequence current and the next move ready.

**COMPLETION STANDARD:** Complete when the client has completed a move on plan and knows what triggers the next — the climb is a relationship, not an event.

**COMMON FAILURE MODE:** *Treating the first successful move as the finish. One move is a transaction; the repeated climb is the wealth — and the options.*

## 4B. The Category Law: Exit Before Entry™

Step 4 is a step in the sequence. But it is also bigger than a step — it is the one law we repeat until the market associates it with us. Promote it out of the sequence and say it everywhere.

> 🟨 **THE LAW:** Exit Before Entry™ — no property move is considered safe until the exit buyer, exit timing, downside buffer, and next-move impact are understood *before* committing. Most buyers enter first and worry about exit later. We reverse it.

Simple, visual, contrarian, and trademarkable. It should appear in every campaign, every VSL, and on the wall of every strategy session. It is Step 4 said as a commandment.

## 5. The Client Takeaway: Position & Direction Summary

Currently the diagnostic is verbal. The upgrade: a **one-page Position & Direction Summary** the client takes home — enough to feel clear and committed, not enough to execute alone. **Give them the map; keep the compass.**

### What Goes On The Page

| Section | What It Contains |
|---|---|
| **Where you are now** | Their real numbers stated back cleanly: nett proceeds, total funds, loan capacity. (From Step 1.) |
| **Where we are climbing to** | The option-value outcome — more options for later life, retirement, next-gen — from a higher asset base. |
| **The route we discussed** | Combined-purchase vs split-two, at direction level only — not the deal mechanics. |
| **What must be true to move safely** | The safety points we check: exit-strength, affordability comfort, risk alignment. |
| **Your next move — and what we drive** | Names explicitly that sequencing, timing and selection research need us. The compass stays with the advisor. |

> 🟦 **WHAT IT LEAVES OUT (on purpose):** No sequence timing. No project selection. No exit research. That is the paid work. They cannot DIY from this page — but they leave with something real, which builds trust and makes you the organised advisor versus every agent who gives them nothing.

*This summary is a byproduct of the discussion, not extra work — it is the discussion points written down. Keep it to one page. Branded, clean, their numbers, our direction.*
