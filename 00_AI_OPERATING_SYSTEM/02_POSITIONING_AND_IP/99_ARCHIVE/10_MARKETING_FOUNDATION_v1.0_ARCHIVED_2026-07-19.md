## 1. Category, core belief and naming

**Category:** The Second Property Ladder™

**Direct desire:** help an existing owner work towards a second property.

**Core belief (category level):** Property was never the goal. Options were.
**Core belief (selection level):** A good property can still be the wrong property for the household's plan.

**Honest advisory position:** a second property is not automatically the correct recommendation. Valid routes include building towards two, strengthening one property first, restructuring, preparing and waiting, or a no-go when the evidence does not support a move.

## 2. Campaign architecture — two doors, one backend

Two campaign doors, not two separate businesses.

### Door A — Second Property

**Audience:** joint-name private-property owners; suitable EC owners; DINK couples; couples considering decoupling; existing owners actively wanting the next property.

**Core question:** *How can we own the second property, and what must happen first?*

**Public wording:** *Already own one property? See whether you can get the next one—and what must happen first.*

**Primary CTA:** Check My Second-Property Route

### Door B — HDB/EC Next Route

**Audience:** HDB owners with an estimated value of approximately **S$800,000+**; EC owners with an estimated value of approximately **S$1.5 million+**; households deciding whether to keep, sell, upgrade, restructure or build towards two.

**Core question:** *What should our current HDB or EC unlock next?*

**Public qualification callout:** *Own an HDB worth around S$800K+ or an EC worth around S$1.5M+?*

**Primary CTA:** Compare My Keep, Sell or Upgrade Routes

Property value is a campaign filter, not final qualification. Final suitability depends on equity, income, funds, ownership, age, loan runway, timeline, alignment and willingness to proceed.

*(The older S$1.3M EC figure appearing in early workshop drafts is superseded — S$1.5M is the only value used anywhere in this package.)*

### Landing-page architecture

- Exactly two message-matched landing pages — one per door, not one per ad or per segment.
- Multiple ad angles may lead to the same page; the page represents the common destination, the ads represent the different thoughts that lead people there.
- Both pages feed the same backend: one Second Property Readiness Check™, one GHL contact database, one pipeline, one Second Property Strategy Session™, one Second Property Route Map™, one implementation and review system.

## 3. Awareness model (locked 5-stage)

Internal campaign-planning stages only — do not publicly claim this replaces Eugene Schwartz's original model, and do not use "Unaware" as a stage (superseded by Desire-Aware).

| Stage | What they know | Messaging job | Example line |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Desire-Aware** | Wants another property, rental income, more options or better use of current equity. | Make the outcome feel possible; show what they may gain; reveal that the current property may be the starting point, not the finish line. | "Your first property may not be the finish line. It could be the starting point for your next one." |
| **2. Problem-Aware** | Recognises names, timing, funds, current property or wrong-order risk as obstacles. | Name the obstacle; show the consequence; create urgency without exaggerating; explain that doing nothing is also a decision. | "Your property may have increased in value—but if it has not created your next move, the gain may still be sitting idle." |
| **3. Solution-Aware** | Knows tactics exist: decoupling, upgrading, selling one and buying two, refinancing, buying under one name. | Challenge the tactic-first belief; show the same tactic produces different results for different households; explain why route, order and selection matter. | "Decoupling can free one name. It cannot tell you whether the property bought under that name is the right one." |
| **4. Product-Aware** | Knows the Second Property Ladder™ and its specific process exist — including *why* the process is different (folded in from the "Mechanism-Aware" messaging job). | Demonstrate how the process works; show sample outputs; show rejected options; show case studies; make the service tangible. | "We first define what your next property must achieve. Then we reject the options that fail the plan before building the shortlist." |
| **5. Decision / Most-Aware** | Is deciding whether to engage; needs proof, scope, process and confidence. | Remove final uncertainty; establish credibility; show engagement steps; demonstrate results; make the CTA clear. | "See how we mapped one couple's route, rejected 14 unsuitable options and narrowed the market to three properties that protected their next move." |

**Marketing principle:** desire attracts, problems create urgency. The strongest campaigns connect *desired future + obstacle + credible new path.*

## 4. Value proposition

**Central belief:** a property is not right because it is a good project. It is right only when it performs the job required by the household's next two to three moves.

**Core line:** *Your next property gets a job description before it gets a name.*

This is stronger than competing on entry price, location, project quality, data-driven recommendations or "personalised advice" — the basic expectation every advisor claims.

| Dimension | Generic professional | Distinguished professional |
|---|---|---|
| Who they serve | Anyone with the general problem | A tightly defined case or client type |
| Decision they own | Broad advice | One important, difficult decision |
| Diagnostic lens | General discovery questions | A recognisable standard for judging the case |
| What they refuse | Tries to serve everyone | Clearly excludes unsuitable cases and options |
| Tangible work | Advice and recommendations | Specific tests, records, reports and implementation |
| Proof | Testimonials saying they were helpful | Cases showing the diagnosis, rejected options, decision and result |

The decision this business owns: *for people who already own one meaningful property, what must their next property do so it strengthens — not blocks — the move after that?*

## 5. The three routes (single canonical naming — Edmund's ruling U-3)

The Second Property Route Map™ shows three side-by-side routes. This naming is used everywhere — internally and client-facing. It supersedes the older "Hold Two / Trade Up First / Prepare Before Moving" naming, which is retired from all use.

**Route A — Keep and Add**
Keep the current property, restructure if relevant, acquire another. Covers: capital required, monthly exposure, ownership implications, sequence, risks, what the next property must achieve.

**Route B — Sell and Rebuild**
Sell the current property, release funds, buy one or potentially two. Covers: sequence, timing, temporary housing issue, affordability, future flexibility.

**Route C — Strengthen One First**
Sell or reposition into one stronger property first; protect future borrowing and optionality; define when a second-property route becomes viable.

These three map onto the broader honest-advisory routes in Section 1 (build towards two, strengthen one first, restructure, prepare and wait, no-go) — a no-go or prepare/wait outcome is delivered as a variant of Route C or as a "not yet" result, never as a fourth branded route.

## 6. Mechanism boundary (public vs backend)

**Share publicly:** how the route is assessed; how the property's job is defined; common rejection principles; anonymised examples; sample redacted outputs; why popular properties can fail a particular plan; how new-launch and resale are compared; why future-buyer affordability matters.

**Keep for engaged clients:** the exact ranked shortlist; complete project and unit research; personalised calculations; detailed scoring and weighting; negotiation and execution plan; unit, stack and price recommendation.

**Backend-only names — never surface to the public:** REI Method, ProDeck, Ascent Model, Trigger Questions, RAM, internal diagnostic codes. The customer never needs to remember them.

**Public selection process (verbatim, locked):**

> Define the Job → Set the Search Boundaries → Remove Weak Fits → Compare the Survivors → Recommend the Best Fit

Do not create another branded framework at this stage, and do not substitute the shorter 3-step or 4-step variants that appear in earlier workshop drafts — this 5-step wording is the only public version.

## 7. Angle families (12 angles)

Two doors × six angle families each. These are twelve psychological entry points feeding two landing pages — not twelve funnels.

Every angle must contain six parts: a recognisable situation, a thought they already have, a problem no one has clearly named, a believable consequence, a new way of seeing the decision, and a low-friction next action.

### Door A — Second Property

| Code | Angle | Hook | CTA |
|---|---|---|---|

| A2 | Both names trapped | Both names inside one property can feel safe—until you want the next one. | See What Our Name Structure Allows |
| A3 | Decoupling is not the strategy | Freeing one name is not the strategy. The real question is what that name must achieve next. | Check Whether Decoupling Makes Sense |
| A4 | The shrinking window | Your income may rise. Your available loan years and family flexibility do not stay unchanged forever. | See What Waiting Could Change |

| Code | Angle | Hook | CTA |

### Emotional message structure

For each angle, define seven items: what they say publicly, what they privately worry about, what they want, what they assume, what that assumption may cost, the new perspective, and the next action. This is the working template for every ad, script and landing-page block — do not skip straight to headline copy without it.

## 8. Proof architecture

Classify proof by what it proves before building ads. Use at least these eight categories:

1. Went from one property to multiple properties
2. Sold and moved into one stronger property
3. Decoupled and used the freed name correctly
4. HDB/EC owner who created a clearer route
5. Client advised not to buy immediately
6. Avoided a wrong project or wrong sequence
7. Increased options or borrowing flexibility
8. Client gained clarity even before transacting

Proof must demonstrate judgement, sequence, disqualification, risk control, outcome and implementation — never profit alone.

## 10. Offer bridge

> Ad or content → Second Property Readiness Check™ → result → appropriate CTA → Second Property Strategy Session™

The Readiness Check must answer: what can the current property unlock; what is blocking the next move; which route appears most relevant; what may become harder if they continue waiting; what must be properly analysed before acting.

## 11. Prohibited claims

Do not promise or guarantee: that everyone can own two properties; that decoupling is always correct; that ABSD can always be avoided; financing approval; profit; that acting immediately is always better than waiting.

Do not publicly state a 90% disqualification figure — internal target only (see `11_MINIMUM_SELECTION_DOCTRINE_v1.0.md`). Public wording stays: *"We screen the relevant market and remove most options before producing the shortlist."*

Do not present rigid universal rejection vetoes — every classification is context-dependent and documented (see doctrine master).

Do not expose backend system names publicly (REI Method, ProDeck, Ascent Model, Trigger Questions, RAM).

## 12. Measurement and testing rules

Start with three core angles per door (see First test priority above) and multiple meaningful variations per angle — vary the psychological reason the angle matters, not only the wording.

Track: hook rate; click-through; page conversion; assessment completion; qualified result; booking; attendance; suitable-client rate; client outcome.
