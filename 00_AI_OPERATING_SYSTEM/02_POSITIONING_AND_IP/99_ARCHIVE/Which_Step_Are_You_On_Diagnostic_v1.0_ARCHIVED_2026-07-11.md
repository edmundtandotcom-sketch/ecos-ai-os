**WHICH STEP ARE YOU ON?**

**The Second Property Ladder™ Scored Diagnostic**

*The complete build document: gates, checkpoints, scoring, result pages, routing, and the GHL build spec.*

REI Advisory · Coach Edmund Tan & Cindior Ho · Singapore Real Estate Insider

Version 1.0 · 9 July 2026 · Built from Mechanism v1.3 (Rank 2\) · Traces to Master Big Idea v2.0 (Rank 1\)

**WHAT THIS IS.**  The first live revenue asset of the category. One question at a time, a Property Climber discovers which of the 5 Steps they are actually on — and why they cannot skip ahead. It qualifies the right buyer ($20k+ combined, $2.5m+ capacity), gates the wrong one politely, and ends every qualified result on one CTA: book the Position Map session.

**1\. Purpose & Funnel Position**

The diagnostic sits at the front of the funnel. Ads, YouTube end-screens, and all six content pillars point one place: **“Which Step Are You On?”** It converts curiosity into a scored position — and a scored position into a booked call.

| Job | How the diagnostic does it |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Name the position** | Every respondent leaves with a Step number (1–5). Nobody leaves with “thanks for playing”. The Step is the hook — it is theirs, it is specific, and it implies a next move. |
| **Qualify silently** | Four gate questions grade the lead (A/B/C/Portfolio) without ever saying “you don’t qualify”. Grades drive routing, not the on-screen experience. |
| **Teach the frame** | The questions themselves install the vocabulary: Position Map, Trade-Up vs Hold-Two, Exit Before Entry™, Climb Cadence. By question 10 the respondent is thinking in our category. |
| **Book the call** | Qualified results end on one CTA: the Position Map session. Unqualified results end on the nurture track — warm, useful, no call pressure. |

**THE RULE.**  The diagnostic never sells a property, a project, or a unit. It sells one thing: knowing your Step. The call sells the climb.

**2\. How It Works — The Architecture**

14 questions, one per screen, under 3 minutes. Two invisible layers run at once:

* 4 Gates (G1–G4) — qualification questions. They never block the quiz; they grade the lead in the background.

* 10 Checkpoints (C1–C10) — two per Step, scored 0/1/2. They determine which Step the respondent is on.

Question order on screen: **G1 → C1 → C2 → C3 → C4 → G2 → C5 → C6 → C7 → C8 → G3 → C9 → C10 → G4 → contact capture → result.** Gates are spread out so the quiz never feels like a mortgage application.

**The Checkpoint Model**

Each Step has two Checkpoints. Step score \= C-pair total (0–4). A Step is **cleared** at 3 or more. **Your Step \= the first Step you have not cleared.** Clear all five and you are a Climber in Motion (Result 6). This is the honest logic of a ladder: you cannot be on Step 4 if Step 1 is not done — and the quiz proves it to them.

**5\. Scoring Logic**

**Step Result (the headline output)**

| Rule | Definition |
| :---- | :---- |
| **Step score** | Sum of the Step’s two Checkpoints. Range 0–4. |
| **Cleared** | Step score of 3 or 4 — at least one full pass, nothing below partial. |
| **Your Step** | The FIRST Step, in order 1→5, that is not cleared. Evaluation stops there — a ladder is climbed in order. |
| **All cleared** | Result 6: Climber in Motion. Rare by design — expect roughly 1 in 20 respondents. |

**Readiness Band (secondary output — drives follow-up copy)**

| Total (0–20) | Band | Meaning in follow-up copy |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **0 – 7** | **Standing Still** | The One-Property Trap has them. Copy leads with the villain and the cost of another year of standing still. |
| **8 – 14** | **Aware but Unsequenced** | They know things; they haven’t ordered them. Copy leads with sequence and the ABSD math. |
| **15 – 20** | **Ready to Move** | Window language. Copy leads with timing, IPA, and the cost of a missed window. |

**Edge Cases**

* Owns 0 properties (G1=E): Checkpoints still run — but the result page swaps to the First Step Not Taken variant (nurture copy, no call CTA). Their answers still tag interests for the content sequence.

* Owns 2+ (G1=D): Step logic runs unchanged; result page adds one Portfolio line: “You’ve started the climb — the question is whether the order was right, and what it’s costing you now.”

* Straight-lining (all identical options in under 45 seconds): tag suspect\_speed; result still shows, follow-up automation holds 24 hours.

* Abandon after contact capture but before finish: partial score saved; abandon sequence triggers with the hook “you were 4 questions from your Step.”

**6\. The Six Result Pages — Full Copy**

One dynamic page, six content states. Every qualified state ends on the same CTA: **Book your Position Map session — 45 minutes, and you leave with your numbers.** Structure per page: headline → where you stand → what it’s costing you → your next move.

**Result 1 · You’re on Step 1: Map the Position**

**HEADLINE**

**You can’t climb from a position you’ve never mapped.**

**WHERE YOU STAND**

You have earning power — but no map. You don’t currently hold a defendable value for your own property, or the exact cash and CPF you’d walk away with. That’s not a criticism; it’s the single most common position in Singapore. Most people who own one property have never seen their own numbers on one page.

**WHAT IT’S COSTING YOU**

This is where the One-Property Trap does its quiet work. It feels responsible — pay it down, stay comfortable. But while the position stays unmapped, every year of decision-making is deferred by default. Standing still has a price; you just never see the invoice.

**YOUR NEXT MOVE**

The Position Map session. 45 minutes. You leave with your defendable value, your CPF refund position, your walk-away cash, and your Step 2 question already framed. Whatever you decide after that, you decide it from a map.

**Result 2 · You’re on Step 2: Set the Direction**

**HEADLINE**

**You know your numbers. You don’t yet know what they’re for.**

**WHERE YOU STAND**

You’ve cleared Step 1 — you can defend your value and you know your walk-away position. Genuinely ahead of most. What’s missing is the destination: an agreed answer, between you and your partner, on what the climb is for — and a target net asset value with a year on it.

**WHAT IT’S COSTING YOU**

Equity without direction doesn’t stay neutral — it becomes rationalisation. “We’re comfortable” is what an unset direction sounds like from the inside. Two more years of comfortable, and the loan-tenure math starts working against you instead of for you.

**YOUR NEXT MOVE**

The Position Map session — with the Direction Statement on the table. We put your numbers next to three destinations (retirement income, next-gen head start, lifestyle upgrade) and you leave with one of them chosen, in writing.

**Result 3 · You’re on Step 3: Sequence the Moves**

**HEADLINE**

**You have a destination. You don’t have an order.**

**WHERE YOU STAND**

Position mapped, direction set — you’re past the point most people ever reach. The open question is the one that decides everything: Trade-Up Path or Hold-Two Path, in what order — with your exact ABSD exposure calculated for keeping versus selling first.

**WHAT IT’S COSTING YOU**

This is the most expensive Step to guess at. One wrong-order move — buying before selling without the ABSD math, or committing to the wrong path for your income shape — is routinely a six-figure error. A good project in the wrong sequence is still a bad move.

**YOUR NEXT MOVE**

The Position Map session, sequence edition. We run both paths against your actual numbers — ABSD, tenure, name count — and you leave knowing which order is yours and what it’s worth.

**Result 4 · You’re on Step 4: Research the Exit First**

**HEADLINE**

**Your sequence is forming. Your exit isn’t researched.**

**WHERE YOU STAND**

You know your path and your order — now the Category Law applies: Exit Before Entry™. No move is safe until you know who buys it from you, when you’re free to move (MOP/SSD), what your downside buffer is, and what this move does to the one after it.

**WHAT IT’S COSTING YOU**

Exit-Later Buying is the trap that catches smart people — money committed first, exit thought about after. Every month the exit stays unresearched, you’re one attractive launch away from buying an asset you can’t leave on your terms.

**YOUR NEXT MOVE**

The Position Map session, exit edition. We profile the exit buyer for the move you’re considering, date your penalty clock to the day, and stress-test the downside — before a dollar is committed.

**Result 5 · You’re on Step 5: Execute & Repeat**

**HEADLINE**

**You’re ahead of almost everyone. Motion is the last discipline.**

**WHERE YOU STAND**

Mapped, directed, sequenced, exit-researched. What’s missing is the operating rhythm: a Climb Cadence you actually keep, and financing pre-cleared so a 90-day window is usable, not just visible.

**WHAT IT’S COSTING YOU**

Window drift. The right window doesn’t announce itself, and it doesn’t wait for an IPA to be processed. Plans without cadence decay quietly — this is how people with perfect strategies still miss their move.

**YOUR NEXT MOVE**

The Position Map session, cadence edition. We set your annual review point, get your IPA current, and define exactly what signal triggers your next move — so when the window opens, you’re already moving.

**Result 6 · Climber in Motion (all five Steps cleared)**

**HEADLINE**

**You’re a Climber in Motion. The question now is pace.**

**WHERE YOU STAND**

All five Steps cleared — fewer than one in twenty respondents get here. You’re not looking for a system; you’re running one. The remaining edge is second-opinion pressure-testing: is the cadence fast enough, is the current sequence still the right one, and is the next move already framed?

**YOUR NEXT MOVE**

A Climb Review — the same 45 minutes, a different job. We pressure-test your current sequence against the market’s next 24 months and confirm, or challenge, your next move. Founders’ note: we run this exact review on our own portfolio every year. We built it before we advised it.

**8\. GHL Build Spec**

**Asset**

* GHL Survey (not Form) — one question per slide, progress bar on, back button off.

* Contact capture slide AFTER question 14, BEFORE result: name, mobile (WhatsApp), email. Copy: “Your Step result is ready — where should we send your full breakdown?”

* Redirect: one results funnel page rendered dynamically from spl\_step\_result. Fall back to six hidden pages only if dynamic render proves unstable.

**Custom Fields (contact level)**

| spl\_step\_result | Dropdown | 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 / 5 / 6 (6 \= Climber in Motion) |
| spl\_total\_score | Number | 0–20 |
| spl\_band | Dropdown | standing\_still / aware\_unsequenced / ready\_to\_move |
| spl\_lead\_grade | Dropdown | A / B / P / C |
| spl\_gate\_ownership | Dropdown | own\_1\_hdb / own\_1\_private / own\_1\_landed / own\_2plus / own\_0 |
| spl\_gate\_income | Dropdown | inc\_30plus / inc\_20\_30 / inc\_12\_20 / inc\_below12 |

| spl\_c1 – spl\_c10 | Number · 10 fields | 0 / 1 / 2 per checkpoint — keep raw answers for call prep |

**Workflow (single master workflow)**

* Trigger: survey submitted → compute step scores → write spl\_step\_result, spl\_total\_score, spl\_band, spl\_lead\_grade.

* Branch on spl\_lead\_grade → apply tag (spl\_hot / spl\_warm / spl\_portfolio / spl\_nurture) → enrol in the matching sequence from Section 7\.

* If GHL native math is too brittle for the “first uncleared step” rule: one Make.com webhook computes and writes back. Keep ALL scoring logic in one place — never split it between GHL and Make.

* Call prep automation: on booking, fire an internal notification with the contact’s 10 raw checkpoint answers — the advisor walks in knowing exactly which answers were 0s.

**Instrumentation**

* Track per-slide drop-off. Expected completion 55–70% for a 14-question, sub-3-minute survey. If any single slide loses 15%+, rewrite that slide first.

* Result-page → booking conversion is THE metric. Baseline target: 25% of A-grade results book within 72 hours.
