Do we need separate landing pages?

My recommendation: yes, exactly two

One generic page would be operationally simpler, but psychologically weaker.

Someone clicking:

“Can we own our second property?”

should not land on a page opening with:

“Should you sell your HDB or EC?”

Likewise, an HDB owner thinking about upgrading may not identify with a page heavily focused on private-property decoupling.

That disconnect is called message mismatch:

the ad raises one question;

the page answers a broader or different question;

the visitor becomes uncertain;

conversion falls.

But creating a new page for every fear, hook or ad concept creates the opposite problem: too many pages to manage, update and track.

Therefore:

One landing page per main audience problem—not one page per ad.

Multiple angles can go to the same page

Yes. Each audience should have several marketing angles.

The page represents the common destination. The ads represent the different thoughts that lead people there.

What stays common behind both pages

Do not build two separate businesses behind the pages.

Both pages feed into:

One Second Property Readiness Check

One GHL contact database

One pipeline

One qualification system

One Second Property Strategy Session

One Second Property Route Map

One implementation and review system

The Readiness Check identifies one of three route outcomes.

Route 1 — Hold Two

The numbers and ownership structure may support keeping or restructuring the current property and acquiring another.

Route 2 — Trade Up First

One stronger asset may be the better immediate route before attempting to hold two later.

Route 3 — Prepare Before Moving

The household may need to improve funds, loan position, ownership structure, timing or decision alignment before acting.

This is how you can honestly lead with the desire for a second property while still recommending a different route when the facts demand it.
