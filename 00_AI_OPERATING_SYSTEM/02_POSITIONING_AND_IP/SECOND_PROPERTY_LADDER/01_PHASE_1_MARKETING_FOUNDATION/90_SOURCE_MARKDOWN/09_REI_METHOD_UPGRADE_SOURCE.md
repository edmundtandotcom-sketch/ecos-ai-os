10. Recommended final selection outcome

Do not give five equal recommendations.

That transfers the decision back to the client.

The output should be:

One primary recommendation

The option with the strongest overall fit.

Two backup recommendations

Used when:

preferred unit unavailable;

price exceeds walk-away level;

client preference changes;

timeline changes.

Up to two conditional alternatives

Only when they offer a genuine trade-off worth considering.

So the maximum is usually three serious recommendations, occasionally five total options including conditional choices.

10. Safety Net formula

Locked starting baseline

12 months.

Still need to define what the 12 months covers

Recommended calculation:

mortgage payments for all properties;

maintenance fees;

property tax;

insurance;

expected vacancy shortfall where rental is involved;

fixed loan commitments connected to the property plan;

essential household expenses if income loss is part of the stress scenario.

Funds that may count

Recommended:

readily available cash;

available CPF OA that is not already committed;

highly liquid funds that can genuinely be accessed.

Do not count:

future bonus;

projected capital gain;

hoped-for rental;

money already reserved for completion, renovation or legal costs;

assets that cannot be accessed quickly.

Risk adjustments

The starting baseline may increase beyond 12 months when:

the client is self-employed;

one income supports the household;

there are multiple properties;

the plan relies heavily on rental;

completion is years away;

family expenses are likely to rise;

the property may require a longer selling period.

A Critical Conflict should normally prevent recommendation unless:

Edmund explains the opportunity cost;

A Significant Risk may proceed when enough strong attributes compensate for it and the client accepts the trade-off.
