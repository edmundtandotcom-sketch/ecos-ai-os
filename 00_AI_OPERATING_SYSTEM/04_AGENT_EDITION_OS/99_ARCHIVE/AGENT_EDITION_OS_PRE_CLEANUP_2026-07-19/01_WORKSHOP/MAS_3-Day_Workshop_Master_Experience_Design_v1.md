# PART 1 — DESIGN PHILOSOPHY: WHAT MAKES A WORKSHOP WORLD-CLASS

Most paid workshops fail the same way: they are content-rich and transformation-poor. Participants take notes for 3 days, feel inspired, go home, and change nothing. The notes go in a drawer. The world-class workshop is designed around a different unit of value: **the built asset, not the learned idea.**

## The Seven Design Principles for MAS

**1. Build, don't teach.**
Every block ends with something that exists that did not exist that morning: a document, a script, a live CRM pipeline, a written plan. The test for every session: "What does the participant physically have at the end of this block?" If the answer is "understanding," the block is redesigned.

**2. The data does the confronting.**
Edmund never tells an agent their business is broken. The Business Audit, the Client Sort, the Revenue Map — the participant's own numbers deliver the verdict. This is the single most important facilitation principle in the programme. It preserves Edmund's position as the architect (the one who shows the way out), never the critic.

**3. Demonstration over instruction.**
The most valuable minutes of the 3 days are when Edmund runs a real discovery conversation on a real volunteer with a real property situation. Participants don't learn the framework from the slide — they learn it from watching 17 years of calibration operate in real time. Every framework gets demonstrated live before participants practice it.

**4. Peer intelligence is half the product.**
30 agents in a room is not just an audience — it is a dataset. Show-of-hands reveals, paired debriefs, group pattern recognition ("how many of you found the same thing?") turn individual embarrassment into collective realisation. This is the Vistage insight: the room teaches as much as the facilitator.

**5. Emotional architecture is deliberate.**
The 3 days follow a designed arc (Part 2). Day 1 breaks certainty. Day 2 builds capability. Day 3 builds machinery and momentum. The sequencing is not negotiable — capability before machinery, diagnosis before either.

**6. The teaching IS the selling.**
There is no separate "pitch block" hidden in the agenda. The Inner Circle is seeded through demonstrated value: every hot seat, every live read Edmund gives, every "in the Inner Circle we go deeper on this" is the sale happening in plain sight. By Day 3 evening, the invitation is a formality for the right people.

**7. The cohort outlives the workshop.**
World-class programmes manufacture belonging. Cohort naming, shared rituals, the WhatsApp group, the alumni identity — these are designed on purpose (Part 8). Community is the retention engine and the referral engine.

# PART 3 — THE PARTICIPANT JOURNEY ARC

The 3 days are an emotional sequence, not just a curriculum sequence. Each day has a designed dominant emotion and a designed exit state.

| Day | Dominant Experience | Morning State | Exit State (engineered) | The Sentence in Their Head Leaving |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Day 1 — Diagnose + Design** | Confrontation → Clarity | Skeptical, guarded, "show me" | Sober but oriented: "I finally see my business accurately — and I have the first two assets" | *"I've been running a job, not a business. Now I know what the business looks like."* |
| **Day 2 — The Advisory Method** | Competence-building | Eager (Day 1 created hunger) | Capable and slightly amazed at themselves: they ran a full advisory conversation today | *"I sounded like a different professional today. Clients will feel this."* |
| **Day 3 — The Scale Engine** | Momentum + machinery | Impatient to build | Equipped and in motion: live CRM, sequences running, 90-day plan, cohort formed | *"It's already running. I'm not starting Monday — I started today."* |

## Micro-arc within each day

Every day follows the same internal rhythm:

1. **Re-anchor (9:00)** — recap, hot seat or share-out. Reconnects the room, surfaces overnight processing.
2. **Concept + Demonstration (morning)** — Edmund shows the thing working, live.
3. **Build (late morning / afternoon)** — participants make their version. Staff circulate.
4. **Pressure-test (late afternoon)** — hot seat, role play, or group review. The asset gets stress-tested.
5. **Commit (close)** — written evening commitment, named out loud or to a partner. Public micro-commitment is the retention device.

## Energy management rules (non-negotiable)

- No block longer than 90 minutes without a state change (pair work, demo, movement, break).
- After lunch is ALWAYS interactive — never lecture into the 13:30 energy dip. (Day 1: ICP build. Day 2: Belief Resolution writing. Day 3: AI hands-on.)
- Edmund's live demonstrations are placed at energy peaks (10:30–11:30 and 15:45–16:30) — the moments of maximum attention.
- Music in transitions and breaks. Silence during builds. This sounds trivial; it is not. Room state is a managed variable.

| Element | Specification | Why |
|---|---|---|

| Walls | 2 foam boards per table + flipcharts; one "Architecture Wall" where each participant's business map is posted Day 1 and updated daily | Visible progress artifact; the wall becomes the closing-day photo moment |

| Role | Count | Responsibilities |
|---|---|---|
| Edmund | 1 | All teaching, all demos, all hot seats. Nothing operational. |

Premium production quality — the workbook is the artifact they keep on their desk for a year. It should look like it cost $200 to make.

# PART 5 — DAY 1 CONDUCT GUIDE: BUSINESS ARCHITECTURE (DIAGNOSE + DESIGN)

**Day promise (stated at 9:00):** "By 5:30 today you will have two things: your business accurately mapped for the first time, and a written positioning statement that changes how you introduce yourself professionally. Everything else this week builds on those two."

## 9:00–9:30 — Opening: Contract With the Room

Not a warm-up. A contract. Edmund covers, in this order:

1. **The story, compressed (5 min).** The leaderboard line — once, briefly. They heard the full version at the preview; repeating it at length would weaken it. "You know why you're here. Let's build."
2. **The 3-day map (5 min).** Show the deliverable stack visually: 8 named assets they will physically possess by Day 3 evening. Point at the Architecture Wall: "Your business goes up on that wall today. By Thursday it looks different."
3. **The rules (5 min).** Phones in the box during build blocks. What's shared in hot seats stays in the room. "I will interrupt you — that's coaching, not rudeness." Full disclosure rule: "The quality of what you build this week equals the honesty of the numbers you work with. Sanitised numbers build a sanitised business."
4. **First micro-commitment (10 min).** Each participant, 20 seconds, standing: name, years in, and one sentence — "What I want my business to look like in 12 months." Producer keeps it moving. This is the first time most of them have said it out loud. Coaches note the answers — they become hot seat fuel and Inner Circle qualification data.

**Facilitation note:** Edmund speaks maybe 12 of these 30 minutes. The room speaks the rest. The signal from minute one: this is a working room, not an audience.

## 9:30–11:00 — Block 1: The Business Audit Reveal

Pre-work was submitted. Coaches have already reviewed every audit the night before and flagged patterns plus 3 candidates for the live walk-through.

**Mechanics, step by step:**

1. **(10 min) Silent re-read.** Participants open their own submitted audit. Edmund: "Read your own numbers like a stranger sent them to you. Circle the one that bothers you most."
2. **(15 min) The hourly-rate calculation, live.** Everyone computes real hourly rate together: total commission ÷ total hours actually worked (including dead viewings, no-show meetings, servicing that went nowhere). Edmund does the math on screen with an anonymised real example: "$240K gross. 2,900 hours. $82 an hour. A senior banker's PA earns more with CPF and leave."
3. **(15 min) The room dataset.** Show of hands in bands: under $50/hr, $50–100, $100–200, above. Then the harder one: "Hands up if more than half your income came from sources you cannot predict or repeat." Most hands go up. Edmund says almost nothing. Pause. Let it sit. The silence here is the most expensive-feeling moment of Day 1 — do not fill it.
4. **(20 min) Teach: the Business Architecture Model.** The 5 elements (Positioning, Pipeline, Conversion System, Delivery System, Retention Loop) shown as the transactional agent's version vs the advisory business's version, side by side. One slide per element, one Singapore-specific example per element.
5. **(20 min) Map yourself.** Each participant marks their A3 model: green (working system), yellow (exists but ad-hoc), red (doesn't exist). Coaches circulate. Typical result: Positioning red, Pipeline yellow, Conversion yellow, Delivery green, Retention red.
6. **(10 min) The wall.** Each participant posts their marked map on the Architecture Wall. Stand back. Thirty maps, mostly red and yellow. Edmund: "That wall is the Singapore agent industry. Twenty of the thirty maps look identical. By Thursday morning, yours won't."

**Output:** One-page business diagnosis + mapped model on the wall.
**Open loop:** "Two of those five elements account for the ceiling you're hitting. We fix the first one this afternoon. The second one takes all of tomorrow."

## 11:00–12:00 — Block 2: The Revenue Map (the math that ends 'work harder')

The MREA economic model, made personal and Singaporean.

1. **(10 min)** Each participant writes their 12-month income target. Real number, not modest number. Coaches push: "The number you'd tell your spouse, not the number you'd tell your colleagues."
2. **(25 min) Reverse-engineer it on the worksheet:** target ÷ average net commission = deals needed → deals ÷ close rate = advisory conversations needed → conversations ÷ booking rate = qualified leads needed → leads ÷ contact-to-lead rate = database touches needed. Every divisor is THEIR number from the audit, not an industry benchmark.
3. **(15 min) The two-lever reveal.** Edmund takes one volunteer's math on screen. Shows the brute-force path (3× the leads = 3× the hours = impossible) vs the architecture path (raise average deal size via client tier + raise close rate via advisory method + add retention/referral loop = same income, fewer transactions). "You don't have an effort problem. You have an architecture problem. Effort is the most expensive way to compensate for missing architecture."
4. **(10 min)** Each participant writes one sentence at the bottom of the map: "The lever I've been ignoring is ___."

**Output:** Completed Revenue Map.
**Facilitation note:** This block is where "I need a system" becomes the participant's own conclusion. Edmund must NOT say it for them. The worksheet says it.

## 12:00–13:00 — Lunch + the 10-Minute AI Seed

At 12:40, screens on, casual tone — deliberately un-produced: Edmund takes one real (anonymised) client situation from the morning and has Claude draft a post-viewing follow-up message in his voice, then re-draft it for a different client temperament. 90 seconds of typing, two good outputs. "Day 3, every one of you builds this into your daily operation. Eat."

Why it works: contrast. After a heavy morning of confronting math, a glimpse of leverage. The seed for Day 3 is planted at the exact moment relief is welcome.

## 13:00–14:30 — Block 3: The Client Sort → Ideal Client Profile

1. **(20 min) The Sort.** Last 10 deals from pre-work, sorted three ways on the worksheet: by profit-per-hour (not commission — profit per hour), by ease (1–5), by origin (referral/repeat vs cold). 
2. **(10 min) Pattern share at tables.** Each table finds its common pattern. Almost universally: the highest profit-per-hour clients were referrals, were easier, AND were a recognisable type. The worst clients came from the channels that consume the most effort.
3. **(15 min) Teach: the Ideal Client Matrix** — financial capacity × decision readiness × relationship origin × strategic need (the four axes). Where Edmund's own practice sits on the matrix and why that was a CHOICE, not luck.
4. **(35 min) Build the ICP document.** Structured template: who they are, what they own, what triggers their next move, what they fear, what they've never been told by an agent, where they congregate. Coaches circulate hard here — this is the afternoon's critical build.
5. **(10 min) Stress test in pairs.** Partner asks: "Would you turn away a $15K commission deal that doesn't fit this profile?" Hesitation reveals an ICP written to please the instructor instead of to run a business. Rewrite until the hesitation is real.

**Output:** Completed Ideal Client Profile.

## 14:30–15:30 — Block 4: The Advisory Positioning Statement

The first true business asset. The one-paragraph statement: who I serve + what situation they're in + what outcome I move them toward + why my process is different.

1. **(10 min)** Teach the structure with 3 worked examples at different tiers (HDB upgrader specialist, landed-property family advisor, investment portfolio repositioner) — proving the framework flexes to THEIR market, not Edmund's.
2. **(15 min)** First draft, longhand, alone. Bad first drafts encouraged: "Write the honest ugly version."
3. **(10 min) AI-assisted refinement, demonstrated then done.** Edmund shows the refine prompt live on one volunteer's draft (the prompt goes into their AI library on Day 3). Participants run their own refinement. Rule: AI suggests, the agent decides. The statement must survive being said out loud to a real person without embarrassment.
4. **(15 min) The Say-It-Standing test.** Pairs stand, deliver their statement as if at a networking event. Partner gives one piece of feedback: "Would you remember this person tomorrow?" Iterate once.
5. **(10 min)** Final version written into the workbook AND onto their Architecture Wall card.

**Output:** Written Advisory Positioning Statement — posted on the wall under their map.

## 15:30–15:45 — Break

## 15:45–17:00 — Block 5: The Advisory Method, First Look (live demonstration)

The Day 1 peak. Edmund runs a 20-minute genuine discovery conversation with a volunteer using the volunteer's real property situation. Full protocol for demos in Part 7.

Then the structured debrief (this is where the learning actually happens):
- **(10 min) Solo notes:** "Write down 3 things I did that you currently don't do."
- **(15 min) Table comparison**, then tables report one observation each. Typical surfacing: "You didn't mention a single property for 20 minutes." "You asked about their parents' flat." "You wrote down numbers and read them back."
- **(15 min) Edmund decodes:** names 4–5 of the moves they spotted, names the framework behind each ("that question is Level 7 of the Intelligence Capture — tomorrow you get all 12 levels"), and explicitly does NOT explain everything: "The rest you learn tomorrow when you run it yourself."

**Open loop:** Tomorrow they don't watch this. They DO this.

## 17:00–17:30 — Hot Seat + Close

Two positioning statements reviewed live in the front chairs (selected by coaches during the afternoon — one strong, one struggling; the struggling one gets the deeper work, and its public repair is the most valuable 10 minutes of the day for the whole room).

**Evening commitment (written, then told to table partner):** (1) Re-read positioning statement before bed, refine once. (2) Identify ONE past client who fits the ICP. (3) Bring that client's full situation tomorrow — it becomes their role-play case.

Edmund's closing line, verbatim: *"Today you found out what your business actually is. Tomorrow you learn the conversation that changes what it earns. Go home. Don't celebrate yet — you haven't built anything dangerous until tomorrow afternoon."*

# PART 6 — DAY 2 CONDUCT GUIDE: THE ADVISORY METHOD

**Day promise:** "By 5:30 today, every one of you will have personally run a complete advisory conversation — not watched one, run one — and you will have your own discovery script, objection responses, and trigger questions in writing."

## 9:00–9:30 — Re-anchor: Overnight Hot Seat

Two volunteers share their refined positioning statement and the ICP client they identified. Edmund gives each 3 minutes of sharpening. Purpose beyond review: it signals that evening commitments are checked. From this morning on, nobody skips homework.

## 9:30–11:00 — Block 1: The Two-Conversation System — Discovery (Conversation 1)

1. **(15 min) The frame.** Why diagnosis before prescription is the entire difference between an agent and an advisor. Medical analogy, made concrete: "A doctor who prescribes before examining loses their licence. An agent who pitches before diagnosing just loses the client — slower, and without ever knowing why."
2. **(30 min) The MAS Discovery Question Framework, taught in full.** All 12 levels of the Intelligence Capture, distributed as the branded document. For each level: the question's purpose, the exact wording, what a strong answer sounds like, what a deflection sounds like, and the follow-up that re-opens a deflection. No level is skipped — this is the "teach everything" commitment in action.
3. **(30 min) Levels 1–4 pair drill.** A/B pairs, 10 minutes each direction, using their real Day-1 client case. Constraint drill: the asker may ONLY ask questions — the moment they explain, advise, or mention property, partner raises a hand. This single constraint rewires the habit that sinks most agents: talking to demonstrate value instead of asking to capture it.
4. **(15 min) Debrief.** "What was hardest about staying in question mode?" Universal answer: silence tolerance. Edmund teaches the 4-second rule: after a real question, the next person who speaks loses information.

## 11:00–12:00 — Block 2: The Full Live Demonstration

Edmund runs the complete discovery conversation — all 12 levels — on a fresh volunteer with a meaningful real situation (coaches have pre-identified candidates from Day 1 intel; the best demo subjects have a genuinely tangled position: an HDB + condo decoupling question, a parents' estate, an over-leveraged upgrade).

Observers work, not watch: each participant tracks ONE assigned dimension (questions asked vs statements made; how deflections were re-opened; what got written down; how money was discussed; how the conversation was closed). Five observation lenses across the room, then the debrief assembles the full picture from the room's own observations. The room teaches itself the demo.

**Output:** Each participant's 3-discoveries note feeds directly into their script draft after lunch.

## 12:00–13:00 — Lunch

Coaches sit distributed, one per table cluster. Assignment: surface each table's hardest live client case. The two best become the 17:00 hot seats.

## 13:00–14:00 — Block 3: The Strategy Session (Conversation 2) + REI Process

*(Taught as the MAS Advisory Process — REI branding internal only.)*

1. **(20 min)** The Strategy Session structure: position summary read-back ("you are here") → market context relevant to them ("this is the terrain") → the 2–3 move sequence ("here is the route") → the decision question. Roadmap, not pitch — the distinction taught explicitly: a pitch asks them to buy something; a roadmap asks them to decide about their own life.
2. **(20 min)** Worked example on screen: a real (anonymised) client case taken from Review → Evaluate → Identify, showing the actual documents Edmund presents.
3. **(20 min)** Participants draft the skeleton Strategy Session for THEIR Day-1 client case on the template.

## 14:00–15:30 — Block 4: Belief Resolution (objections) + Trigger Questions

1. **(15 min)** The reframe: an objection is not resistance — it is the client reporting which belief was not yet resolved. The 5 core unresolved beliefs in the Singapore market: *"market might drop, I should wait"* / *"I can find this information myself"* / *"another agent quoted lower commission"* / *"we're not ready yet"* / *"my friend/relative is also an agent."*
2. **(30 min)** For each: the resolution language, taught and distributed. Not scripts to recite — structures to inhabit. Each follows the same chassis: acknowledge → expose the hidden cost of the belief → offer a reframe through THEIR data from discovery → return the decision to them.
3. **(30 min) Personal scripting.** Each participant writes their own version of all 5 in their own voice, plus 3 Trigger Questions for their specific ICP (the question that makes their ideal client stop scrolling/stop walking — e.g., for upgrader specialists: "Do you know what your flat's value will need to be in 2029 for your upgrade math to still work?").
4. **(15 min)** Table read-alouds, best of each table read to the room.

## 15:30–15:45 — Break. Seeding line, exact wording: *"What you've built in the last two days only pays you if it runs without your memory. Tomorrow we build the machine."*

## 15:45–17:00 — Block 5: Full Simulation (the Day 2 peak)

A/B pairs, 30 minutes per direction: full discovery (compressed to levels 1–6 + 2 deep levels of choice) + opening of strategy session + one objection (B-partner instructed to deploy their real-life hardest one).

Edmund and coaches roam with the interrupt protocol (Part 7): freeze the conversation mid-flow, correct in 30 seconds, replay the moment, move on. Public correction of one pair = teaching for all thirty; the producer ensures every interrupt is audible.

**Output:** Every participant has run the advisory conversation end to end, today, before any real client hears it.

## 17:00–17:30 — Hot Seats: "Bring Me Your Hardest Case"

The two pre-surfaced cases. Edmund runs the advisory process live under pressure on real, current, unsolved situations. This block is deliberately unscripted — the room watches the method survive contact with genuine difficulty. It is also the strongest Inner Circle advertisement of the entire workshop, without one word of pitch: *this is what having Edmund in your business looks like.*

**Evening commitment:** Draft full Discovery Script from the template. Identify 3 live pipeline clients to run the process with in the next 30 days — names written in workbook.

Closing line: *"Yesterday you saw your business. Today you changed what you're capable of in a conversation. Tomorrow we make it so the business runs even on the days you don't feel like a different professional."*

# PART 7 — CORE FACILITATION METHODOLOGIES

These four protocols are the craft of the workshop. They are also the protocols the Inner Circle runs on — participants experience them here, then buy ongoing access to them.

## 7.1 The Live Demonstration Protocol

1. **Real volunteer, real situation, real stakes.** Never role-played personas. The room can smell fiction.
2. **Consent + boundary, stated publicly:** "We'll use your real numbers. Anything you don't want shared, say 'pass' — no explanation needed." (Safety creates depth; depth creates the demo's power.)
3. **No commentary during.** Edmund never breaks the conversation to explain. The demo is a sealed unit — explanation contaminates demonstration.
4. **Debrief is structured extraction**, not Edmund explaining: solo notes → table comparison → room report → THEN Edmund decodes. The room earns the insight before receiving it.
5. **One thing stays unexplained**, named but not taught — the open loop is part of the protocol.

## 7.2 The Hot Seat Protocol (Vistage-derived, MAS-adapted)

| Phase | Time | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Presentation | 3 min | Hot-seat holder states the situation + the specific question. No interruptions. |
| 2. Clarifying questions | 5 min | Room asks questions ONLY. Anyone who smuggles advice into a question gets stopped by the facilitator. ("Have you considered…" is advice wearing a question costume.) |
| 3. Restatement | 1 min | Edmund restates the REAL problem — frequently different from the presented one. This restatement is the visible 17-years-of-calibration moment. |
| 4. Counsel | 8 min | Edmund advises; selected room members add experience ("when I faced this…" — experience-sharing, never prescription). |
| 5. Commitment | 2 min | Holder states what they will do and by when. Written in their workbook. Producer logs it for the 30-day group follow-up. |

## 7.3 The Interrupt-Coaching Protocol (role plays)

Freeze ("hold — stay in it") → name the moment in one sentence → give the replacement move → rewind 30 seconds and replay → reinforce ("that — did everyone hear the difference?") → resume. Maximum 45 seconds per interrupt. Interrupt the strong performers too — being corrected by Edmund must read as a privilege, not a punishment.

## 7.4 The Build-Block Protocol (worksheet sessions)

Teach (≤20 min) → demonstrate one worked example → build alone in silence (music off) → coach circulation with the 3-question unblock rule (coaches ask up to 3 questions before ever suggesting an answer) → pair stress-test → finalise into workbook. Every build ends physically: written, posted, or configured. Never "think about."

# PART 8 — DAY 3 CONDUCT GUIDE: THE SCALE ENGINE (HANDS ON KEYBOARD)

**Day promise:** "You do not leave tonight with a plan to build a system. You leave with a system that is already running — pipeline live, contacts loaded, first sequence active, AI working, and a 90-day plan with your name on it."

**Day 3 operating mode is different and announced as such:** laptops open all day, tech lead on standby, pace is sprint-based — build sprints with hard time-boxes, not open-ended sessions. The producer runs a visible countdown for each sprint. The shift in format on the final day re-spikes energy precisely when 3-day fatigue would otherwise set in.

## 9:00–9:30 — Re-anchor + Triage

Quick poll: who drafted the full discovery script, who has their 3 pipeline names. Anyone who struggled with Day 2's role play gets a coach assigned for a 10-minute sidebar during the first build sprint. Nobody enters Day 3 broken.

## 9:30–10:30 — Sprint 1: GHL Pipeline Build (live, mirrored)

Edmund's screen on screen 1, the build spec on screen 2. Participants build THEIR pipeline in their own GHL trial: stages mapped to the Two-Conversation System (New Lead → Contacted → Discovery Booked → Discovery Done → Strategy Session → Decision → Client → Retention), custom fields from the Intelligence Capture levels, tags from their ICP segments. Tech lead handles casualties at the rescue desk so the room never stalls.

**Sprint deadline mechanic:** pipeline screenshot posted to the cohort WhatsApp group by 10:30. (This doubles as the group's activation — the community's first ritual is everyone proving the build.)

## 10:30–11:30 — Sprint 2: Contact Import + Segmentation

Import last 50 contacts, segment against the ICP: A (fits profile, warm), B (fits, cold), C (doesn't fit, service-only). The uncomfortable discovery, surfaced deliberately at 11:15: most agents find fewer than 10 A-contacts in their last 50. Edmund: "Now you know why income is unpredictable — the database was never built on purpose. From today it is."

## 11:30–12:00 — Sprint 3: Sequence 1 Live (New Lead)

The 10 GHL-ready templates distributed. Sequence 1 (new lead, 7 touches/90 days) configured and ACTIVATED before lunch. The producer announces it formally: "Every person in this room now has an asset working while you eat lunch." Applause moment — engineered, deserved.

## 12:00–13:00 — Lunch

## 13:00–14:00 — Sprint 4: The AI Operating Layer

The 50-prompt AI Library distributed, organised by use: pre-meeting research briefs, discovery follow-up summaries, strategy session prep, content drafts, objection rehearsal (AI plays the difficult client — the sleeper hit of the library). Each participant runs 2 prompts on a REAL client from their imported contacts and saves the outputs. AI taught exactly as decided: an operating layer inside their existing workflows — never a separate magic topic.

## 14:00–15:00 — Sprint 5: Content Brief (positioning made visible)

The 4-week content framework: each week = one client belief addressed (from the Belief Resolution work), one format, one platform — chosen by where THEIR ICP actually is. Participants complete the 4-week brief and AI-draft piece #1 in the room. Marketing taught precisely as scoped: a consequence of positioning, not media buying. For ads questions, the scripted redirect: *"Your ads will tell you when your positioning is wrong, because they will produce bad leads. Positioning first. The deeper ads work is Inner Circle terrain."*

## 15:00–15:15 — Break. Final seeding, exact wording: *"Everything you've built runs on what you knew as of today. The market will move. Scripts will age. What's working in ads and content will change by September. The people I work with year-round don't get stale. That's the last thing I'll show you this afternoon."*

## 15:15–16:15 — Sprint 6: Referral Loop + Reactivation

The referral conversation framework (give-first structure: deliver value to the client, THEN open the referral doorway with the trigger phrase: "Who else in your circle is sitting on a property decision they keep postponing?"). Participants write and personalise reactivation messages to 5 real A/B contacts. **The Send Moment, 16:05:** they actually send them, in the room, now. Phones up, send, done. Several will get replies before 17:00. The producer watches for the first reply and announces it — that reply is the workshop's proof-of-life moment and frequently the best testimonial clip of the event.

## 16:15–17:00 — Sprint 7: The 90-Day Scale Plan

The full plan on the structured template: 90-day income target (from the Day 1 Revenue Map) → required conversations → weekly activity numbers → first 30 days calendared week by week → the one system to deepen each month. Coaches pressure-test every plan against one question: "Show me where in week 1 this plan touches a real client."

## 17:00–17:30 — Final Hot Seats: 3 Plans Presented

Three participants present their 90-day plan in the front chairs (full hot seat protocol). Selection by coaches: one strong plan (sets the bar), one ambitious-but-fragile plan (gets repaired publicly — highest teaching value), one from a quiet participant (signals everyone matters).

## 17:30–17:45 — The Wall Walk + Cohort Close

Everyone stands at the Architecture Wall. Day 1's red-and-yellow maps now carry positioning statements, ICPs, and plan cards. Edmund, briefly: *"Wednesday morning that wall was the industry. This evening it's thirty businesses. Same people. Different architecture."* Cohort photo at the wall. The cohort gets its name (Part 9) and the 30-day group rules. THEN —

## 17:45–18:30 — The Inner Circle Invitation

Run exactly per the confirmed design: short, direct, made from demonstrated value — not pitched to the room but announced to it, with the application form available for the right people. Edmund already knows his shortlist (the qualification observations were gathered all 3 days — Part 10). The room-facing words stay within the confirmed script: monthly room, 20 serious agents, Edmund's current read, Edmund in your business, application only. No stack, no countdown, no discounting. At $35K the offer's power is its restraint.

# PART 9 — COMMUNITY DESIGN: HOW A COHORT BECOMES A COMMUNITY

The workshop creates the assets. The community creates the implementation. Programmes without community design get 10% implementation; the WhatsApp group, rituals, and identity below are what make the testimonial flywheel possible.

## Cohort identity

- Every cohort is named and numbered: **MAS Cohort 1** (or themed naming Edmund prefers — e.g., named after the month/year). Members refer to themselves by cohort. Scarcity of each cohort (30 max, dated) makes membership a fact of biography, not a purchase.
- The cohort photo at the Architecture Wall is the badge. Posted in the group, used (with consent) in future marketing.

## The 30-Day Activation Group (WhatsApp) — operating rules

| Element | Design |
|---|---|
| Duration | 30 days, hard stop announced upfront (scarcity keeps it alive; open-ended groups decay into silence) |
| Ritual 1 — Monday Numbers | Every Monday 9am: each member posts 3 numbers — discovery calls run / sequences active / A-contacts touched. Producer compiles a simple leaderboard. Peer visibility is the accountability engine; Edmund never chases anyone. |
| Ritual 2 — Win Wednesday | Members post one win, any size. Coaches seed the first two weeks so the norm sets. |
| Ritual 3 — Edmund's Friday Voice Note | Once a week, 3–5 minutes, Edmund only: one observation from the group's numbers + one current market note. This is his ONLY scheduled appearance — scarcity of presence sustains its value and previews the Inner Circle's "live read" rhythm. |
| Hot seat carry-over | The workshop hot-seat commitments (logged by the producer) get checked at day 10 and day 25, publicly and warmly. |
| Inner Circle seeding | Weeks 3–4: members visibly implementing get a personal (not group) message from Edmund inviting an application conversation. The group itself is never pitched. |
| Day 30 close | Final leaderboard, top-3 implementer shout-outs, the 90-day video series carries engagement onward, and the 90-day checkpoint is calendared. |

## The alumni layer (designed now, activated after Cohort 2)

Cross-cohort alumni group opens only to members who completed Monday Numbers at least 3 of 4 weeks — implementation-gated, which keeps it the highest-signal free room in the Singapore agent world and the permanent top of the Inner Circle funnel. Alumni get first invitations to future preview events as guests-who-bring-guests: the referral engine.

# PART 10 — INNER CIRCLE QUALIFICATION: OBSERVED ACROSS ALL 3 DAYS

Edmund's shortlist is built from observation, not application essays. Coaches log these signals per participant (simple 1–5 grid, reviewed each evening):

| Signal | Where It Shows |
|---|---|
| Implements immediately without negotiating the exercise | Every build block — do they build, or do they ask why first? |
| Honest numbers | Day 1 audit — sanitised numbers predict a sanitised coaching relationship |
| Already producing ($300K+ trajectory) but ceiling-aware | Opening intros + Revenue Map targets |
| Asks questions about THEIR business, not hypotheticals | Q&A moments, hot seats |
| Generous in pairs — coaches their partner well | Role plays (givers thrive in a 20-person room; takers poison it) |
| Volunteers for pressure | Demo volunteers, hot seat seekers |
| Financial capacity without strain | $35K must be a business decision, not a gamble — surfaced in the application call, never in the room |

Day 3 lunchtime: Edmund + coaches finalise the shortlist (target: 6–10 names per cohort). Those people get a quiet word from Edmund personally before the 17:45 announcement: "When I open applications this evening, I'd like you to take a form." That sentence converts better than the entire room-facing presentation.

# PART 12 — WORKING BACKWARDS: WHAT THIS DESIGN DICTATES FOR THE 1-DAY PREVIEW

Now that the 3-day is fully designed, the preview's job description writes itself. The preview must:

1. **Create the hunger the 3-day feeds.** The preview's Business Audit moment (lite version) must produce the "$82/hour" realisation — because Day 1 of the workshop deepens exactly that wound and then heals it.
2. **Demonstrate, once, what they'll do thirty times.** The preview's live discovery demo (5 questions) is the trailer for Day 2's full 12-level method. Same protocol, deliberately truncated.
3. **Sell the deliverable stack, not the curriculum.** The enrollment offer lists the 8 named assets from this design (mapped business + positioning statement + ICP + discovery script + objection scripts + live CRM with sequences + AI library in use + 90-day plan). Assets sell; topics don't.
4. **Pre-frame the formats.** Mention hot seats, the wall, the send-moment by name in the preview — "in the implementation workshop there's a moment on Day 3 where you message 5 past clients live, in the room" — concreteness of experience is what separates this from every PowerPoint seminar they've attended.
5. **Recruit the right 30.** The preview's $97 filter + the enrollment conversation should bias toward the Part 10 signals. A cohort of implementers produces the proof that markets Cohort 2. One cohort of tourists produces nothing.
