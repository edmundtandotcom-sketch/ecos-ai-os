## 1. Segment Rule

A buyer segment is a client psychology profile.

It is not automatically a campaign pack, ad set, funnel, or offer.

A segment becomes a campaign pack only when we decide it deserves a dedicated market-facing campaign.

## 3. Segment 1 — FCC: First-Climb Existing Owner / First-Condo Compounder

### Profile
Singapore owner or couple whose property has appreciated, but whose next move is unclear. They may have one private property, a strong equity position, and a sense that they “did well,” but appreciation has not yet become a structured next move.

### Ascent Stage
A1 — First Climb

### Core Question
“My property went up. What now?”

### What They Say Out Loud
- “We are not in a rush.”
- “Maybe we just continue holding.”
- “The property is doing okay.”

### What They Think Privately
- “It went up, but I don’t know what to do with that gain.”
- “How many property cycles do I actually have left?”
- “Other people seem to have made a move. We have not.”

### Wrong Assumption
“As long as my property appreciated, the strategy is working.”

### Hard Truth
A property can appreciate and still fail to create your next move. Appreciation without a next move is only a paper win.

### Better Question
“What does this property unlock next — and is that window still open?”

### Recommended Campaign Direction
Idle Million / Your Property Went Up / One Property Trap / Next 3 Moves

## 4. Segment 2 — DINK: Golden Window DINK

### Profile
Dual-income couples with no dependants or fewer financial constraints. Their advantage is flexibility, but that advantage reduces as life becomes more complex.

### Ascent Stage
A1 — First Climb

### Core Question
“Are we wasting our most flexible years?”

### What They Say Out Loud
- “We are enjoying life right now.”
- “No rush. We still have time.”
- “We will figure it out later when life changes.”

### What They Think Privately
- “Are we being lazy with the best financial years of our lives?”
- “If this is our most flexible period, should we be building something more?”
- “Peers who moved earlier seem to have more options now.”

### Wrong Assumption
“Waiting is responsible because we are still young and flexible.”

### Hard Truth
The golden window is not just about income. It is about low constraints, clean decision-making, and time to compound. Once life complexity arrives, the same move may become harder.

### Better Question
“If this is our most financially flexible period, what should it be building?”

### Recommended Campaign Direction
Golden Window DINK / One Property Trap / Next 3 Moves

## 5. Segment 3 — FHA: Forever-Home Attached Owner

### Profile
Owners or families who bought with an own-stay mindset and became emotionally attached. The property may have appreciated or become fully paid / near paid, but they have not reviewed whether holding still serves the future.

### Ascent Stage
A1 — First Climb or A2 — Compound, depending on current position

### Core Question
“This is our home. But are we stuck?”

### What They Say Out Loud
- “This is our home.”
- “We are comfortable here.”
- “We cannot just sell and move for money.”

### What They Think Privately
- “Is loving this home quietly costing us?”
- “Are we avoiding the review because it feels emotional?”
- “Can we keep the family meaning and still think strategically?”

### Wrong Assumption
“Reviewing the property means we are already deciding to leave it.”

### Hard Truth
A home can hold memories. A property must still hold strategy. These are different questions.

### Better Question
“If this property belonged to someone else and you reviewed it objectively, would you hold, exit, or redeploy?”

### Recommended Campaign Direction
Forever Home Trap / Family Asset Planning / One Property Trap

## 6. Segment 4 — DFP: Decoupling / Free-Name Planner

### Profile
Couples who are considering decoupling, have already decoupled, or are attracted to freeing one name. They understand the mechanism but may not have the strategy behind what the free name must achieve.

### Ascent Stage
A1 — First Climb or A2 — Compound

### Core Question
“If we free a name, what should we buy?”

### What They Say Out Loud
- “We already decoupled. Now we just need the right project.”
- “Which project has the best entry price?”
- “We want to use the free name properly.”

### What They Think Privately
- “This next purchase needs to justify the cost of freeing the name.”
- “Everyone is showing us a different project.”
- “What if we use the free name wrongly?”

### Wrong Assumption
“Freeing the name is the strategy.”

### Hard Truth
Decoupling is a mechanism, not a strategy. A free name without clear next 2–3 moves is an expensive starting position with no destination.

### Better Question
“Before buying with the free name, what does this purchase need to unlock two moves from now?”

### Recommended Campaign Direction
Decoupling Is Not The Strategy / Exit-First Before You Buy / New Launch vs Resale

## 7. Segment 5 — RM: Ready Mover

### Profile
Buyers close to commitment. They may be shortlisting, visiting showflats, planning to sign, comparing new launch vs resale, or preparing to deploy capital.

### Ascent Stage
A1 — First Climb or A2 — Compound

### Core Question
“Before I sign, is this really the right move?”

### What They Say Out Loud
- “The unit looks good.”
- “We need to decide soon.”
- “The project seems strong.”

### What They Think Privately
- “What if I missed something important?”
- “What if this locks us into the wrong position?”
- “The showflat looks good, but is the strategy right?”

### Wrong Assumption
“If the project is good, the decision is safe.”

### Hard Truth
A good project can still be the wrong move. Entry is only one part. Exit, future buyer pool, quantum, household fit, and next 2–3 moves matter too.

### Better Question
“Before you sign, who buys this from you later, at what price, and under what conditions?”

### Recommended Campaign Direction
Before You Sign The OTP / Exit-First Before You Buy / New Launch vs Resale / Showflat Loop

## 8. Segment 6 — HECO-Q: Qualified HDB/EC Upgrade-Window Household

### Profile
Not every HDB or EC owner. This segment refers only to households with a real upgrade-window decision, enough financial relevance, and serious advisory fit.

### Ascent Stage
A1 — First Climb

### Valid Qualifying Signals
- Their HDB or EC is approaching or past a meaningful sale / upgrade window.
- There is meaningful equity, cash, income, or lending ability to consider a private-property move safely.
- Their name structure matters to the next move.
- They are trying to avoid using two clean names poorly.
- They are not just asking for the cheapest upgrade.
- They are willing to review household numbers and long-term structure.

### Core Question
“When we exit, what should this unlock next?”

### What They Say Out Loud
- “Should we upgrade now or wait?”
- “Should we buy one private property or plan further?”
- “What happens after we sell?”

### What They Think Privately
- “If we sell, we may have one big chance to reset the family structure.”
- “Are we using our clean names properly?”
- “Is upgrading to one private property enough, or are we missing a better path?”

### Wrong Assumption
“Selling HDB/EC and buying one private property is automatically the upgrade.”

### Hard Truth
The exit may give you a window. But a window is not a strategy. Using two clean names to build only one unclear move may waste the structure.

### Better Question
“Before exiting, what should this move unlock: one home, one investment, or a longer 1-to-2 pathway?”

### Recommended Campaign Direction
HDB/EC Upgrade Window / 1-to-2 Window / Upgrade Without Overstretching

### Guardrail
Do not target broad HDB/EC owners with generic wealth claims. This segment must be qualified by numbers, timeline, name structure, and advisory readiness.

## 9. Segment 7 — FAP: Family Asset Planner

### Profile
Families thinking beyond the immediate property. Their decisions may involve children, parents, retirement, future affordability, legacy planning, or holding assets across family names.

### Ascent Stage
A2 — Compound (the former A3 Legacy stage is RETIRED per Decision 063 — family situations classify as A1/A2)

### Core Question
“How should property support our family’s next chapter?”

### What They Say Out Loud
- “We want to plan properly for the family.”
- “We do not want to make a short-sighted move.”
- “We need to think about the children / retirement / long-term security.”

### What They Think Privately
- “If we do nothing, will our children face a harder property market?”
- “Are our assets positioned properly for the next generation?”
- “Are we keeping too much wealth locked in one place?”

### Wrong Assumption
“Family property planning can wait until later.”

### Hard Truth
Family asset decisions compound quietly. Waiting may not feel costly today, but future options can narrow without warning.

### Better Question
“What should our current property position do for the family over the next 10–20 years?”

### Recommended Campaign Direction
Family Asset Planning / Next 3 Moves (the Family Legacy campaign door is RETIRED per Decision 058 — do not build new creative to it)

## 10. Segment Usage Rule

Do not turn every segment into a campaign immediately.

Use this library to diagnose. Then Content Marketing Engine decides whether the segment deserves a dedicated campaign pack, angle, script, or ad set.
